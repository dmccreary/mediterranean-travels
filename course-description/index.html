    Trip Description¶

    Title¶

    Mediterranean Travels

    Summary¶

    This is not a textbook. It is a personal travel guide for a thirteen-day journey through Turkey and Greece, from Wednesday, November 11 to Monday, November 23, 2026. The trip has three parts: three days on our own in Istanbul, the Viking Star's seven-night Ancient Mediterranean Treasures cruise from Istanbul to Athens, and three days on our own in Athens.

    The guide is meant to be read one chapter per day, ideally at breakfast or the night before. Each chapter prepares us for that day's place: what happened there, who built it and why, what to look for when we are standing in front of it, what to eat, and who lives there now. The goal is not to tick off sights. It is to notice more, understand more, and meet the people of each place with real empathy.

    Audience¶

    The travelers on this trip: curious adults who love the history of civilization, culture, and food, and who want to understand the people in each place, not just see the monuments. We have no professional background in archaeology, classics, or Middle Eastern or Balkan history.

    Prerequisites¶

    None. The guide assumes only general adult knowledge, such as having heard of ancient Greece, the Roman Empire, Christianity, Islam, and the Ottoman Empire. Every other term is explained when it first appears.

    Itinerary¶

    Cruise port times are from the cruise-agency listings for the November 14, 2026 sailing (Cruisebound). Viking can change times and ports, so we should confirm with Viking before we go.

    Day Date Place Plan

    2 Thu Nov 12 Istanbul, Turkey On our own
    3 Fri Nov 13 Istanbul, Turkey On our own; Friday is the main day of congregational prayer

    November is off-season. Opening hours are shorter, some sites close for the season, and it can be cool and rainy, so each chapter should flag what to check and what to wear.

    Structure¶

    • One chapter per day of the itinerary (thirteen chapters). Istanbul and Athens each get several chapters, so each day can focus on a different layer of the city instead of repeating an overview.
    • A short orientation chapter to read before departure: the big timeline of the region, a few words of Turkish and Greek, and how the trip fits together.
    • A short closing chapter to read on the way home: connecting what we saw across all the stops.

    Every daily chapter should cover, where it fits:

    1. What we will see, in the order we are likely to see it.
    2. The history behind it, told as stories about people, not lists of dates.
    3. Things to notice that most visitors walk past.
    4. Food and drink to try that day, and the history in the dish.
    5. The people who live there now: daily life, work, faith, and what tourism means to them, especially in the off-season.
    6. Etiquette and a few useful words, so we can be good guests.
    7. Practical notes: timing, distances, closures, and weather.

    Topics¶

    1. The long timeline of the Eastern Mediterranean, from the Minoans to modern Turkey and Greece
    2. Minoan Crete: Knossos, palace culture, and Europe's first civilization
    3. Troy, the Bronze Age, and how Homer's Iliad blends myth and history
    4. Classical Athens: democracy, philosophy, theater, and the Acropolis
    5. The Hellenistic and Roman worlds: Ephesus as a great Greco-Roman city
    6. Early Christianity in Anatolia and Greece: Paul at Ephesus, the House of the Virgin Mary, and the Orthodox Church
    7. Byzantium and Constantinople: Hagia Sophia, the cistern, and a Christian empire that lasted a thousand years
    8. The Crusades and the Knights Hospitaller of Rhodes
    9. Venice in the Eastern Mediterranean: the forts and harbors of Crete
    10. The Ottoman Empire: Topkapi Palace, the mosques, the bazaars, and a multi-faith empire
    11. The end of empires: Gallipoli, the birth of modern Turkey, Greek independence, and the 1923 population exchange between Greece and Turkey
    12. Jewish communities of the region, including Rhodes' Jewish Quarter and the deportation of its community in 1944
    13. Islam in daily life: prayer times, mosque etiquette, and the call to prayer
    14. Turkish cuisine: breakfast, meze, kebab, simit, tea, coffee, and sweets
    15. Greek and Cretan cuisine: olive oil, bread, cheese, fish, wine, and the Cretan diet
    16. Daily life in Turkey and Greece today: families, work, the economy, faith, and attitudes toward visitors
    17. Being a thoughtful traveler: empathy, etiquette, a few words of Turkish and Greek, and seeing past the tourist script
    18. Reading a ruin: how to look at walls, columns, mosaics, and frescoes and understand what they tell us

    Learning Outcomes¶

    By the end of the trip, we will be able to:

    • Remember: name the main civilizations of the region in order, and the key site that represents each one on our route.
    • Understand: explain why each place mattered in its own time, and how empires, trade, and faith shaped its people.
    • Apply: use the daily chapter to plan each day around opening hours, port times, prayer times, and weather, and to order and enjoy local food with confidence.
    • Analyze: compare how Greek, Roman, Byzantine, Venetian, and Ottoman builders left their marks on the same places, and spot those layers on our own.
    • Evaluate: weigh competing stories about contested history, such as Troy, Gallipoli, Hagia Sophia, and the population exchange, and understand why Turks and Greeks may remember the same events differently.
    • Create: keep a short daily journal of what we noticed, ate, and learned from the people we met, and turn it into our own story of the journey.
