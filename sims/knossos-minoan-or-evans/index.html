    Knossos: Minoan, Evans, or Copy?¶

    Run the Knossos: Minoan, Evans, or Copy? MicroSim Fullscreen

    About This MicroSim¶

    Knossos is the most famous case of reconstruction in Europe. What a visitor sees there comes from three different hands: the Minoans, who built the palace; Arthur Evans, who excavated it from 1900 and rebuilt parts of it between about 1905 and 1930, mainly in reinforced concrete from the 1920s; and the artists who painted copies of frescoes whose originals went to the Heraklion Archaeological Museum. This MicroSim asks one question of ten features: who made this?

    Bin What belongs in it
    Minoan original Built or made by the Minoans and still at the site
    Evans reconstruction Built for Arthur Evans between about 1905 and 1930
    Modern copy A painted copy at the site of an original now in the Heraklion museum

    The cards come in a fixed order. Four are Minoan originals: the lower walls and floor plan around the central court, the gypsum throne, the storage jars in the West Magazines, and the drains and water channels. Three are Evans reconstructions: the red concrete columns at the North Entrance, the concrete upper floor over the Throne Room, and the large horns of consecration. Three are modern copies: the charging bull relief, the dolphin painting in the so-called Queen's Megaron, and the Cup-Bearer.

    Each card is scored once, on the first answer. The reason appears at once and the card joins its correct bin, outlined green if the first choice was right and red if it was not. After answering, What to look for shows the material clue for that card, following the chapter's rule of thumb: check the material, then the color. Opening it is exploration, not part of the score.

    How to Use¶

    1. Read the three bin definitions.
    2. Read the card in the white panel and choose a bin with the buttons below, or press 1 to 3.
    3. Read the feedback. Press What to look for if you want the material clue, then Next card (or Enter).
    4. After the tenth card, press See result for your score. Hover over any card in a bin to review it.
    5. Try Again repeats all ten cards as an unscored practice round; your first-attempt score stays at the top right.

    Lesson Plan¶

    Audience: adult travelers preparing for a morning at Knossos on Day 9.
    Duration: 10–15 minutes.
    Prerequisites: restoration and reconstruction (Chapter 4), the Knossos Palace, Arthur Evans, the Evans reconstructions, the Throne Room, and storage pithoi.
    Learning objective (Bloom: Analyze): Distinguish surviving Minoan remains at Knossos from Arthur Evans's reconstructions and from modern copies of originals now in the Heraklion Archaeological Museum, sorting at least 8 of 10 features correctly on the first attempt.

    Activities¶

    1. Predict (2 min): Before starting, guess how many of the ten features are Minoan originals.
    2. Sort the cards (6 min): For each card, say aloud what it is made of and where the original might be before you choose.
    3. Review the misses (3 min): Hover over any red-bordered card and read its "What to look for" clue. Which of the four misconceptions tripped you: everything old-looking is Minoan, the red columns are original, the outdoor paintings are originals, or the whole site is fake?
    4. On the day: At Knossos, or in the Heraklion museum, find one example from each bin and photograph it with its label.

    Assessment¶

    Mastery is 8 of 10 correct on the first attempt. Follow-up question: A friend says Knossos is "all fake concrete." How would you answer? The plan, the lower walls, the throne, the storage jars, and the drains are Minoan and still in place; much of the height and color is Evans's concrete and modern copies, and the originals of the paintings are in the museum.
