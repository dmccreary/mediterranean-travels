    Eras of the Eastern Mediterranean Timeline¶

    Run the Eras of the Eastern Mediterranean Timeline MicroSim Fullscreen

    About This MicroSim¶

    More than five thousand years separate the first Bronze Age villages from our trip, and that span is hard to feel. This timeline draws the nine periods of Eastern Mediterranean history to scale, from the Bronze Age (c. 3200 BCE) to the modern era (2026 CE), so their lengths can be compared by eye. The Bronze Age fills almost half the line. The Classical period, the age of the Parthenon and Socrates, is a thin sliver, and the Byzantine period is longer than most travelers expect.

    The timeline shows periods only. Events are left out on purpose, so the band lengths are the one thing to look at. Clicking a band names one or two sites on our route from that period. The full version, with 29 events and the chapter that covers each, is the Events of the Eastern Mediterranean Timeline in the appendix.

    The timeline is also a short quiz. It asks six questions about proportion, one at a time. Each answer is committed before the relevant bands are highlighted and their lengths shown, so the display checks the learner's mental picture instead of handing over the answer.

    How to Use¶

    1. Answer question 1. The nine bands are visible at full scale. Choose an answer by judging the band lengths.
    2. Read the feedback. The bands named in the question are highlighted, their lengths in years appear on the bands, and the timeline zooms to them. The feedback explains the answer either way.
    3. Press Next. The view returns to the full span and the next question appears.
    4. After question 6 the right-hand panel shows the final score. Press Full span to see all nine periods, then click any band to read its dates, length, and the sites on our route from that period.
    5. Restart clears the score and returns to question 1.

    Drag the timeline sideways to pan. The mouse wheel scrolls the page, not the timeline.

    Reading the Dates¶

    Years BCE are counted down and years CE are counted up, and there is no year zero. The axis marks the line between them as BCE | CE. The quiz uses the chapter's rule for spans that cross that line: add the two numbers and subtract one. From 31 BCE to 330 CE is 31 + 330 − 1 = 360 years. Dates marked "c." (circa) are approximate. Period boundaries are rounded and vary from place to place, so each band's infobox says so.

    Lesson Plan¶

    Audience¶

    The travelers on this trip: curious adults who love the history of civilization, culture, and food.

    Learning Objective¶

    Compare the order and length of the nine periods of Eastern Mediterranean history by answering six proportion questions before the answers are revealed, scoring at least 5 of 6 (Bloom: Understand, verb: compare).

    Duration¶

    10 minutes

    Prerequisites¶

    The sections "The Historical Timeline" and "BCE and CE Dating" in Chapter 1: historical timeline, period (era), BCE and CE dating, no year zero, and the names of the nine periods.

    Activities¶

    1. Predict (5 min): Answer the six questions in order by eye, without working out the numbers first. Note which answers surprised you.
    2. Explore (3 min): After question 6, click each band and find the period of the site you most want to see.
    3. Reflect (2 min): Using the 24-hour clock from the chapter, say in one sentence why the Classical period feels long but is not.

    Assessment¶

    Mastery is 5 of 6 correct on the first attempt. Exploring the timeline and clicking bands are practice, not evidence. Each question targets a misconception:

    Question Misconception it tests
    1. Bronze Age or Classical? The Classical period was long because it is famous.
    2. How much of the timeline is the Bronze Age? Prehistory is a short prelude to "real" history.
    3. Byzantine or Ottoman? The Byzantine period was a short epilogue to Rome.
    4. Classical + Hellenistic + Roman, or Byzantine? The Byzantine period was a short epilogue to Rome.
    5. Length of the Roman period, 31 BCE to 330 CE? Spans across the BCE/CE line are found by subtracting.
    6. When was writing almost unknown? Writing in Greece was continuous from the Bronze Age on.

    References¶

    1. Bronze Age - Wikipedia - Overview of the period, its metallurgy, and the regional chronologies behind its rounded dates.
    2. Late Bronze Age collapse - Wikipedia - The destruction of palace societies around 1200–1150 BCE and the loss of Linear B.
    3. Byzantine Empire - Wikipedia - The eastern Roman Empire from the founding of Constantinople to 1453.
    4. Year zero - Wikipedia - Why the BC/AD and BCE/CE systems have no year zero, and how astronomers number years.
    5. vis-timeline documentation - vis.js - The JavaScript library used to draw this timeline.
