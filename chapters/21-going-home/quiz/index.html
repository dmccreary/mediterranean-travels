    Quiz: Going Home: Learning With Communities, Not To Them¶

    Test your understanding of the Learning for All lessons gathered across the route, the shadow side of good news, and what it means to widen learning with communities rather than to them with these review questions.

    1. What has UNESCO long said about the language children should learn in?¶

    1. Every child should learn first in a major world language, such as English, to reach the most knowledge
    2. The best medium for teaching is the mother tongue of the pupil, and many people lack education in a language they understand
    3. Children should learn two foreign languages before learning to read their own
    4. Mother-tongue schooling works well even without trained teachers or books in that language
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is B. A 1953 UNESCO report called it axiomatic that "the best medium for teaching is the mother tongue of the pupil." A 2016 UNESCO policy paper asked, "If you don't understand, how can you learn?", and estimated that as much as 40 percent of the world's people lack education in a language they speak or understand. Research finds that a strong first-language start supports a second language. But mother-tongue programs succeed only with trained teachers and books in that language, which rules out option D.

    2. Istanbul printed its first Ottoman book in Arabic script about 275 years after Gutenberg. What lesson does the chapter draw about the cost of delaying a technology?¶

    1. Every new learning technology should be adopted at once, since delay is always the greater harm
    2. The delay was caused by ignorance, since no one in Istanbul knew how printing worked
    3. Delay costs nothing, because manuscripts served readers just as well as print
    4. Count what waiting costs and who pays it, while remembering that printing made copies, not readers, and that rushing has costs too
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is D. The cost of delaying technology is real: books not printed and readers not made. But Istanbul had Jewish, Armenian, and Greek presses long before 1729, so the delay was not ignorance (option B). Even after the press came, most people could not read the script, so printing made copies, not readers. And waiting has an opposite cost: a school can buy AI faster than it can judge it. The lesson is not "adopt everything at once" but "count what waiting costs, and who pays it."

    3. A school district plans to bring AI tutors into every classroom. Applying the lesson of scribal resistance, whom should the planners make sure to hear first?¶

    1. Only the technology vendors, since they understand the tools best
    2. Only the parents of the highest-achieving students, since they will benefit most
    3. The teachers, aides, tutoring companies, and publishers whose skill or living depends on the way things are done now
    4. No one in particular, since resistance to change is always irrational
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is C. Scribal resistance is opposition from people whose skill, income, or prestige depended on an old technology: Homer's reciters, Demetrius the silversmith, Ottoman copyists, and the abbot Trithemius. They were not irrational: Demetrius was right about his market, and the copyists were right that the press would end their way of life, which rules out option D. Resistance is information about who stands to lose. The scribes' successors today, teachers, aides, tutoring companies, and publishers, are owed a hearing.

    4. Drawing up a ledger for the 1928 alphabet reform, the chapter sorts its costs into those that came with any change of script and those that came from the method. Which costs belong to the method?¶

    1. The ban on the old script, the absence of a vote, and the speed set by a one-party state
    2. Children no longer learning the old letters once schools taught the new ones
    3. The new alphabet's one letter for each sound, which made reading easier to learn
    4. The rise in literacy, which happened only because the reform was imposed quickly
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is A. Some costs come with any change of script: once schools teach new letters, children stop learning the old ones unless someone teaches both, which is option B. Others came from choices of method: the ban on the old script, the absence of a vote, and the speed set by a one-party state. Those choices are why the reform is still argued over. The ledger does not say whether the reform was right; it shows that speed bought by command is paid for later, often by the grandchildren.

    5. Which three questions make up the chapter's test for evangelism's shadow?¶

    1. How much does it cost? How fast can it spread? How many will adopt it?
    2. Is the news true? Is it new? Is it popular?
    3. Who paid for it? Who built it? Who will maintain it?
    4. Who chose? What is treated as the obstacle? What happens to those who say no?
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is D. Evangelism's shadow is the harm that follows when good news is imposed rather than chosen, or when its carriers treat those who decline it with contempt. Three questions would have caught each shadow on the route. Who chose? The 1928 alphabet was chosen for Turks, not by them. What is treated as the obstacle? Residential schools treated a child's language as the obstacle. What happens to those who say no? Paul's later readers turned his words against those who declined the message.

    6. An AI reading program for a rural region requires that all lessons be in the national language and asks teachers to discourage children from using their home language in class. Which part of the shadow test does it fail most clearly?¶

    1. "Who chose?", because the program is free of charge
    2. "What is treated as the obstacle?", because it treats the children's own language as something to get around
    3. "What happens to those who say no?", because families can always opt out
    4. It passes all three tests, because the program teaches reading
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is B. The plan treats the children's home language as the obstacle to learning, the same mistake as the church-run residential schools in Canada, where the Truth and Reconciliation Commission reported that many Indigenous children were punished for speaking their own languages. The intention was to bring education; the method taught children that their language, and so their families, stood in the way. A free program can still fail "Who chose?", and nothing in the plan shows families can opt out, so option C assumes too much.

    Concept Tested: Evangelism's Shadow

    See: Evangelism's Shadow

    7. A ministry plans to machine-translate a reading series into a small regional language and send it straight to classrooms. What is the main flaw in the plan, and what is the remedy?¶

    1. AI translates small languages poorly but fluently, so fluent speakers from the community must check samples and fix errors before children see them
    2. AI translation is too expensive for small languages, so the series should stay in the national language
    3. Machine translation is always word-for-word, so it should only be used for science lessons
    4. There is no flaw, because a smooth-sounding translation is a correct translation
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is A. AI translation could put lessons into small languages for a tiny fraction of human translation costs, so option B is wrong. But AI systems learn from digitized text, mostly in a few large languages, so they translate smaller languages poorly, often fluently and wrongly; fluent is not the same as right (option D). The remedy is to involve the speakers, as Masakhane, "we build together," does for African languages, and to have a fluent speaker review a sample before any lesson reaches a child.

    8. The chapter says "the last step is decided at the second." What does it mean?¶

    1. The second year of any reform decides whether it will last
    2. A change should be tested twice before it is announced
    3. Those left out at the end are usually those absent when the opening was designed, so inviting them in early shrinks who is left out
    4. Resistance always comes second, so leaders should ignore early objections
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is C. Chapter 2's four-step pattern runs: learning locked up, opened by a new technology or institution, resisted, and widened but never all the way. The people left out at the fourth step are most often the people who were not in the room when the opening, the second step, was designed. Inviting them in at step two is the cheapest way to shrink step four. That is the core of education with communities: designing, running, and judging a change with the people it serves.

    9. Which example from the route is the strongest case of education done with a community rather than to it?¶

    1. Justinian's law of 529 deciding by belief who could teach philosophy
    2. Turkey's Village Institutes, which trained teachers from village families and sent them home to teach
    3. The Roman Senate's expulsion of Greek philosophers and teachers of rhetoric in 161 BCE
    4. The 1923 population exchange, which sorted millions by religion without asking their language
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is B. Education with communities designs, runs, and judges a change together with the people it serves, in their language and with their hands on how it continues. The Village Institutes trained teachers from village families and sent them home, a piece of the 1928 era built closer to communities. The other options are top-down decisions made far away: Justinian's law, the Senate's expulsion, and the exchange convention that decided where millions would live without asking what language they spoke at home.

    10. You are writing a good news message about AI and education from your visit to Rhodes. Which draft best meets the chapter's four tests?¶

    1. "AI will transform every school in the world, and anyone who resists is standing in the way of progress."
    2. "I read online that Rhodes once had famous teachers, and AI can now replace teachers everywhere."
    3. "AI tutors are cheap and fast, so governments should install them in every classroom this year."
    4. "In Rhodes's Old Town we heard how a list of names helped find every Jewish family in 1944. An AI tutor that knows each child could help, but only if her family and teachers decide what data it keeps."
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is D. A good news message comes from a story we actually saw, is honest about the cost, names who the good news is with, and is heard as good by the people it concerns. Draft D starts from what we learned on Rhodes, names the risk of learner data, and puts the family and teachers in charge. Option A despises those who decline, the shadow's mark. Option B is not a story we saw, and option C is speed by command, with no one asked.
