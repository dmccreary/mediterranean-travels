    Quiz: Day 12: Delphi¶

    Test your understanding of the Sanctuary of Apollo, its monuments and records, the Pythian Games, the museum, and modern Delphi with these review questions.

    1. You arrive at Delphi in late morning after a two-and-a-half-hour drive, and the sun sets about 5:15 PM. What is the best way to plan your visit?¶

    1. See everything on the site and in the museum, since a full day is plenty of time
    2. Decide before arriving which three or four things matter most, since an hour on the site is not enough to linger everywhere
    3. Skip the museum entirely, since the originals are all on the site
    4. Start at the stadium and work down after dark, when the site is quieter
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is B. The day trip is about 180 km and two and a half hours each way. Our own photos on the site span about an hour, enough to walk the Sacred Way to the temple and theater at a steady pace but not to linger. Add the museum, which deserves at least an hour, plus lunch, and much of the drive home is in the dark. The museum holds originals such as the Charioteer, so skipping it (option C) would miss them.

    2. What was the real job of the more than twenty treasuries along the Sacred Way?¶

    1. They were banks where pilgrims deposited money for safekeeping
    2. They were lodgings for envoys waiting to consult the oracle
    3. They were schools where priests trained the Pythia
    4. They held a city's offerings and stood in the god's precinct with the city's name on them, advertising its wealth and piety
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is D. A treasury was not a bank (option A). These small, temple-shaped buildings held gifts of gold, silver, and bronze, ritual equipment, and spoils of war, but their real job was to stand in Apollo's precinct with their city's name on them. Rival cities lined the Sacred Way with them. The Corinthian treasury held Croesus' gifts, and Siphnos built its marble treasury around 525 BCE with young women holding up its porch. Most are now only foundations.

    3. Pausanias says the Athenian Treasury was built from the spoils of Marathon, and a nearby base carries a Marathon inscription. Why is the honest verdict only "possibly"?¶

    1. Many archaeologists date the building's style a few years before 490 BCE, and the inscription belongs to the terrace, which may have held a separate monument
    2. The treasury was built by Sparta, not Athens, and only later taken over
    3. The treasury is a 1906 replica with no original blocks, so it cannot be dated
    4. Pausanias never visited Delphi and invented the story from a distance
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is A. The inscription saying the Athenians dedicated the spoils of Marathon sounds conclusive, but it stands on a triangular terrace beside the treasury, which may have held a separate monument. Many archaeologists date the building's style to around 500 BCE, before Marathon. So Athens certainly used the spot to advertise Marathon, but whether the victory paid for the building is unsettled. The French rebuilt it in 1903–1906 from its original blocks, which rules out option C.

    4. On the polygonal wall you read a record in which a woman is "sold" to Pythian Apollo, with a clause that she must stay and serve her former owner until the owner's death. What does that clause mean?¶

    1. The sale is fake, and she remains enslaved forever
    2. Apollo's priests now own her and will put her to work in the temple
    3. Her freedom is recorded and guaranteed, but under paramone it becomes complete only after years of further service
    4. She must pay the price again each year to keep her freedom
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is C. In a Delphic manumission, the owner "sold" the enslaved person to Apollo, but the money was the person's own savings, and since no one could claim a god's property, the person became free. The record named the archon, a guarantor, and witnesses. Many records add paramone, "remaining": the freed person had to stay and serve, often until the owner's death, before freedom was complete. The god did not keep her as a servant (option B); the sale was the legal route to freedom.

    5. The bronze Serpent Column stands in Istanbul's Hippodrome, while Delphi keeps its base and a bronze copy. Why does the chapter say neither city tells the story alone?¶

    1. Istanbul has the object, Delphi has the place where it stood beside the altar, and the written sources supply the rest
    2. Both columns are modern copies, so the story survives only in books
    3. The column was cut in half, and each city holds one piece of the bronze
    4. Istanbul's column is a different monument with no link to Delphi
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is A. The Serpent Column was cast after Plataea in 479 BCE to hold a golden tripod beside Apollo's altar, and Constantine moved it to his capital by 330 CE. Istanbul has the twisted bronze trunk and the upper jaw of one head; Delphi has the base near the altar and a bronze copy showing how tall it rose. Herodotus gives the spoils and place, Thucydides the erased boast, and Pausanias the loss of the gold in the Third Sacred War. Istanbul's bronze is the original (option B).

    6. Which description fits the Pythian Games after their reorganization in 582 BCE?¶

    1. A purely athletic festival held every year, with prizes of gold coins
    2. A festival every four years that put music first, then athletics and horse races, with a laurel crown as the prize
    3. A chariot race held only for the rulers of Sicily
    4. A festival of drama in which playwrights competed with tragedies
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is B. The Pythian Games began as a contest in singing a hymn to Apollo with the kithara. After the First Sacred War, the Amphictyonic League reorganized them in 582 BCE as a festival every four years, adding athletics and horse races. Music came first in the program, then events in the stadium, then the chariot and horse races on the plain. The prize was a crown of laurel, Apollo's tree. Sicilian rulers did compete in the chariot races, but the games were open to Greeks from every city.

    7. At the round Tholos in the Athena Pronaia sanctuary, a guide says with confidence that it was a shrine to an earth goddess. How should you weigh that?¶

    1. Accept it, because Vitruvius records the building's purpose in detail
    2. Accept it, because all round buildings in Greece were shrines to earth goddesses
    3. Treat it as one proposal among several, since no ancient writer says what the Tholos was for
    4. Reject it, because the Tholos is known to have been the oracle's treasury
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is C. The Tholos, of the early 4th century BCE, had 20 Doric columns outside and 10 Corinthian columns inside. Vitruvius says its architect, Theodorus of Phocaea, wrote a book about it, but no ancient writer says what it was for, so option A is wrong. Scholars have proposed a shrine to an earth goddess, a hero's memorial, a hall for a lost rite, or a treasury. The best-preserved building can still be the least explained, and option D claims a certainty no one has.

    8. Why did the bronze Charioteer of Delphi survive when thousands of other bronzes at the sanctuary did not?¶

    1. It was carried to Rome by Nero and returned in the 19th century
    2. It was kept inside the Temple of Apollo, where looters never entered
    3. It was made of a special alloy that could not be melted down
    4. It was buried and forgotten, probably by the earthquake of 373 BCE, until French excavators found it in 1896
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is D. The Charioteer, made around 478 to 470 BCE for a Pythian chariot victory, was found in 1896 buried near the temple, probably by the earthquake of 373 BCE. Thousands of bronzes once stood at Delphi; Nero alone took about 500, and most of the rest were melted for their metal. This one survived because it was lost, so the museum shows what escaped, not a fair sample. Nero's bronzes went to Rome and did not come back (option A).

    9. What trade-off does the chapter name in the French excavation of Delphi that began in 1892?¶

    1. The excavators destroyed the temple's foundations to reach older layers
    2. The village of Kastri, which stood on the sanctuary, was bought, demolished, and moved so the ruins could be uncovered
    3. Greece gave France the Charioteer and the Delphic Hymns in exchange for the right to dig
    4. The excavators rebuilt the whole sanctuary in concrete, hiding the original stones
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is B. By the time western travelers came, the ruins lay under the mountain village of Kastri, whose houses stood on the terraces and reused ancient stones. After years of negotiation, Greece gave the excavation rights to the French School at Athens; Kastri's houses were bought and demolished, and its people moved to a new village that took the name Delphi. For them it meant losing homes, fields, and family graves. The sanctuary we walk exists because a village was taken away. The Charioteer is in the Delphi museum (option C).

    10. What lesson about students' learning records does the chapter draw from the freedom records on the polygonal wall?¶

    1. A record is trusted when a neutral keeper stands behind it and the person it describes can always point to it, so learning records should be portable and under the learner's control
    2. Records should be carved in stone so that no one can ever correct them
    3. Learning records are best kept private inside each school's own system
    4. Students should not have records of their achievements, since records were once used to control people
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is A. At Delphi, freedom was written in stone in the busiest place in the sanctuary, dated, witnessed, and backed by a guarantor and the god, so a woman whose freedom was questioned could point to the wall. Today a student's learning leaves records in many systems that belong to the system, not the learner, and they disappear or stay locked when the student moves on. Option C describes exactly that problem. The chapter's lesson is a trusted keeper plus records the learner controls.
