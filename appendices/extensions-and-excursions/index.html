    Extensions and Excursions¶

    Viking sells two kinds of add-ons to the Ancient Mediterranean Treasures cruise. Extensions are hotel stays of two to four nights before the ship leaves Istanbul or after it reaches Athens. Shore excursions are guided outings from the ship in each port. This appendix lists both, as Viking showed them for the 2026 Istanbul-to-Athens sailing, and notes which ones we took.

    We booked neither extension. We spent three days on our own in Istanbul before boarding and three days on our own in Athens after leaving the ship, including the drive to Delphi. Comparing our own plan with Viking's packages shows what the packages cover and what we covered by planning ourselves.

    Check before you book

    These lists come from Viking's itinerary pages in November 2026. Viking changes its excursions, prices, and hotels from year to year, so confirm any option on the Ancient Mediterranean Treasures page or in My Viking Journey before booking.

    How the Two Kinds of Add-On Work¶

    Extensions Shore excursions
    When Before embarkation or after disembarkation During each port day
    Length 2–4 nights in a hotel A few hours to a full day
    What is included Hotel, transfers, some meals, and one to three guided tours A guide and transport; some include a meal or tasting
    Cost Extra, priced per extension One excursion per port is included in the fare; the rest cost extra
    Where to book With the cruise booking, or later in My Viking Journey In My Viking Journey, before the trip or on board

    The included excursion in each port is usually the classic introduction: the Old Town walk in Rhodes, the main street of Ephesus, or the archaeological museum in Heraklion. The optional excursions go farther away, go deeper into one subject, or add food, wine, or a boat.

    Pre-Cruise Extensions in Istanbul¶

    Viking offered three ways to arrive early. The starting prices below were Viking's "from" prices for the 2026 sailing.

    Extension Nights From Hotel Highlights
    Istanbul 2 $999 Conrad Istanbul Bosphorus Blue Mosque, Basilica Cistern, Bosphorus cruise with lunch, Grand Bazaar
    Cappadocia 3 $1,799 Conrad Istanbul (1 night), Yunak Evleri cave hotel in Ürgüp (2 nights) Flights to central Anatolia, fairy chimneys, an underground city, Göreme Open Air Museum, optional hot-air balloon ride
    Best of Istanbul 3 $2,799 Four Seasons Istanbul at Sultanahmet Topkapı Palace, Blue Mosque, Hagia Sophia, Basilica Cistern, Beylerbeyi Palace, Spice Bazaar, a countryside brunch

    Istanbul (2 nights) is the short version. One guided day covers the Blue Mosque, the Basilica Cistern, a Bosphorus cruise with a Turkish lunch, and a carpet presentation in the Grand Bazaar. The first and last days are for arriving and moving to the ship.

    Cappadocia (3 nights) is the only extension that leaves the coast. After one night in Istanbul, the group flies to central Anatolia and stays in a hotel carved into the soft volcanic rock. The full day there covers the fairy chimneys of the Dervent Valley, a pottery demonstration in Avanos, an underground city, the Göreme Open Air Museum with its rock-cut churches, and Uçhisar Castle. A dawn hot-air balloon ride is an optional extra. Viking notes that the cave hotel is not recommended for guests who have trouble walking.

    Best of Istanbul (3 nights) is the fully escorted version of what we did on our own. It has three guided tours: the Sultanahmet monuments, a gourmet lunch and the Grand Bazaar, and a day on the Asian shore with Beylerbeyi Palace and the Spice Bazaar.

    What We Did Instead¶

    Our three days in Istanbul before boarding covered most of the Best of Istanbul ground at our own pace:

    • Day 1, Istanbul, a City on Two Continents: the Bosphorus, the Golden Horn, and the city's layout.
    • Day 2, Byzantine Constantinople and Hagia Sophia: Hagia Sophia, the Basilica Cistern, and the Hippodrome.
    • Day 3, Ottoman Istanbul, Topkapı, and Friday Prayer: Topkapı Palace and the Süleymaniye and Blue Mosques.

    The one thing none of our days covered is Cappadocia, which is a flight away. It is the strongest case for an extension on this route, because it cannot be done as a day trip from the ship.

    Shore Excursions by Port¶

    Each port's included excursion is marked Included. All others cost extra. Viking's pages do not list durations or activity levels for each excursion, so check those in My Viking Journey. "The Kitchen Table," offered in most ports, is a market visit with the ship's chefs followed by a dinner on board, and it is left out of the tables below.

    Miletus is where Paul said goodbye to the elders of Ephesus (Acts 20), so the Miletus excursion connects directly to the Paul chapters.

    Our Excursion: The Villages of Crete¶

    Crete was the one port where we chose a village day over the famous ruins. The excursion drove inland from Heraklion to two stops. The first was a vineyard and winery at Avli, with a tasting of Cretan wines. The second was Thrapsano, a village known across Greece for its potters, who throw the tall storage jars called pithoi.

    Those jars connect the village day back to Knossos. The storerooms of the Minoan palace held rows of pithoi almost as tall as a person, used for olive oil, wine, and grain about 3,500 years ago. Thrapsano's potters still make jars in the same shape for the same purposes, and for gardens. A village workshop shows how the craft moved from palace to farm to the present day, something a museum case cannot show.

    The village day also fits the book's habit of asking who lives in a place now. Heraklion's Old Town shows Crete's history. Thrapsano and the vineyards show how many Cretans make a living today: in farming, wine, and crafts, most of it far from the cruise port.

    Post-Cruise Extensions in Athens¶

    The ship arrives in Piraeus on Day 10, and guests leave it on Day 11. Viking offered three ways to stay on in Greece.

    Extension Nights From Style Highlights
    Athens 2 $899 Hotel and transfers, sightseeing on your own Athens Marriott, optional Cape Sounion, Agora, and Plaka outings
    Classic Greece 4 $1,599 Fully guided Acropolis and Acropolis Museum, Mycenae and Epidaurus, Delphi
    Greek Traditions 3 $1,799 Fully guided Nafplio, ancient Nemea, olive oil, honey, wine, and a cooking class

    Athens (2 nights) is mostly a hotel stay with a Viking host, two breakfasts, and transfers. Sightseeing is on your own, with optional guided outings to Cape Sounion, the Agora, and the Plaka.

    Classic Greece (4 nights) is the guided tour of the mainland's great sites: a day for the Acropolis and its museum, a day for Mycenae and Epidaurus in the Peloponnese, and a day for Delphi, with lunch in the mountain village of Arachova.

    Greek Traditions (3 nights) moves to Nafplio, the first capital of modern Greece, and centers on food and farming: an olive oil mill, an apiary, a winery, a cooking class, and the ruins of the Temple of Zeus at Nemea.

    What We Did Instead¶

    Our three days in Athens after the ship follow the Classic Greece route, except for Mycenae and Epidaurus:

    • Day 11, The Agora, Democracy, and Paul on the Areopagus.
    • Day 12, Delphi, a drive of about 2½ hours each way.
    • Day 13, Museums, Tutors, and the City Today.

    Mycenae and Epidaurus are the main sites on this route that we did not reach. They would fill one more day from Athens, as a day trip from the ship or as part of the Classic Greece extension.

    Choosing Well in November¶

    • Short days. Daylight ends before 5:30 PM in Athens in late November, and many sites close earlier in winter. The longest excursions (Delphi from Piraeus, Gallipoli, Lindos, Phaistos) leave little slack.
    • Weather. Open-air sites like Troy, Ephesus, Lindos, and Delphi have little shelter. Bring a rain layer and shoes with grip for wet marble.
    • Walking. Lindos (195 steps), the Acropolis, and Assos involve steep or uneven climbs. The museum excursions are the gentle choice in each port.
    • One deep day beats two shallow ones. In a port with two famous sites, such as Troy and Gallipoli, or Knossos and Heraklion's museum, choose the one you most want to understand and read that chapter the night before.
