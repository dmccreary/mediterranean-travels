# References: Before We Go, Part 2: Traveling Well and the Learning for All Thread

1. [Writing system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Writing_system) - Wikipedia - Explains the main kinds of writing, such as logographic, syllabic, and alphabetic scripts, and how they developed. It supports this chapter's "Sort the Scripts" activity and its section on writing systems.

2. [Greek alphabet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_alphabet) - Wikipedia - Covers the Greek letters, their sounds, their Phoenician origin, and the Greek addition of vowels. Read it before the trip so that this chapter's "Read a Greek Sign" practice feels familiar.

3. [Literacy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Literacy) - Wikipedia - Defines literacy and traces how reading spread from a few scribes to whole populations. It gives background for the Learning for All thread, which begins in this chapter with the question of who can read.

4. *Travel as a Political Act* (2009) - Rick Steves - Nation Books - Steves frames travel as a way to build empathy, arguing that meeting people on their own terms changes how we see our own country. His approach fits this chapter's thoughtful traveler and empathy in travel sections.

5. *The Story of Writing: Alphabets, Hieroglyphs & Pictograms* (2007) - Andrew Robinson - Thames & Hudson - Robinson sets the world's scripts side by side with clear pictures, from cuneiform to the alphabet, and shows how scholars crack unknown scripts. This second edition is a visual companion to this chapter's writing systems section.

6. [Literacy](https://ourworldindata.org/literacy) - Our World in Data - Interactive charts of literacy rates over two centuries, by country and by gender, with sources. They match this chapter's world literacy diagram and let readers check the numbers behind the Learning for All story.

7. [Room to Read](https://www.roomtoread.org/) - Room to Read - A nonprofit that helps children in low-income countries learn to read, writing and printing local-language books and supporting girls' education. It shows that the Learning for All question in this chapter is still a live, global issue.

8. [Elements of AI](https://www.elementsofai.com/) - University of Helsinki and MinnaLearn - A free online course that explains what artificial intelligence is, and what it can and cannot do, for people with no technical background. A good primer for this chapter's sections on AI and AI in education.

9. [Greek Alphabet](https://www.omniglot.com/writing/greek.htm) - Omniglot - A one-page chart of Greek letters with their names, sounds, and a short history of the script. Save it on a phone for reading street signs and museum labels, as this chapter's Greek language section suggests.

10. [Phoenician Alphabet](https://www.omniglot.com/writing/phoenician.htm) - Omniglot - A chart and short history of the Phoenician consonant alphabet that the Greeks borrowed and adapted. It shows the starting point of this chapter's story of the alphabet and why adding vowels mattered.
