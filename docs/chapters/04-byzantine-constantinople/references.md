# References: Day 2: Byzantine Constantinople and Hagia Sophia

1. [Hagia Sophia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hagia_Sophia) - Wikipedia - Covers the building's design, its great dome, its mosaics, and its lives as cathedral, mosque, museum, and mosque again. It is the main background for this chapter's visit to Hagia Sophia.

2. [Justinian I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Justinian_I) - Wikipedia - Describes the emperor who rebuilt Hagia Sophia, survived the Nika Riots, and reorganized Roman law. It fills in the ruler behind this chapter's Hippodrome and Hagia Sophia sections.

3. [Basilica Cistern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basilica_Cistern) - Wikipedia - Explains the huge underground water reservoir built under Justinian, its reused columns, and the two Medusa heads. It supports this chapter's section under the street and its lesson on spolia.

4. *Byzantium: The Surprising Life of a Medieval Empire* (2007) - Judith Herrin - Princeton University Press - Herrin skips the usual parade of emperors and answers the questions people actually ask, in short themed chapters on Hagia Sophia, icons, eunuchs, and Greek fire. Her question-led approach suits this chapter.

5. *Lost to the West: The Forgotten Byzantine Empire That Rescued Western Civilization* (2009) - Lars Brownworth - Crown - Brownworth, a former high school teacher, tells Byzantine history as a string of rulers' stories. His lively chapters on Justinian and Theodora cover the Nika Riots and the building of Hagia Sophia.

6. [Procopius: On the Great Church (Hagia Sophia)](https://sourcebooks.web.fordham.edu/source/procop-deaed1.asp) - Fordham University Internet History Sourcebooks - Justinian's own court historian describes the new church and its dome in his book on the emperor's buildings. A true primary source for this chapter's lessons on sources and on the Hagia Sophia dome.

7. [Procopius: The Nika Riots](https://sourcebooks.web.fordham.edu/source/procop-wars1.asp) - Fordham University Internet History Sourcebooks - Procopius's account of the 532 riot in which chariot-racing fans nearly toppled Justinian. Read it with this chapter's Hippodrome section and ask what the writer may have left out.

8. [In Our Time: Byzantium](https://www.bbc.co.uk/programmes/p00547j9) - BBC Radio 4 - Melvyn Bragg and historians discuss Byzantine culture, faith, and legacy in a free radio program. A relaxed overview of the empire whose church, mosaics, and capital this chapter explores.

9. [A Truthful Record: The Byzantine Institute Films](https://www.doaks.org/resources/online-exhibits/a-truthful-record) - Dumbarton Oaks, Harvard University - An online exhibit of films made while the Byzantine Institute uncovered and conserved mosaics in Hagia Sophia and other churches. It shows the restoration work behind the mosaics this chapter describes.

10. [Ecumenical Patriarchate](https://ec-patr.org/en/) - Ecumenical Patriarchate of Constantinople - The official site of the senior church of Eastern Orthodoxy, still based in Istanbul. It connects to this chapter's sections on the Orthodox Church, the Great Schism of 1054, and the Patriarchate today.
