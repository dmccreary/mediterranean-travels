# References: Paul at Ephesus, Part 2: Ideas, Legacy, and Lessons in Leading Change

1. [Council of Jerusalem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Council_of_Jerusalem) - Wikipedia - Explains the early meeting that decided gentile followers did not need to follow all of Jewish law. It is the starting point for this chapter's section on Paul's central ideas and the "essential versus inherited" question.

2. [Reformation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reformation) - Wikipedia - Covers the sixteenth-century break in Western Christianity led by Luther and others, who drew heavily on Paul's letters. It supports this chapter's section on how Paul's ideas traveled to individual conscience.

3. [Diffusion of innovations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diffusion_of_innovations) - Wikipedia - Describes Everett Rogers's theory of how new ideas spread, from innovators and early adopters to laggards. It supports this chapter's sections on diffusion of innovation and the pilot school as a hub.

4. *Tinkering Toward Utopia: A Century of Public School Reform* (1995) - David Tyack and Larry Cuban - Harvard University Press - Tyack and Cuban named the "grammar of schooling," the age-graded classes, bells, and subjects that survive reform after reform. Their idea is central to this chapter's essential versus inherited discussion.

5. *Leading Change* (1996) - John P. Kotter - Harvard Business School Press - Kotter lays out eight stages of change, from creating urgency and a guiding coalition to anchoring new habits in the culture. His model offers a lens for this chapter's case study of Paul as a change leader.

6. [In Our Time: St Paul](https://www.bbc.co.uk/programmes/b00kjk8z) - BBC Radio 4 - Scholars discuss Paul's influence on the early church and on Christian thought in a free radio program. Good listening for this chapter's sections on grace, faith, and Paul's contested legacy.

7. [Augustine of Hippo](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/augustine/) - Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy - A scholarly entry on Augustine's life and thought, including how he read Paul on grace and the will. It supports this chapter's section on how Paul's ideas traveled.

8. [Antisemitism in History: From the Early Church to 1400](https://encyclopedia.ushmm.org/content/en/article/antisemitism-in-history-from-the-early-church-to-1400) - United States Holocaust Memorial Museum - The museum's encyclopedia traces Christian anti-Judaism from early church writers through the Middle Ages. Essential context for this chapter's honest look at the harmful uses of Paul's words.

9. [What Is the Carnegie Unit?](https://carnegiefoundation.org/what-is-the-carnegie-unit/) - Carnegie Foundation for the Advancement of Teaching - The foundation that created the Carnegie Unit explains what it measures and why it has lasted. Background for this chapter's section on seat time and the Carnegie Unit.

10. [Tinkering Toward Utopia: A Century of Public School Reform](https://www.gse.harvard.edu/ideas/ed-magazine/04/03/tinkering-toward-utopia-century-public-school-reform) - Harvard Graduate School of Education - A Harvard Ed. magazine piece on Tyack and Cuban's account of why many school reforms fade. A quick way into the grammar of schooling before reading the full book.
