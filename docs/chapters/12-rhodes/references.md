# References: Day 8: Rhodes, From the Colossus to the Knights

1. [Colossus of Rhodes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colossus_of_Rhodes) - Wikipedia - Explains the giant bronze statue of Helios, one of the Seven Wonders, how it was built, how an earthquake toppled it, and myths about its pose. It supports this chapter's section on the sun god's city.

2. [Knights Hospitaller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knights_Hospitaller) - Wikipedia - Follows the military religious order from a Jerusalem hospital to its rule of Rhodes and later Malta. It is the background for this chapter's Street of the Knights and Palace of the Grand Master.

3. [Siege of Rhodes (1522)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siege_of_Rhodes_%281522%29) - Wikipedia - Tells how Suleiman the Magnificent's army besieged the knights for months and allowed them to leave with honor. It supports this chapter's section on the siege and the Ottoman period that followed.

4. *The Knights Hospitaller* (2001) - Helen Nicholson - Boydell Press - Nicholson, a leading historian of the military orders, gives a short, clear history of the order from a pilgrim hospital to the loss of Rhodes in 1522. She explains the knights' hospital and walls in this chapter.

5. *Empires of the Sea: The Siege of Malta, the Battle of Lepanto, and the Contest for the Center of the World* (2008) - Roger Crowley - Random House - Crowley tells the sixteenth-century struggle for the Mediterranean as narrative history, beginning with Suleiman's capture of Rhodes. He shows where this chapter's siege of 1522 fits in a wider war.

6. [Medieval Town of Rhodes](http://odysseus.culture.gr/h/3/eh351.jsp?obj_id=7029) - Odysseus, Hellenic Ministry of Culture - The official page on the walled old town, its knights' buildings, and its later layers. Use it with this chapter's "Who Built What We See in the Old Town?" activity.

7. [Lindos](http://odysseus.culture.gr/h/3/eh351.jsp?obj_id=2383) - Odysseus, Hellenic Ministry of Culture - The official history of the Lindos acropolis and its sanctuary of Athena Lindia. Read it before climbing to the acropolis described in this chapter.

8. [Acropolis of Rhodes](http://odysseus.culture.gr/h/3/eh351.jsp?obj_id=2599) - Odysseus, Hellenic Ministry of Culture - The Hellenistic acropolis of the ancient city, with temples, a stadium, and a small theater. It recalls the wealthy city of the Colossus and the school of rhetoric where Romans studied.

9. [Jewish Museum of Rhodes](https://rhodesjewishmuseum.org/) - Jewish Museum of Rhodes - The museum beside the Kahal Shalom Synagogue preserves the history of the island's Sephardic community. It connects to this chapter's Jewish Quarter and the deportation of 1944.

10. [Deportation of the Hasson Family from Rhodes in 1944](https://www.yadvashem.org/exhibitions/last-deportees/rhodes-auschwitz.html) - Yad Vashem - An online exhibit that follows one family through the July 1944 deportation from Rhodes to Auschwitz. It gives a human scale to this chapter's section on the Rhodes deportation.
