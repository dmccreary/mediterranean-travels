# References: Before We Go, Part 1: The Long Timeline of the Eastern Mediterranean

1. [History of the Mediterranean region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_Mediterranean_region) - Wikipedia - Traces the peoples of the Mediterranean from prehistory to modern times, period by period. It gives a backbone for this chapter's walk from the Bronze Age through the Roman, Byzantine, and Ottoman eras.

2. [Ancient Greece](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Greece) - Wikipedia - Covers Greek history from the Mycenaeans through the Archaic, Classical, and Hellenistic periods, including city-states, colonies, and culture. It helps place the Greek eras on this chapter's timeline before we reach Athens.

3. [Anatolia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anatolia) - Wikipedia - Describes the peninsula that is now most of Turkey, from the Hittites and Greek colonies to Romans, Byzantines, and Ottomans. It shows why Troy, Ephesus, and Istanbul share one layered land.

4. *The Making of the Middle Sea: A History of the Mediterranean from the Beginning to the Emergence of the Classical World* (2013) - Cyprian Broodbank - Thames & Hudson - Broodbank tells the story through the sea itself, showing how islands, coasts, winds, and boats shaped Mediterranean peoples. It explains why Crete, the Aegean, and Anatolia mattered so early, as this chapter's map section describes.

5. *The Great Sea: A Human History of the Mediterranean* (2011) - David Abulafia - Oxford University Press - Abulafia writes the sea's history through the people who crossed it, such as traders, pilgrims, and pirates, rather than through geography alone. His approach links every era in this chapter into one human story.

6. [Introduction to Ancient Greek History (CLCV 205)](https://oyc.yale.edu/classics/clcv-205) - Open Yale Courses - Donald Kagan's free lecture course runs from the Bronze Age through the Peloponnesian War. Its lectures on the Dark Age, the polis, and the Archaic period fill in this chapter's early eras.

7. [Internet Ancient History Sourcebook](https://sourcebooks.web.fordham.edu/ancient/asbook.asp) - Fordham University - A free library of translated primary sources arranged by era, from the ancient Near East and Greece to Rome and early Christianity. It lets readers hear each period on this chapter's timeline in its own voice.

8. [Greeks](https://www.bbc.co.uk/history/ancient/greeks/) - BBC History - Short, illustrated articles on Greek city-states, Athens and Sparta, the Olympic Games, and the Greek legacy. A gentle starting point for the city-state, Archaic, and Classical periods described in this chapter.

9. [Pleiades](https://pleiades.stoa.org/) - Institute for the Study of the Ancient World, New York University - A free, searchable gazetteer of ancient places with maps and coordinates. Use it to locate Troy, Ephesus, Knossos, and Delphi and see the names each place carried in different eras.

10. [Odysseus: Monuments, Archaeological Sites, and Museums](http://odysseus.culture.gr/index_en.html) - Hellenic Ministry of Culture - The Greek government's official guide to its heritage sites and museums, with histories and photos. Browse it to see how the Bronze Age, Classical, Byzantine, and Ottoman layers in this chapter appear on the ground.
