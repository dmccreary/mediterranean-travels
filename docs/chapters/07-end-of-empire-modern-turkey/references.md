# References: Day 5: The End of Empire and the Birth of Modern Turkey

1. [Mustafa Kemal Atatürk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mustafa_Kemal_Atat%C3%BCrk) - Wikipedia - Follows Atatürk from Ottoman officer and Gallipoli commander to founder and first president of the Republic of Turkey. It is the main background for this chapter's Atatürk and Memorial Day sections.

2. [Atatürk's reforms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atat%C3%BCrk%27s_reforms) - Wikipedia - Lists the political, legal, religious, and cultural reforms of the 1920s and 1930s, including secularism and the new alphabet. It supports this chapter's sections on Atatürk's Republic and secularism in Turkey.

3. [Population exchange between Greece and Turkey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Population_exchange_between_Greece_and_Turkey) - Wikipedia - Explains the 1923 agreement that moved about 1.6 million people based on religion rather than language or birthplace. It matches this chapter's "Who Was Exchanged in 1923?" diagram.

4. *Atatürk: The Biography of the Founder of Modern Turkey* (1999) - Andrew Mango - John Murray - Mango built his biography on Turkish-language sources, separating the man from the myth. He follows Mustafa Kemal from Gallipoli through the War of Independence to the reforms covered in this chapter.

5. *Twice a Stranger: How Mass Expulsion Forged Modern Greece and Turkey* (2006) - Bruce Clark - Granta - Clark tells the 1923 population exchange through interviews with surviving refugees on both sides of the Aegean, as well as archives. He gives human faces to this chapter's population exchange section.

6. [Treaty of Lausanne](https://wwi.lib.byu.edu/index.php/Treaty_of_Lausanne) - World War I Document Archive, Brigham Young University - The English text of the 1923 treaty that replaced Sèvres and set the borders of modern Turkey. Read it alongside this chapter's "Sèvres or Lausanne?" diagram.

7. [Turkey Profile: Timeline](https://www.bbc.com/news/world-europe-17994865) - BBC News - A chronology of key events from the Ottoman conquest of 1453 through the Republic to recent years. It helps place Atatürk's reforms, secularism, and today's Hagia Sophia debate in order.

8. [Anıtkabir](https://www.anitkabir.tsk.tr/) - Anıtkabir Command, Republic of Türkiye - The official site of Atatürk's mausoleum in Ankara, with its history and museum. It connects to this chapter's Atatürk Memorial Day, when Turkey pauses each year on November 10.

9. [The Greek War of Independence: Re-appraising Its Economic Legacies](https://www.lse.ac.uk/lse-player/the-greek-war-of-independence-re-appraising-its-economic-legacies) - London School of Economics - A recorded LSE panel on what the revolution of 1821 left to the modern Greek state. It extends this chapter's sections on the Greek War of Independence and the modern Greek state.

10. [Mustafa Kemal Atatürk Introduces the New Turkish Alphabet](https://www.ieg-ego.eu/en/mediainfo/mustafa-kemal-ataturk-fuhrt-das-neue-turkische-alphabet-ein) - European History Online (EGO) - A 1928 photograph of Atatürk teaching the new Latin-based letters in public, with a short explanation of the reform. It illustrates this chapter's Learning for All section on the alphabet reform of 1928.
