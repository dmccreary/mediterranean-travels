# References: Day 6: Troy and Gallipoli

1. [Troy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Troy) - Wikipedia - Covers the site at Hisarlık, its nine main layers, the excavations by Calvert, Schliemann, and later teams, and the link to Homer. It is the main background for this chapter's morning at Troy.

2. [Iliad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iliad) - Wikipedia - Summarizes Homer's epic of the wrath of Achilles, its characters, its oral style, and its place in Greek culture. It supports this chapter's sections on Homer, oral tradition, and the Trojan War.

3. [Gallipoli campaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gallipoli_campaign) - Wikipedia - Explains the 1915 Allied attempt to force the Dardanelles, the Anzac landings, Ottoman defense under Mustafa Kemal, and the cost on both sides. It is the background for this chapter's afternoon at Gallipoli.

4. *In Search of the Trojan War* (1985) - Michael Wood - BBC Books - Wood treats the Trojan War as a detective case, testing Homer's story against Schliemann's digs, Hittite tablets, and Bronze Age archaeology. His method matches this chapter's question of how much of the legend is history.

5. *1177 B.C.: The Year Civilization Collapsed* (2014) - Eric H. Cline - Princeton University Press - Cline explains the Bronze Age collapse as a chain reaction of drought, earthquakes, invasions, and broken trade across linked kingdoms. He sets Troy, the Hittites, and Mycenae in this chapter's wider world.

6. [Çanakkale Archaeological Site of Troy](https://www.turkishmuseums.com/museum/detail/2020-canakkale-archaeological-site-of-troy/2020/4) - Turkish Museums, Ministry of Culture and Tourism - The official visitor page for the Troy site, with a short history and visiting information. Check it before this chapter's shore excursion to the Hisarlık mound.

7. [Homer, Iliad](http://www.perseus.tufts.edu/hopper/text?doc=Perseus:text:1999.01.0134) - Perseus Digital Library, Tufts University - Homer's Iliad in English with links to the Greek text and dictionaries. Read the opening book, or Hector's farewell in Book 6, before standing on the mound at Hisarlık.

8. [Milman Parry Collection of Oral Literature](https://mpc.chs.harvard.edu/) - Harvard University - The archive of recordings Milman Parry and Albert Lord made of South Slavic singers composing long epics aloud. It is the evidence behind this chapter's oral tradition section and its Homeric singer activity.

9. [In Our Time: The Trojan War](https://www.bbc.co.uk/programmes/b01j6srl) - BBC Radio 4 - Historians and classicists discuss whether the Trojan War happened and what the archaeology shows. A free listen that matches this chapter's look at the historicity of Troy.

10. [Gallipoli](https://www.awm.gov.au/articles/encyclopedia/gallipoli) - Australian War Memorial - The Memorial's encyclopedia entry on the 1915 campaign, with dates, losses, and the Anzac landing. Read it before this chapter's visit to Anzac Cove and the Çanakkale Martyrs' Memorial.
