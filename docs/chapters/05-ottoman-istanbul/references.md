# References: Day 3: Ottoman Istanbul, Topkapı, and Friday Prayer

1. [Topkapı Palace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Topkap%C4%B1_Palace) - Wikipedia - Describes the sultans' palace, its courtyards, harem, treasury, and Sacred Relics chamber, and its later life as a museum. It is the main background for this chapter's palace visit.

2. [Mimar Sinan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mimar_Sinan) - Wikipedia - Tells the life and work of the Ottoman chief architect who designed the Süleymaniye and hundreds of other buildings. It supports this chapter's section on Suleiman, Sinan, and Ottoman mosque architecture.

3. [Five Pillars of Islam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Five_Pillars_of_Islam) - Wikipedia - Explains the declaration of faith, daily prayer, charity, fasting, and pilgrimage. It gives the framework for this chapter's section on Islam as it is lived, including prayer times and Friday prayer.

4. *Constantinople: City of the World's Desire, 1453–1924* (1995) - Philip Mansel - John Murray - Mansel tells Ottoman history through the capital and its court, from palace kitchens and the harem to sultans and diplomats. His approach makes the rooms and rituals of Topkapı in this chapter feel lived in.

5. *Islam: A Short History* (2000) - Karen Armstrong - Modern Library - Armstrong explains Islamic history from the inside, stressing how believers understood their faith in each era. A short, readable background for this chapter's Five Pillars, daily prayer, and mosque etiquette sections.

6. [Islam](https://www.bbc.co.uk/religion/religions/islam/) - BBC Religion - BBC's guide to Islamic beliefs, worship, and practice, including prayer, the mosque, and the Five Pillars. Useful before visiting a working mosque and reading this chapter's mosque etiquette section.

7. [Religious Architecture and Islamic Cultures](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/4-614-religious-architecture-and-islamic-cultures-fall-2002/) - MIT OpenCourseWare - Free course materials from MIT on mosque design across the Islamic world, with readings and lecture notes. They help explain this chapter's mihrab, minbar, minaret, and Ottoman domed mosque.

8. [Süleymaniye Mosque](https://www.ktb.gov.tr/EN-113783/suleymaniye-mosque.html) - Republic of Türkiye Ministry of Culture and Tourism - The ministry's description of Sinan's masterpiece and its setting on the hill above the Golden Horn. Read it before visiting to connect the mosque to this chapter's sections on Sinan and the külliye.

9. [The Age of Sultan Süleyman the Magnificent](https://www.nga.gov/sites/default/files/migrate_images/content/dam/ngaweb/research/publications/pdfs/age-of-sultan.pdf) - National Gallery of Art - A free but very large PDF of Esin Atıl's 1987 exhibition catalogue of Ottoman court arts, including manuscripts, textiles, and İznik ceramics. It shows the treasures and tiles described in this chapter.

10. [Istanbul Crossroads: Treasure House](https://festival.si.edu/2002/the-silk-road/istanbul-treasure-house/smithsonian) - Smithsonian Folklife Festival - A Smithsonian essay on Topkapı Palace as the Ottoman seat of government and its treasures, including Chinese porcelain. A short read before visiting the palace treasury.
