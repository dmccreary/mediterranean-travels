# References: Going Home: Learning With Communities, Not To Them

1. [Multilingual education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multilingual_education) - Wikipedia - Explains teaching in more than one language, starting from a child's first language and adding others. It is the main background for this chapter's section on mother-tongue learning.

2. [Machine translation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Machine_translation) - Wikipedia - Traces automatic translation from rule-based systems to neural networks, including limits for languages with little data. It supports this chapter's section on AI translation.

3. [Democratization of knowledge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratization_of_knowledge) - Wikipedia - Describes how access to knowledge has widened through printing, libraries, schools, and the internet. It sums up the Learning for All thread that this chapter gathers together.

4. *Pedagogy of the Oppressed* (1970) - Paulo Freire - Herder and Herder - Freire contrasted "banking" education, where teachers deposit facts into students, with dialogue in which learners name their own world. His idea underpins this chapter's case for learning with communities, not to them.

5. *Geek Heresy: Rescuing Social Change from the Cult of Technology* (2015) - Kentaro Toyama - PublicAffairs - Toyama, after years running technology projects in India, argues that technology only amplifies the intent and capacity people already have. A caution for this chapter's sections on top-down reform and evangelism's shadow.

6. [Why Mother Language-Based Education Is Essential](https://www.unesco.org/en/articles/why-mother-language-based-education-essential) - UNESCO - UNESCO explains why children learn best when taught first in a language they understand. It is a source for this chapter's section on mother-tongue learning.

7. [Loud and Clear: Effective Language of Instruction Policies for Learning](https://www.worldbank.org/en/topic/education/publication/loud-and-clear-effective-language-of-instruction-policies-for-learning) - World Bank - A World Bank policy paper arguing that poor language of instruction choices harm learning, access, and equity. It supports this chapter's argument for teaching in learners' own languages.

8. [Technology in Education: GEM Report 2023](https://www.unesco.org/gem-report/en/publication/technology) - UNESCO Global Education Monitoring Report - UNESCO's global review of what education technology has and has not achieved, asking whose terms it serves. It supports this chapter's sections on costs of top-down reform and technology evangelism.

9. [Global Education](https://ourworldindata.org/global-education) - Our World in Data - Charts on school enrollment, years of schooling, and learning outcomes around the world over time. They give the big-picture data behind this chapter's good news message and its shadow.

10. [Masakhane](https://www.masakhane.io/) - Masakhane - A grassroots research community building language technology for African languages, led by African researchers. A living example of this chapter's idea of AI translation built with communities rather than for them.
