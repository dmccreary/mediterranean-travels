# References: Day 1: Istanbul, a City on Two Continents

1. [Bosporus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bosporus) - Wikipedia - Describes the narrow strait that splits Istanbul between Europe and Asia, its currents, and its long role in trade and war. It explains the water that made the city, this chapter's starting point.

2. [Walls of Constantinople](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walls_of_Constantinople) - Wikipedia - Details the city's land and sea walls, especially the Theodosian Walls with their moat, outer wall, and inner wall. It matches this chapter's diagram of the wall layers.

3. [Fall of Constantinople](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fall_of_Constantinople) - Wikipedia - Tells the 1453 siege step by step, from Mehmed II's great cannons to the ships dragged overland into the Golden Horn and the final assault. Use it alongside this chapter's siege diagram.

4. *Istanbul: A Tale of Three Cities* (2017) - Bettany Hughes - Weidenfeld & Nicolson - Hughes tells the city's story as three cities in one place, Byzantium, Constantinople, and Istanbul, digging down through its layers like an archaeologist. Her framing mirrors this chapter's city with three names.

5. *1453: The Holy War for Constantinople and the Clash of Islam and the West* (2005) - Roger Crowley - Hyperion - Crowley rebuilds the siege day by day from eyewitness accounts on both sides of the walls. His narrative makes Mehmed the Conqueror and the city's last defenders vivid for this chapter's fall of Constantinople.

6. [The Early Middle Ages, 284–1000 (HIST 210)](https://oyc.yale.edu/history/hist-210) - Open Yale Courses - Paul Freedman's free lecture course covers Constantine, the new capital, and the early Byzantine Empire. It adds depth to this chapter's story from Byzantium to Constantinople.

7. [Theodosian Code XVI.i.2: Banning of Other Religions](https://sourcebooks.web.fordham.edu/source/theodcodeXVI.asp) - Fordham University Internet History Sourcebooks - English excerpts of the laws under Theodosius I that made Nicene Christianity the empire's official religion. Readers can study the Theodosian decrees discussed in this chapter in their own words.

8. [The Siege of Constantinople in 1453: Primary Sources](https://www.deremilitari.org/RESOURCES/SOURCES/siegeofconstantinople.htm) - De Re Militari - Translated eyewitness accounts of the siege, including the diaries of Nicolò Barbaro and George Sphrantzes. They pair with this chapter's siege diagram and show how historians know what happened.

9. [In Our Time: Constantinople Siege and Fall](https://www.bbc.co.uk/programmes/p0038xbd) - BBC Radio 4 - A free radio discussion with historians on why the Ottomans took the city in 1453 when earlier sieges had failed. Good listening on the flight or the first evening in Istanbul.

10. [Turkish Coffee Culture and Tradition](https://ich.unesco.org/en/RL/turkish-coffee-culture-and-tradition-00645) - UNESCO Intangible Cultural Heritage - UNESCO's listing explains how Turkish coffee is made, served, and shared as a sign of friendship and hospitality. It connects to this chapter's food as history section and the coffee we will taste.
