# References: Day 7: Ephesus, a Greco-Roman City

1. [Ephesus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ephesus) - Wikipedia - Covers the city's history from Greek colony to Roman capital of Asia, its harbor, its monuments, and its role in early Christianity. It is the main background for this chapter's walk.

2. [Library of Celsus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Library_of_Celsus) - Wikipedia - Explains the library built in the second century CE as a tomb and memorial for Celsus, its facade statues, and its reconstruction. It supports this chapter's section on the library and Marble Street.

3. [Temple of Artemis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Temple_of_Artemis) - Wikipedia - Tells the history of the great temple near Ephesus, one of the Seven Wonders of the Ancient World, and its rebuilding. It supports this chapter's section on the goddess at the gate.

4. *St. Paul's Ephesus: Texts and Archaeology* (2008) - Jerome Murphy-O'Connor - Liturgical Press - Murphy-O'Connor gathers what ancient writers said about Ephesus and sets it beside the excavated ruins, ending with Paul's years in the city. His method suits this chapter's walk and the silversmiths' riot.

5. *The Classical Language of Architecture* (1980) - John Summerson - Thames & Hudson - Summerson teaches the classical orders as a language with grammar and vocabulary, so readers learn to read columns and facades. This revised edition of his BBC talks helps with this chapter's Doric, Ionic, or Corinthian activity.

6. [Ephesus](https://www.ktb.gov.tr/EN-398042/ephesus.html) - Republic of Türkiye Ministry of Culture and Tourism - The ministry's overview of the ancient city as a Roman port with a harbor channel, temples, and public buildings. Background for this chapter's sections on Kuşadası, Selçuk, and harbor silting.

7. [Ephesus](https://www.livius.org/articles/place/ephesus/) - Livius - An illustrated guide to the city's history and monuments by the ancient historian Jona Lendering. Use it to review Curetes Street, the Library of Celsus, and the theater before or after the visit.

8. [Ephesus](https://www.bibleodyssey.org/articles/ephesus/) - Bible Odyssey, Society of Biblical Literature - A scholarly article on Ephesus in the New Testament world, including Paul's stay in the city. It connects the ruins to this chapter's Great Theater and the riot of the silversmiths.

9. [Vitruvius on Architecture, Book IV](https://penelope.uchicago.edu/Thayer/E/Roman/Texts/Vitruvius/4*.html) - LacusCurtius, University of Chicago - The Roman architect's own account of the Doric, Ionic, and Corinthian orders and the stories of their origins. A primary source for this chapter's classical orders section.

10. [Ephesus: A Journey Back in Time](https://artsandculture.google.com/story/IgXxWeaH8McUaA) - Google Arts & Culture - An illustrated online story from Türkiye's Directorate General of Cultural Assets and Museums, with photos of the Library of Celsus and other monuments. A visual review of this chapter's walk.
