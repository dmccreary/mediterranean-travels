# References: The Oracle and the Algorithm, Part 1: The Oracle

1. [Pythia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pythia) - Wikipedia - Describes the priestess who spoke for Apollo at Delphi, how consultations worked, and the theories about vapors from the ground. It is the main background for this chapter's sections on the Pythia and the consultation.

2. [Oracle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oracle) - Wikipedia - Explains oracles as a practice across ancient cultures, with Delphi as the most famous Greek example. It supports this chapter's opening on the human need for foresight and prediction.

3. [Croesus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Croesus) - Wikipedia - Tells the story of the rich king of Lydia who asked Delphi whether to attack Persia and misread the answer. It supports this chapter's section on the Croesus prophecy and hedged answers.

4. *The Delphic Oracle: Its Responses and Operations* (1978) - Joseph Fontenrose - University of California Press - Fontenrose sorted every known Delphic answer into historical, quasi-historical, legendary, and fictional, and found the famous riddles were mostly legend. His catalog is a key tool for this chapter's "Famous Answers, Tested."

5. *The Oracle: The Lost Secrets and Hidden Message of Ancient Delphi* (2006) - William J. Broad - Penguin Press - Broad, a science journalist, follows the geologists and chemists who tested whether gas from a fault inspired the Pythia. His account explains this chapter's geological fault theory and the debate around it.

6. [Herodotus, The Histories, Book 1, Chapter 53](http://www.perseus.tufts.edu/hopper/text?doc=Perseus:text:1999.01.0126:book=1:chapter=53) - Perseus Digital Library, Tufts University - Herodotus's account of Croesus asking the oracles whether to march on Persia, in English with the Greek text. Read the source behind this chapter's Croesus prophecy.

7. [Plato, Apology, Section 21a](http://www.perseus.tufts.edu/hopper/text?doc=Perseus:text:1999.01.0170:text=Apol.:section=21a) - Perseus Digital Library, Tufts University - Socrates tells the jury how Chaerephon asked Delphi whether anyone was wiser than Socrates. The primary source for this chapter's section on Chaerephon's question.

8. [Plutarch, On the Failure of Oracles](https://penelope.uchicago.edu/Thayer/E/Roman/Texts/Plutarch/Moralia/De_defectu_oraculorum*.html) - LacusCurtius, University of Chicago - Plutarch, who served as a priest at Delphi, discusses why oracles declined and mentions the vapor said to inspire the Pythia. An ancient insider's view for this chapter's doubts and debates.

9. [Delphi](https://www.livius.org/articles/place/delphi/) - Livius - An illustrated guide to the sanctuary's history and monuments by the ancient historian Jona Lendering. It supports this chapter's sections on the Sanctuary of Apollo, the temple, and the Amphictyonic League.

10. [The Ancients Were Right: Delphi Was a Gas!](https://www.eurekalert.org/news-releases/805301) - Geological Society of America, via EurekAlert! - The 2001 news release on research that found crossing faults and ethylene in springs at Delphi. It shows the scientific claim behind this chapter's geological fault theory.
