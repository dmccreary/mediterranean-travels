# References: Day 11: Athens, the Agora, Democracy, and Paul on the Areopagus

1. [Athenian democracy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Athenian_democracy) - Wikipedia - Explains how the Assembly, the Council, the courts, selection by lot, and ostracism worked, and who counted as a citizen. It is the main background for this chapter's section on rule by the people.

2. [Ancient Agora of Athens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Agora_of_Athens) - Wikipedia - Describes the marketplace and civic center below the Acropolis, its stoas, the Temple of Hephaestus, and its excavation. It supports this chapter's walk through the Agora.

3. [Areopagus sermon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Areopagus_sermon) - Wikipedia - Summarizes Paul's speech to the Athenians in Acts 17, including the altar to an unknown god and his quotation of Greek poets. It supports this chapter's section on Paul on the Areopagus.

4. *Democracy: A Life* (2016) - Paul Cartledge - Oxford University Press - Cartledge writes a biography of democracy, following it from Athens in 508 BCE to today and showing that ancient Greek democracies were many and varied. He supports this chapter's "Who Could Vote in Athens?" activity.

5. *The Trial of Socrates* (1988) - I. F. Stone - Little, Brown - Stone investigates the trial as a reporter would, asking how a city proud of free speech could put a philosopher to death. A lively companion to this chapter's section on the trial of Socrates.

6. [Ancient Agora of Athens and Areios Pagos](http://odysseus.culture.gr/h/3/eh351.jsp?obj_id=2485) - Odysseus, Hellenic Ministry of Culture - The official page on the Agora and the Areopagus hill, with history and visiting details. Read it before this chapter's walk from the Stoa to the Areopagus.

7. [About the Excavations](https://ascsa.edu.gr/excavations/athenian-agora/about-the-excavations) - American School of Classical Studies at Athens - The school explains its excavation of the Agora since 1931 and the rebuilt Stoa of Attalos that houses the museum. It shows how we know what this chapter describes.

8. [Pnyx](http://odysseus.culture.gr/h/3/eh351.jsp?obj_id=2580) - Odysseus, Hellenic Ministry of Culture - The official page on the hill where the Athenian Assembly met to debate and vote. It supports this chapter's section on the Pnyx and democracy's workplaces.

9. [Stoicism](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/stoicism/) - Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy - A scholarly overview of Stoic ideas about reason, virtue, and nature, from Zeno's Painted Porch onward. It helps with this chapter's "Paul's Speech Through Stoic Ears" activity.

10. [Stages of Concern](https://www.air.org/resource/stages-concern-concerns-based-adoption-model) - American Institutes for Research - Explains the stages of concern people move through when asked to adopt a new practice, part of the Concerns-Based Adoption Model. It is the source behind this chapter's meeting people where they are section.
