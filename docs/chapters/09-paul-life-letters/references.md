# References: Paul at Ephesus, Part 1: The Apostle's Life, Letters, and Ephesian Years

1. [Paul the Apostle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_the_Apostle) - Wikipedia - Covers Paul's life from Tarsus and his Pharisee training through his conversion, journeys, letters, and death. It is the main background for this chapter's life of Paul.

2. [Pauline epistles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pauline_epistles) - Wikipedia - Lists the thirteen letters attributed to Paul and explains which seven scholars treat as undisputed and why others are debated. It supports this chapter's section on the letters.

3. [Koine Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Koine_Greek) - Wikipedia - Describes the common Greek spread by Alexander's conquests, the everyday language of the eastern Roman world and the New Testament. It explains how Paul could write to communities across the region.

4. *Paul: A Biography* (2018) - N. T. Wright - HarperOne - Wright writes Paul's life as a story of journeys rather than a theology textbook, opening each chapter with a map of his travels. His approach follows the road to Damascus and the missionary journeys in this chapter.

5. *Paul: The Pagans' Apostle* (2017) - Paula Fredriksen - Yale University Press - Fredriksen places Paul firmly inside first-century Judaism, a Jew preaching Israel's God to gentiles who expected the end of the age. Her framing helps with this chapter's sections on Pharisees, Jewish law, and gentiles.

6. [Introduction to New Testament History and Literature (RLST 152)](https://oyc.yale.edu/religious-studies/rlst-152) - Open Yale Courses - Dale Martin's free lectures on the New Testament include several on Paul's letters and the Book of Acts. They support this chapter's sections on the Pauline letters and the Book of Acts.

7. [Paul](https://www.bibleodyssey.org/articles/paul/) - Bible Odyssey, Society of Biblical Literature - A short scholarly article on Paul's life and letters written for general readers. A trustworthy companion to this chapter's "Who Says So?" activity on sorting claims about Paul.

8. [From Jesus to Christ: The First Christians](https://www.pbs.org/wgbh/pages/frontline/shows/religion/) - PBS Frontline - The website for Frontline's documentary series, with scholars' interviews and essays on Jesus, Paul, and the early Jesus movement. Background for this chapter's sections on Jesus of Nazareth and his first followers.

9. [Socrates](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/socrates/) - Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy - A careful scholarly entry on Socrates, his method of questioning, and the sources for his life. It supports this chapter's opening section on Greek philosophy in Athens.

10. [ORBIS: The Stanford Geospatial Network Model of the Roman World](https://orbis.stanford.edu/) - Stanford University - An interactive map that estimates travel time and cost across the Roman Empire by road, river, and sea. Try plotting Paul's journeys from Tarsus to Ephesus, as in this chapter's journeys diagram.
