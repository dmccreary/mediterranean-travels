# References: The Oracle and the Algorithm, Part 2: The Algorithm

1. [Machine learning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Machine_learning) - Wikipedia - Explains how computer systems learn patterns from training data instead of following hand-written rules. It is the main background for this chapter's section on learning from examples and predictive models.

2. [Large language model](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Large_language_model) - Wikipedia - Describes how large language models are trained to predict the next word and what they can and cannot do. It supports this chapter's sections on next-word prediction and temperature.

3. [Self-fulfilling prophecy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Self-fulfilling_prophecy) - Wikipedia - Explains how a prediction can cause itself to come true, with examples from schools, banks, and policing. It supports this chapter's "When a Prediction Feeds Itself" diagram.

4. *Superforecasting: The Art and Science of Prediction* (2015) - Philip E. Tetlock and Dan Gardner - Crown - Tetlock ran forecasting tournaments and scored every prediction, showing that accuracy and calibration can be measured and improved. His method is central to this chapter's "How Calibrated Are You?" activity.

5. *Weapons of Math Destruction* (2016) - Cathy O'Neil - Crown - O'Neil, a mathematician, shows how hidden models in hiring, lending, and policing create feedback loops that harm the people they score. Her cases match this chapter's sections on data bias and predictive policing.

6. [Neural Networks](https://www.3blue1brown.com/?topic=neural-networks) - 3Blue1Brown - Grant Sanderson's animated video series explains how neural networks learn and how transformer models predict text. Clear visuals for this chapter's sections on machine learning and large language models.

7. [Artificial Intelligence](https://ourworldindata.org/artificial-intelligence) - Our World in Data - Charts and articles on AI's growth, including training compute and test performance over time. They support this chapter's sections on AI capability forecasting and doubling curves.

8. [Machine Bias](https://www.propublica.org/article/machine-bias-risk-assessments-in-criminal-sentencing) - ProPublica - A 2016 investigation of a risk score used in U.S. courts that found unequal error rates by race. A classic case for this chapter's discussion of data bias and keeping a human in the loop.

9. [Measuring AI Ability to Complete Long Tasks](https://metr.org/blog/2025-03-19-measuring-ai-ability-to-complete-long-tasks/) - METR - A 2025 study finding that the length of tasks AI agents can complete has been doubling about every seven months. It is a source for this chapter's AI doubling curves and prediction horizon.

10. [Machine Learning Crash Course](https://developers.google.com/machine-learning/crash-course) - Google for Developers - A free course with short videos and exercises on training data, models, and fairness. A hands-on next step after this chapter's plain-language explanation of machine learning.
