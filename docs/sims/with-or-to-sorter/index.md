## About This MicroSim

Chapter 2 drew a line that runs through the whole trip: a change in learning
can be made *with* the people it affects or *to* them. This MicroSim asks us to
judge eight cases against four questions that stay on screen the whole time:

1. Who chose it?
2. Whose language is it in?
3. Who was consulted?
4. Who keeps it going?

| # | Card | Judgment | From |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Greek traders adopt the Phoenician letters and add vowels, about 800 BCE | Done with people | Chapter 2 |
| 2 | The Roman Senate expels Greek philosophers and teachers of rhetoric, 161 BCE | Done to people | Chapter 12 |
| 3 | Justinian's law forbidding pagans to teach, 529 CE | Done to people | Chapter 4 |
| 4 | The ibn Nahmias brothers print a code of Jewish law in Istanbul, dated 1493 | Done with people | Chapter 6 |
| 5 | Carnegie library buildings, given only if the town pays for books, staff, and upkeep, 1883–1929 | Done with people | Chapter 10 |
| 6 | Turkey's Latin-based alphabet, imposed by law in 1928 with the old script banned | Done to people | Chapter 7 |
| 7 | One English-language AI tutor installed in every primary school, without asking anyone | Done to people | Imagined AI project |
| 8 | Village teachers and parents choose and check their AI reading tutor's stories and language | Done with people | Imagined AI project |

Two cards carry the lesson. The 1928 alphabet widened literacy and still sits
in "Done to people", because whether a reform helped and how it was made are
separate questions. The Carnegie libraries were paid for by one of the richest
men of his age and still sit in "Done with people", because each town chose its
library and kept paying for it. The two imagined AI projects carry the same
judgment into our own time: AI is not top-down in itself; it depends on who
chose it and who keeps checking it.

**Notes on the evidence.** Cards 1 to 6 summarize stories told in the chapters
listed. Each is simplified to its decision. Historians debate how directly
Justinian's law of 529 ended philosophical teaching in Athens, and the date of
the Hebrew press is the one printed in its colophon, 1493, which some scholars
place about a decade later. Cards 7 and 8 are illustrative scenarios written
for this book, not reports of real projects, and the sim labels them
"Imagined AI project".

## How to Use

1. Read the four judging questions and card 1. The cards come in a fixed
   order, one at a time, and none can be skipped.
2. Place the card: press **Done with people** or **Done to people**, click a
   bin, or press W or T. Feedback appears at once, and only this first
   placement counts toward the score "First try: n of 8".
3. If the first placement was wrong, you may move the card to the other bin.
   It is marked "moved", and the score does not change.
4. Press **Next card** (or Enter) to go on. After card 8, press
   **See result** to see the score and both bins with all eight cards. Hover
   over any card to read it and its feedback again.
5. **Start over** clears every placement.

## Lesson Plan

**Audience:** adult travelers reading Chapter 21, "Going Home: Learning With
Communities, Not To Them", on the flight home.  
**Duration:** 10 minutes.  
**Prerequisites:** costs of top-down reform, evangelism's shadow, education with
communities, mother-tongue learning, and Chapter 2's with-versus-to
distinction; the route stories in Chapters 2, 4, 6, 7, 10, and 12.  
**Learning objective (Bloom: Evaluate):** Judge whether each of eight efforts
to widen or control learning, six from the trip and two imagined AI projects,
was done with the people it affected or done to them, sorting at least 6 of 8
correctly on the first attempt.

### Activities

1. **Predict (1 min):** Before starting, decide whether a reform that worked
   can still be "done to people". Keep your answer in mind for card 6.
2. **Judge (6 min):** Work through the eight cards, answering the four
   questions for each before placing it. Note the first-try score.
3. **Discuss (3 min):** Pick a technology project at a school you know. Answer
   the four questions for it. Which answer would you most want to change, and
   who would need to be in the room to change it?

### Assessment

Mastery is at least 6 of 8 cards placed correctly on the first try. A
follow-up question: *A charity gives every school in a region free tablets in
the children's home language, but teachers were not asked and no one is paid
to repair them. With or to?* A strong answer uses the questions: the language
fits, but the choice, the consultation, and the upkeep all sit outside the
community, so it leans toward "done to", and it names what would change that.
