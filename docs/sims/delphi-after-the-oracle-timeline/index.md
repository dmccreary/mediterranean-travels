## About This MicroSim

The ruins we walk at Delphi were not always visible. After the oracle closed,
the sanctuary was built over, and for centuries it lay under a mountain
village called Kastri. Its return happened in four stages:

| Stage | What happens to the site |
|---|---|
| Silenced | The oracle closes and the sanctuary is abandoned or built over. |
| Found | Travelers identify the site from ancient texts and inscriptions. |
| Dug up | Excavation reveals and recovers the sanctuary and its finds. |
| Shown | The site and its finds are rebuilt, displayed, performed in, or recognized for visitors. |

This MicroSim reveals nine events one at a time, in date order. Each starts
with only its date and label. We predict its stage first; then the description,
the correct stage, and the reason appear, and the event drops into its stage's
row on the timeline.

| # | Date | Event | Stage |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 391–392 CE | Theodosian Decrees: sacrifice banned, temples closed, the oracle falls silent | Silenced |
| 2 | 1436 | Cyriac of Ancona visits and copies inscriptions | Found |
| 3 | 1676 | Jacob Spon and George Wheler identify the village of Kastri as Delphi | Found |
| 4 | 1892 | The French School at Athens, led by Théophile Homolle, begins the great excavation after Kastri is moved west | Dug up |
| 5 | 1896 | Excavators find the bronze Charioteer buried near the temple | Dug up |
| 6 | 1903 | The first museum opens to display the finds | Shown |
| 7 | 1903–1906 | The Athenian Treasury is rebuilt from its original blocks, paid for by the city of Athens | Shown |
| 8 | 1927 | Angelos and Eva Palmer-Sikelianos stage *Prometheus Bound* at the first Delphic Festival | Shown |
| 9 | 1987 | Delphi is listed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site | Shown |

Two patterns stand out at the end. More than a thousand years separate the
silence from the first rediscovery: from 392 to 1436 CE is 1,044 years. And
four of the nine events are about how Delphi is **shown**, not how it was
found. What we see today was shaped by museums, rebuilding, and festivals as
well as by the excavators.

**About the time scale.** No events fall between 400 and 1400 CE, so the
timeline squeezes those 1,000 years ten to one into a hatched band. Everything
else is drawn to scale, and the final view labels the full length of the gap.
The modern events crowd together, so from event 4 on the timeline moves to a
close-up of 1880–2000; **Full span** and **Close-up** switch between the two.

**Notes on the evidence.** The dates follow the chapter. The French
excavation is often called the *Grande Fouille*, "the great dig", and ran
from 1892 to 1903; the moving of Kastri's houses to the new village took
several years around the start of the dig, so "after Kastri is moved" is a
simplification. The Delphic Festivals were held twice, in 1927 and 1930; the
timeline shows the first. A Christian town occupied the site in late
antiquity before the village of Kastri grew on its ruins, so "silenced" means
the end of the oracle, not of all life on the slope.

## How to Use

1. Read the four stages on the colored buttons.
2. Read the event's date and label. Press the stage you predict (or keys 1
   to 4). Only your prediction counts, and it cannot be changed.
3. The description, the correct stage, and the reason appear. The event's
   badge moves from the **Next** row into its stage's row; a dashed red
   outline marks a missed prediction.
4. Press **Next event** (or Enter). After event 9, press **See all stages**
   to see every event by stage with the 1,044-year silence marked.
5. Hover over or click any badge to read it again. **Restart** clears all
   predictions.

## Lesson Plan

**Audience:** adult travelers reading Chapter 18, "Day 12: Delphi", before
walking from the modern village to the site.  
**Duration:** 10 minutes.  
**Prerequisites:** the closing of the oracle and the Theodosian Decrees
(Chapters 3 and 17), the village of Kastri, the French excavation of Delphi,
the Athenian Treasury, the Charioteer of Delphi, the Delphi Archaeological
Museum, and the Delphic Festivals in the Theater of Delphi (Chapter 18).  
**Learning objective (Bloom: Understand):** Classify nine events in Delphi's
history after the oracle into the four stages silenced, found, dug up, and
shown, with at least 8 of 9 correct, predicting each event's stage before its
description is revealed.

### Activities

1. **Read the stages (1 min):** Say in your own words the difference between
   *found* and *dug up*.
2. **Predict (6 min):** Work through the nine events in order.
3. **Discuss (3 min):** On the final view, why might travelers in 1436 and
   1676 have recognized Delphi without digging at all? What did the people of
   Kastri lose so that event 4 could happen?

### Assessment

Mastery is at least 8 of 9 predictions correct. A follow-up question: *Is the
rebuilt Athenian Treasury part of how Delphi was found, or how it is shown?*
A strong answer says *shown*: it was already found and dug up, and rebuilding
it from its blocks made it something visitors can see.

7. [Athenian Treasury](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Athenian_Treasury) — Wikipedia. The treasury rebuilt from its original blocks in 1903–1906 with funds from the city of Athens.
8. [Eva Palmer-Sikelianos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eva_Palmer-Sikelianos) — Wikipedia. The Delphic Festivals of 1927 and 1930 and the staging of *Prometheus Bound* in the ancient theater.
