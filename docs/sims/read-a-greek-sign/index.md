## About This MicroSim

Most Greek capital letters are the same as ours, and a few more are familiar
from mathematics. A handful of look-alikes cause nearly all the trouble: Η is
the vowel *i*, not *h*; Ρ is *r*, not *p*; Χ is *ch*, not *x*; Υ is *y*,
sounding *ee*; and Ω is *o*, not *w*. This MicroSim gives twelve signs we are
likely to see on the trip and asks us to type each one in Latin letters.

| # | Sign | Spelled on signs | Meaning | Sounds like |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | ΑΘΗΝΑ | athina | Athens | a-THEE-na |
| 2 | ΠΕΙΡΑΙΑΣ | peiraias | Piraeus, the port of Athens | pee-reh-AHS |
| 3 | ΡΟΔΟΣ | rodos | Rhodes | RO-thos |
| 4 | ΗΡΑΚΛΕΙΟ | irakleio | Heraklion | ee-RA-klee-o |
| 5 | ΚΝΩΣΟΣ | knosos | Knossos | kno-SOS |
| 6 | ΔΕΛΦΟΙ | delfoi | Delphi | del-FEE |
| 7 | ΑΚΡΟΠΟΛΗ | akropoli | Acropolis | a-KRO-po-lee |
| 8 | ΜΟΥΣΕΙΟ | mouseio | Museum | moo-SEE-o |
| 9 | ΕΙΣΟΔΟΣ | eisodos | Entrance | EE-so-thos |
| 10 | ΕΞΟΔΟΣ | exodos | Exit | EX-o-thos |
| 11 | ΚΛΕΙΣΤΟ | kleisto | Closed | klee-STO |
| 12 | ΑΝΟΙΧΤΟ | anoichto | Open | a-nee-KHTO |

The spellings follow ELOT 743, the standard used on Greek road and site signs.
Common informal spellings are accepted too, such as *pireas*, *knossos*,
*delphi* and *klisto*. Capital letters, spaces, hyphens and accent marks are
ignored when checking an answer. Each pronunciation's stressed syllable
matches the word's Wiktionary entry.

## How to Use

1. Read the sign and type it in Latin letters in the box below. Press
   **Check** or Enter.
2. Press **Letter key** at any time to see all 24 Greek capitals with their
   sign spellings. Press **Hide key** to return.
3. If the first try is wrong, the feedback names the sign's trap letters.
   Try once more.
4. After a correct answer or a second miss, the sign appears letter by letter,
   each Greek letter above its Latin letter, with trap letters in red. A sign
   counts as read if it is correct within two tries.
5. Press **Next sign** (or Enter) to continue. After the twelfth sign, the
   summary lists every sign and tallies the trap letters in the signs you
   missed at least once.
6. **Restart** clears all answers.

## Lesson Plan

**Audience:** adult travelers preparing for the trip.  
**Duration:** 10–15 minutes.  
**Prerequisites:** alphabet, the Greek language, the Greek capital letters and
their sign spellings, and the letter pairs ΟΥ, ΑΙ, ΕΙ and ΟΙ.  
**Learning objective (Bloom: Apply):** Use the Greek letter table to
transliterate twelve Greek signs from our route into Latin letters, with at
least 10 of 12 correct within two attempts each.

### Activities

1. **Warm up (2 min):** Open the Letter key and find the five look-alikes: Η,
   Ρ, Χ, Υ and Ω. Say what each one really is.
2. **Read (8 min):** Work through all twelve signs. Type each one before
   opening the key; use the key only on a second try.
3. **Sound it out (3 min):** For ΚΛΕΙΣΤΟ and ΑΝΟΙΧΤΟ, read the pronunciation
   aloud with the stress in the right place. These are the two words that
   matter most on a November afternoon.

### Assessment

Mastery is 10 of 12 signs read within two tries. A follow-up question:
*A sign in Heraklion reads ΛΙΜΑΝΙ. Type it in Latin letters.* It reads
*limani*, "harbor."
