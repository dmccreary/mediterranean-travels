## About This MicroSim

The front of an olive oil bottle makes a claim; the back label holds the
evidence. This MicroSim shows nine illustrative bottles, one at a time. For
each, we read the claim on the front, turn the label to read the back, and
judge whether the back supports the front. Beside the bottle is the
standard we judge by:

| Rule | Standard |
|---|---|
| Extra virgin | Free acidity 0.8% or less (and no taste defects) |
| Virgin | Free acidity 2.0% or less |
| Olive oil (refined and virgin blend) | Free acidity 1.0% or less; never extra virgin |
| Lampante | Free acidity above 2.0%; not fit to eat until refined |
| "Cold extraction" | Only for virgin or extra virgin oil kept below 27 °C |
| Freshness | Read the harvest date, not the best-before date |
| Origin | Where the olives were grown and pressed, not where the oil was bottled |
| Calories | Every olive oil has about 120 kcal per tablespoon |

Three labels support their claims and six do not. The pair of "cold
extraction" labels, one at 25 °C and one at 32 °C, and the three honest
labels keep us from rejecting every marketing phrase on sight: the skill is
checking a claim against evidence, not distrusting all claims. After each
judgment the back label's deciding detail is highlighted in yellow.

The labels are invented for practice and do not describe real products.

## How to Use

1. Read the claim on the front of the bottle.
2. Press **Turn the label** (or T) to read the back, and compare each
   detail with the standard on the right.
3. Choose **Supports the claim** (S) or **Does not support the claim** (D).
   Only the first judgment counts.
4. Read the feedback and the highlighted detail, then press **Next label**
   (or Enter).
5. After label 9, press **See result** for a summary of all nine. Hover
   over a numbered circle to review any label. **Try Again** starts over.

## Lesson Plan

**Audience:** adult travelers preparing for Day 8 on Rhodes, and anyone
buying olive oil in a Greek shop.  
**Duration:** 10–15 minutes.  
**Prerequisites:** olive oil, the four grades and their free-acidity
limits, malaxation, cold extraction below 27 °C, harvest date versus
best-before date, and calories per tablespoon (all in the chapter section
"Olive Oil").  
**Learning objective (Bloom: Evaluate):** Judge whether each of nine olive
oil labels supports the claim on its front, using the EU grade limits, the
cold-extraction rule, harvest dates, and origin wording, with at least 7 of
9 correct on the first attempt.

### Activities

1. **Read the standard (2 min):** Before the first label, name the one
   number that separates extra virgin from virgin oil.
2. **Judge the nine labels (8 min):** For each, say aloud which back-label
   detail you are using before choosing.
3. **Find the pattern (2 min):** In the summary, group the six unsupported
   labels by the kind of detail that decided them: acidity, temperature,
   ingredients, harvest, or origin.
4. **In the shop (on the day):** Read the back of two real bottles in the
   Old Town and judge their front claims the same way.

### Assessment

Mastery is 7 of 9 correct on the first attempt. Follow-up question: *A
bottle says "Extra virgin olive oil, cold extraction" and "best before
March 2028," with no harvest date. What can and can't you conclude?* The
cold-extraction claim means it was kept below 27 °C, but without a harvest
date we cannot tell whether the oil is from this season.
