## About This MicroSim

The Fourth Crusade was called to attack Egypt. It ended by sacking Christian
Constantinople. This timeline lays out the seventeen events that explain how,
in three rows:

| Row | What it follows |
|---|---|
| Church | The excommunications of 1054 in Hagia Sophia, a symbol of the drift between Rome and Constantinople |
| Crusades | From Urban II's call at Clermont in 1095 to the sack of April 1204 |
| Byzantium and Venice | Manzikert, Venice's trading privileges, the arrests of 1171, the massacre of 1182, and the Latin Empire to its end in 1261 |

Each event is numbered in date order. Clicking an event, or stepping through
them with **Previous event** and **Next event**, shows its date and
description. Four cause questions then ask which earlier event best explains
a later one. The rows run separately for most of the period and converge in
1202 to 1204, when a debt to Venice, a Byzantine prince's unpaid promise, and
a generation of anger at Western merchants met outside the city walls.

The timeline corrects four common ideas: that the Fourth Crusade set out to
attack Constantinople; that the schism of 1054 was a single clean break felt
by all Christians at once; that the Crusades were a single war; and that
Venice and Byzantium were always enemies (event 3 shows them as allies).

## How to Use

1. Read the prompt in the yellow bar, then scan the three rows from 1054 to
   1261.
2. Press **Next event** to read the events in date order, or click any event.
   Hover over an event to see its title and date.
3. Press **Close-up: 1200–1205** to separate the crowded events of the Fourth
   Crusade, and **Full span** to return. Drag the timeline sideways to pan.
4. Answer the four cause questions on the right. Each is scored once; the
   correct answer is shown, and the events it points to are highlighted.
5. **Restart** clears the questions and starts again.

## Lesson Plan

**Audience:** adult travelers preparing for Day 2 in Istanbul.  
**Duration:** 15 minutes.  
**Prerequisites:** the Crusades, the Great Schism of 1054, the Fourth
Crusade, the Republic of Venice, the Byzantine Empire and the Seljuk Turks.  
**Learning objective (Bloom: Understand):** Explain how earlier events from
1054 to 1203 led to the sack of Constantinople in 1204, by choosing the
earlier event that best explains each of four later events, with at least 3
of 4 correct.

### Activities

1. **Read in order (5 min):** Step through all seventeen events with
   **Next event**. At each one, say which row it belongs to.
2. **Find the convergence (2 min):** Open the close-up. Name the events of
   1202 to 1205 and the row each comes from.
3. **Answer the cause questions (5 min):** For each question, find the
   earlier event on the timeline before choosing.
4. **Explain it aloud (3 min):** In three sentences, explain to a travel
   companion how a crusade bound for Egypt ended in Constantinople. Use at
   least three event numbers.

### Assessment

Mastery is 3 of 4 cause questions correct on the first attempt. A follow-up
question: *Why is it wrong to say that the Schism of 1054 caused the sack of
1204?* The schism soured relations, but the direct causes were the crusaders'
debt to Venice and Alexios IV's unpaid promise; the pope in fact
excommunicated the crusaders for attacking a Christian city at Zara.
