## About This MicroSim

Placing a site in its era comes down to one question: when was it *built*?
This MicroSim gives fifteen cards, each naming a building or object we will
see on the trip and the date it was built. Our job is to drop each one into
the right period on the chapter's timeline:

| Period | Dates used in this guide |
|---|---|
| Bronze Age | c. 3200–1100 BCE |
| Iron Age | c. 1100–800 BCE |
| Archaic Period | c. 800–480 BCE |
| Classical Period | 480–323 BCE |
| Hellenistic Period | 323–31 BCE |
| Roman Period | 31 BCE–330 CE |
| Byzantine Period | 330–1453 CE |
| Ottoman Period | 1453–1922 CE |

The ninth period, the Modern era, has no bin because none of the cards needs
it. A boundary year belongs to the period it begins, so 480 BCE is Classical
and 1453 CE is Ottoman.

Two of the cards are traps. The Palace of the Grand Master in Rhodes was built
by crusader knights, not Byzantines, but its 14th-century date puts it in the
Byzantine period: a period name tells *when*, not always *who*. The Stoa of
Attalos in Athens was rebuilt in the 1950s, but it belongs to the Hellenistic
period, when King Attalos II first built it.

## How to Use

1. Read the card in the white panel: what it is and when it was built.
2. Click the period button under the bin where you think it belongs. The
   buttons sit in timeline order, under the bins they fill.
3. The feedback appears at once. The correct bin is outlined in green, a wrong
   choice in red, and the card settles into its correct bin. Each card has one
   attempt.
4. Press **Next card** to continue. The score in the top right counts correct
   first attempts.
5. After the fifteenth card, press **See result**. All fifteen cards now sit in
   their bins along the timeline; missed cards have a red border. Hover over any
   card to read its date and explanation again.
6. **Restart** reshuffles the cards and clears the score.

## Lesson Plan

**Audience:** adult travelers preparing for the trip.  
**Duration:** 10–15 minutes.  
**Prerequisites:** historical timeline, BCE and CE dating, the nine periods and
their dates, and the idea that a period name tells when, not always who.  
**Learning objective (Bloom: Apply):** Apply the chapter's period boundaries to
assign each of 15 sites and objects on the trip to the period in which it was
built, with at least 12 of 15 correct on the first attempt.

### Activities

1. **Warm up (2 min):** Before starting, cover the bin headers and try to say
   the eight periods in order with their boundary years. Then check against
   the headers.
2. **Sort (6 min):** Work through all fifteen cards. For each one, say the
   boundary years aloud before clicking: "537 is after 330 and before 1453."
3. **Review the traps (3 min):** After the result, hover over the Palace of the
   Grand Master and the Stoa of Attalos. Explain in one sentence each why their
   period is not the one their builders or restorers might suggest.
4. **Extend (3 min):** Apply the same rule to Hagia Sophia's later life. It was
   converted to a mosque in 1453. In which period was it *built*, and in which
   period did that first change happen?

### Assessment

Mastery is 12 of 15 correct on the first attempt. A follow-up question: *The
Venetian Koules fortress in Heraklion was rebuilt in stone between 1523 and
1540. Which period holds that date, and who ruled Crete then?* The date falls in
the Ottoman period on our timeline, but Venice, not the Ottomans, ruled Crete
until 1669: the same lesson as the Grand Master's Palace.
