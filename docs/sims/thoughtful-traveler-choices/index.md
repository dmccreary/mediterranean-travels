## About This MicroSim

The six thoughtful-traveler principles are easy to agree with and harder to
use when a real person is standing in front of us. This MicroSim puts them to
work on eight situations from our route. Each situation has three responses: a
weak one, a tempting one, and the one that best fits the principles. We commit
to one, and the MicroSim marks the best response and names the principle that
decides it.

| Principle | Short form shown on screen |
|---|---|
| Curiosity | Ask and look closer |
| Humility | We are guests |
| Consent | People and sacred places are not scenery |
| Reciprocity | Fair exchange, no false promises |
| Patience | Fit the place's rhythm |
| Many stories | Listen to more than one account |

The tempting answers are the interesting ones. Most of them sound polite:
keeping a conversation with a steward short, refusing a carpet seller's tea,
photographing a street vendor from far enough away that she does not notice,
promising to "come back later." Each one quietly avoids contact with the
person in front of us. The feedback explains why the best response treats that
person as someone with a whole life rather than as part of the view.

All eight situations are illustrative, written for this guide. The Grand
Bazaar's Sunday closing and Friday as the main day of congregational prayer
come from our trip itinerary.

## How to Use

1. Read the situation and the three responses. The six principles stay at the
   top of the screen for reference.
2. Press **A**, **B** or **C** (or type the letter) to commit to a response.
   Each situation allows one choice.
3. The best response turns green, a different choice turns red, and the
   deciding principle lights up at the top. The feedback panel explains why.
4. Press **Next situation** (or Enter) to continue. The score in the top right
   counts best choices.
5. After the eighth situation, press **See summary**. It lists every
   situation, its deciding principle, our choice, and how many times each
   principle decided.
6. **Restart** clears all answers and the score.

## Lesson Plan

**Audience:** adult travelers preparing for the trip.  
**Duration:** 10–15 minutes.  
**Prerequisites:** thoughtful traveler, the six principles (curiosity,
humility, consent, reciprocity, patience, many stories), tourist script,
empathy, and cognitive and emotional empathy.  
**Learning objective (Bloom: Evaluate):** Judge which of three responses best
fits the six thoughtful-traveler principles in each of eight situations on our
route, choosing the best response in at least 7 of 8 situations on the first
attempt.

### Activities

1. **Warm up (2 min):** Before starting, say each principle's question aloud
   from the chapter's table, for example "Whose home, workplace, or holy place
   is this?" for Humility.
2. **Judge (6 min):** Work through all eight situations. Before pressing a
   button, name the principle that you think decides the case.
3. **Compare (3 min):** On the summary, find the two principles that decided
   two situations each. What do the two Consent cases share? What do the two
   Patience cases share?
4. **Extend (3 min):** Write a ninth situation for a stop we have not covered,
   such as the Day 9 visit to Knossos, with one weak, one tempting and one best
   response. Which principle decides it?

### Assessment

Mastery is 7 of 8 best choices on the first attempt. A follow-up question:
*Response B in the Gallipoli situation, politely pointing out what the film
said, sounds respectful. Which principle does it miss, and what would a better
first move be?* It misses Many stories: the better move is to listen first and
ask what the battle means to the guide's own family.
