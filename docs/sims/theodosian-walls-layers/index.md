## About This MicroSim

The Theodosian land walls, completed in 413 under Theodosius II, were not one
wall but a defense in depth about 60 m deep. An army coming from Thrace, in
the west, met five layers in turn:

| Order | Layer | Approximate size |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Moat | About 20 m wide and several meters deep, with a low battlemented wall on its inner edge |
| 2 | Outer terrace | A narrow strip between the moat and the outer wall |
| 3 | Outer wall | About 8.5 m high and 2 m thick, with smaller towers of its own |
| 4 | Inner terrace (*peribolos*) | About 15 to 20 m wide |
| 5 | Inner wall | About 12 m high and nearly 5 m thick, with 96 towers up to about 20 m tall |

The MicroSim shows a schematic cross-section with five numbered positions
and the five layer names in random order. The drawing keeps the relative
heights right (the moat below ground, the inner wall and its towers taller
than the outer wall) but is not to scale, and all the sizes are approximate:
they vary along the 5.7 km of wall, and sources give slightly different
figures.

Part 1 asks us to place the names before anything is labeled. After one
submission each position is marked, the sizes appear on the drawing, and
each position's callout explains what the layer was for. Part 2 asks three
questions: why the inner wall was taller, which emperor completed the walls,
and how the crusaders got in in 1204.

## How to Use

1. Read the question at the top: *An army arrives from the west. What does it
   meet first, and what last?*
2. Click a name below the drawing, then click a numbered box to place it.
   You can also drag a name onto a box. Box 1 is the outside, toward Thrace;
   box 5 is next to the city.
3. Rearrange freely: clicking a filled box sends its name back, and placing a
   name in a filled box swaps the old name out.
4. When all five boxes are filled, press **Submit**. There is one submission.
   Each box turns green or red and shows the correct layer.
5. Click any box, or any numbered marker on the drawing, to read that layer's
   size and purpose.
6. Answer the three questions in Part 2, one attempt each, pressing **Next
   question** after each. The final line gives both scores.
7. **Restart** clears both parts and reshuffles the names.

## Lesson Plan

**Audience:** adult travelers preparing for the trip.  
**Duration:** 10 minutes.  
**Prerequisites:** moat, terrace, tower, breach, outer wall, inner wall, and
*peribolos*.  
**Learning objective (Bloom: Remember):** Identify the five layers of the
Theodosian land walls in the order an attacker from the west meets them, with
no more than one placement error.

### Activities

1. **Recall (3 min):** Without looking back at the chapter, place the five
   names and submit.
2. **Read the callouts (3 min):** Open all five callouts in order, 1 to 5.
   For each, say what an attacker standing there could and could not do.
3. **Answer Part 2 (2 min):** Answer the three questions. Notice that
   question 1 asks about the design and questions 2 and 3 about the walls'
   history.
4. **Connect to the trip (2 min):** At Yedikule or Edirnekapı, the moat is now
   gardens and the outer wall is broken in places. Predict which layers will
   still be easy to see, and which will need imagination.

### Assessment

Mastery is at least 4 of 5 positions correct on the first submission. A
follow-up question: *In 1453 Mehmed II's cannon broke sections of the outer
and inner walls near the Gate of St. Romanus. Why did each breach still leave
the defenders a chance?* A strong answer says that a breach in the outer wall
put attackers on the inner terrace, under fire from the higher inner wall,
and that the defenders rebuilt breaches at night.
