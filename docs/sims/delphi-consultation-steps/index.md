## About This MicroSim

A consultation at Delphi was not a matter of walking in and asking. It was a
sequence of ritual steps, and several of them were gates that could stop a
consultant before the god was ever asked. No ancient writer sets out the whole
procedure in one place, so the order used here is the chapter's reconstruction,
pieced together from Plutarch, Herodotus, Euripides, Aeschylus, inscriptions
and the plan of the temple.

The MicroSim has two parts.

**Part 1: put the day in order.** Eight step cards appear shuffled. We click
them into eight slots and press **Check order**. Each broken rule is named,
for example *The goat is tested before it is sacrificed*, and each card is
marked green if it sits in a place it can hold in a valid order. Steps 2 and 3
(purifying at the Castalian Spring and paying the fee) may come in either
order; every other step has one place. After a second invalid Check, the sim
shows the chapter's order so the walk can begin.

| Step | What happens | Main source |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Arrive on a consultation day, usually said to be the seventh of the month, in one of the nine months when Apollo was present | Plutarch, *Greek Questions* 9 (originally only on the seventh of Bysios) |
| 2 | Purify with water from the Castalian Spring | Euripides, *Ion* |
| 3 | Pay the fee (*pelanos*); a foreigner comes through a Delphian sponsor (*proxenos*) | Euripides, *Ion*; Delphic inscriptions |
| 4 | The priests sprinkle a goat with cold water to test whether the god is present | Plutarch, *On the Obsolescence of Oracles* |
| 5 | The goat is sacrificed on the great altar before the temple's east front | Plutarch; the altar of the Chians |
| 6 | Wait for a turn: those with *promanteia* first, the rest by lot | Herodotus 1.54; Aeschylus, *Eumenides* |
| 7 | Enter the temple, make an offering, and ask; the Pythia answers from the tripod in the adyton | Plutarch; the Aegeus cup in Berlin |
| 8 | Receive the answer from the prophet, write it down, and carry it home | Herodotus 7.142 |

**Part 2: walk through the day.** The consultation then plays out one step at
a time. At steps 4, 6 and 8, a checkpoint stops the walk and asks us to commit
to one of four answers before the outcome is shown:

- **A, the goat test.** The goat does not tremble. What happens? There is no
  consultation: the god is absent. Plutarch tells of priests who pressed on
  after poor omens; the Pythia answered in a strange voice, rushed out
  screaming, and died a few days later.
- **B, the line.** A Lydian envoy of Croesus and a farmer from Attica are both
  waiting. The Lydian goes first: Herodotus says the Delphians gave Croesus and
  all Lydians *promanteia*, the right to consult first, and freedom from fees.
- **C, the answer.** About 100 CE, the answer comes in plain prose. By
  Plutarch's day the god no longer answered in hexameter verse, and Plutarch
  wrote a dialogue defending the change.

**What is uncertain.** The step order is a modern reconstruction. The monthly
calendar is the usual reading of the sources, but it rests on late and
scattered evidence; Plutarch says that in early times the god answered only
once a year. How much the prophets and other officials shaped the Pythia's
words is still debated, and the step 8 card follows the chapter in having the
prophet hand over the answer.

## How to Use

1. Hover over a card on the right to read it in full in the panel below.
2. Click a card to put it in the next empty slot on the left. Click a placed
   card to take it back. **Clear** empties all the slots.
3. When all eight are placed, press **Check order** (or Enter). Fix any broken
   rules and check again; there are two Checks.
4. Press **Begin the walk**, then **Next step** (or Enter) to move through the
   day.
5. At a checkpoint, choose one of the four answers (or press keys 1 to 4). Only
   one answer counts. Read the outcome and the source, then go on.
6. After step 8, press **See the summary**. **Try Again** reshuffles the cards
   and starts over.

## Lesson Plan

**Audience:** adult travelers preparing to visit Delphi.  
**Duration:** 10–15 minutes.  
**Prerequisites:** the Pythia, the adyton, the *pelanos*, the *proxenos*,
*promanteia*, the prophet, the Castalian Spring, the goat test, and verse and
prose answers (Chapter 17, Parts Two and Three).  
**Learning objective (Bloom: Understand):** Explain the sequence of a Delphic
consultation, from arrival to the written answer, by placing eight steps in a
valid order and choosing the correct outcome at three checkpoints.

### Activities

1. **Predict the gates (2 min):** Before touching a card, list what could stop
   a consultant from ever reaching the god.
2. **Order the day (5 min):** Place the eight cards and check. For each broken
   rule, explain *why* that step has to come first.
3. **Walk and predict (5 min):** At each checkpoint, commit to an answer before
   reading the outcome, then connect the outcome to its source.
4. **Discuss (3 min):** Who benefited from each gate: the god, the priests, the
   town of Delphi, or the favored consultants?

### Assessment

Mastery is a valid order within two Checks and at least 2 of 3 checkpoints
correct on the first attempt. As a follow-up, ask: *Name two gates that could
keep a consultant from hearing the god's answer, and say who controlled each.*
A strong answer names the calendar (only consultation days, only nine months a
year), the goat test (controlled by the priests), or the line (*promanteia* for
favored cities and people, the lot for everyone else).
