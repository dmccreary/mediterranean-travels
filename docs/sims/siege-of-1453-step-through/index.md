## About This MicroSim

Constantinople did not fall to one cannon or one clever trick. It had five
defenses, and Mehmed II's campaign took them apart one at a time over more
than a year. This MicroSim walks through eight turning points, from the
building of Rumeli Hisarı in 1452 to the final assault before dawn on May 29,
1453, and asks the same question at each one: *which of the city's defenses
does this defeat?*

The six possible answers are the same at every step:

| | Answer |
|---|---|
| A | The strait: supply from the Black Sea |
| B | The chain across the Golden Horn |
| C | The land walls |
| D | Help from the West |
| E | The defenders' numbers and morale |
| F | None: the defenders held this time |

Each step highlights its place on a schematic map of the historic peninsula,
the land walls, the Golden Horn and its chain, Galata, the Bosphorus with
Rumeli Hisarı and Anadolu Hisarı, and the Sea of Marmara. Two of the eight
steps are days when the defenders won, so the answer is not always an
Ottoman success. The summary at the end lists every step and counts how many
fell under each defense.

The map is schematic: shapes and distances are simplified so that every
place in the story fits in one small frame.

## How to Use

1. Read the step's date and event on the right, and find the highlighted
   place on the map.
2. Choose one of the six answers (or press a letter key, A to F). The answer
   locks: the correct answer turns green, a wrong choice red, and the reason
   appears.
3. Press **Next step** (or Enter, or the right arrow). **Previous** (or the
   left arrow) revisits an earlier step, but answers cannot be changed.
4. After step 8, press **See summary**. The table shows the correct answer
   for each step and your answer, and the line below counts the steps under
   each defense.
5. **Restart** clears all answers and returns to step 1.

## Lesson Plan

**Audience:** adult travelers preparing for the trip.  
**Duration:** 10–15 minutes.  
**Prerequisites:** the Bosphorus Strait, the Golden Horn, the Theodosian
Walls, moat, breach, Rumeli Hisarı, the chain across the Golden Horn,
Janissaries, Mehmed II, Constantine XI, and Giovanni Giustiniani Longo.  
**Learning objective (Bloom: Analyze):** Attribute each of eight turning
points of the 1453 siege to the defense of Constantinople it overcame, or
recognize that the defenders held, with at least 6 of 8 correct on the
first attempt.

### Activities

1. **List the defenses (2 min):** Before starting, name the city's
   advantages from the chapter: water on three sides, the Golden Horn
   harbor, the land walls, supply from the Black Sea, and allies in the West.
2. **Step through (6 min):** Work through all eight steps. Before choosing,
   say in one sentence what the event made harder for the defenders.
3. **Read the summary (3 min):** Count the steps under each defense. Which
   defenses were already gone before the final assault? Which two steps were
   defenders' victories, and why did neither save the city?
4. **Discuss (3 min):** A common story says the city fell because of the
   great cannon. Using the summary, write two sentences that explain why that
   story is incomplete.

### Assessment

Mastery is 6 of 8 correct on the first attempt. A follow-up question: *The
ships hauled over the hill on April 22 never attacked the sea walls. Why did
they still matter?* A strong answer says that they went around the chain, so
the defenders had to move men from the land walls to guard the harbor walls,
thinning a garrison of only about 7,000.
