# Who Built What We See in the Old Town?

[Run the Who Built What We See in the Old Town? MicroSim Fullscreen](./main.html){ .md-button .md-button--primary }

## About This MicroSim

Rhodes is a layered city, and its layers do not sort themselves by style.
A pointed Gothic arch may be the work of the knights in the 1480s, or of an
Italian architect in the 1920s. This MicroSim asks one question of twelve
buildings and features: **who built what we see here today?** Each card
goes into one of four layers, stacked from oldest at the bottom to newest
at the top.

| Period | Builders | Dates |
|---|---|---|
| Ancient | The Greek or Roman city | before about 400 CE |
| Knights | The Knights Hospitaller | 1310 to 1522 |
| Ottoman | Builders under Ottoman rule | 1523 to 1912 |
| Italian | Builders under Italian rule, new or rebuilt | 1912 to 1943 |

The cards come in a fixed shuffled order, the same for every learner, with
the four trap cards spread apart. Three traps are Italian buildings that
look medieval: the Palace of the Grand Master, rebuilt from 1937 to 1940
after the explosion of 1856; the Liberty Gate, opened in 1924; and the
Church of the Annunciation at Mandraki, a 1925 copy of the knights' lost
cathedral. The fourth is the Kahal Shalom Synagogue, completed in 1577 by
the Jewish community under Ottoman rule, which tests the idea that only
Muslims built under the Ottomans. Two cards, the temple remains and the
acropolis on Monte Smith, show that the ancient city survives even though
the Colossus does not.

After each answer the reason appears and the card settles into its correct
layer, outlined green if the first choice was right and red if it was not.
After twelve cards the stack holds Ancient 2, Knights 3, Ottoman 4, and
Italian 3.

## How to Use

1. Read the four periods on the left of the stack.
2. Read the card in the white panel and choose a period with the buttons
   below the stack, or press **1** to **4**.
3. Read the feedback. The correct layer is outlined in green; a wrong
   choice is outlined in red. Press **Next card** (or Enter).
4. After the twelfth card, press **See result** for your score. Hover over
   any card in the stack to review it.
5. **Try Again** repeats all twelve cards in a new order.

## Lesson Plan

**Audience:** adult travelers preparing for Day 8 on Rhodes.  
**Duration:** 10–15 minutes.  
**Prerequisites:** the Rhodes Medieval Town, the Street of the Knights,
the Palace of the Grand Master, the Hospital of the Knights, the Rhodes
city walls, Ottoman Rhodes, Italian rule of Rhodes, and the Kahal Shalom
Synagogue.  
**Learning objective (Bloom: Analyze):** Attribute twelve buildings and
features of the city of Rhodes to the period whose builders made what we
see today, with at least 10 of 12 correct on the first attempt, including
the Palace of the Grand Master and the Liberty Gate.

### Activities

1. **Predict (2 min):** Before starting, guess how many of the twelve
   cards will land in the Knights layer.
2. **Sort the cards (6 min):** Work through all twelve. For each, say
   aloud whether you are judging by style or by date before you choose.
3. **Review the traps (3 min):** Hover over the four trap cards in the
   finished stack. For each, name what makes it look older than it is.
4. **Walk it (on the day):** In the Old Town, find one feature from each
   layer and photograph it, with a plaque or guidebook date as evidence.

### Assessment

Mastery is 10 of 12 correct on the first attempt, with the Palace of the
Grand Master and the Liberty Gate both correct. Follow-up question: *A
guide calls the Palace of the Grand Master "a 14th-century palace." Is that
right?* Partly: the knights built it in the 14th century, but what stands
today is an Italian reconstruction of 1937 to 1940 on medieval foundations.
