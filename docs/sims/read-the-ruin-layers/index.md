# Read the Ruin: Which Layer?

<iframe src="main.html" height="584px" width="100%" scrolling="no" title="Place twelve Day 2 sights into five historical layers"></iframe>

[Run the Read the Ruin: Which Layer? MicroSim Fullscreen](./main.html){ .md-button .md-button--primary }

## About This MicroSim

Almost nothing we see on Day 2 stands as its first builders left it. This
MicroSim practices one rule for reading a city of layers:

> A thing belongs to the layer in which it **arrived at this place**, or, for
> a removal, the layer in which it was taken away.

The five layers are stacked like the ground itself, oldest at the bottom:

| Layer | Dates | What it was |
|---|---|---|
| Republic | 1923–today | The Turkish Republic |
| Ottoman | 1453–1922 | The sultans' capital |
| Latin occupation | 1204–1261 | Rule by the crusaders and Venice after the Fourth Crusade |
| Byzantine | 330–1453, except 1204–1261 | The Christian imperial capital |
| Roman | before 330 CE | The city before Constantine's capital |

Twelve cards name things we see in the Hippodrome, Hagia Sophia and the
Basilica Cistern. Three of them are **trap cards**: the Egyptian obelisk, the
Serpent Column and the Medusa heads were all made long before they reached
Constantinople, so their made date and their layer disagree. Other cards test
the idea that a removal is a layer (the bare stone of the Walled Obelisk), that
a European-looking monument can still be Ottoman (the German Fountain of
1900), and that not everything inside Hagia Sophia is Byzantine (the great
calligraphic roundels and the ablution fountain).

The cards come in a fixed shuffled order, the same for every learner, so a
group can compare answers card by card.

## How to Use

1. Read the rule in the yellow box and the five layers in the stack.
2. Read the card in the white panel and press the button for the layer in
   which it arrived (or press 1 to 5). Each card has one attempt.
3. The feedback shows when the thing was made and when it arrived or
   happened here, and the reason. The correct layer is outlined in green, a
   wrong choice in red, and the card settles into its correct layer.
4. Press **Next card** to continue. The number at the right of each layer
   counts its cards.
5. After the twelfth card, press **See result**. Hover over any card in the
   stack to review it; missed cards have a red border.
6. **Try Again** repeats the same twelve cards in a new order.

## Lesson Plan

**Audience:** adult travelers preparing for Day 2 in Istanbul.  
**Duration:** 10–15 minutes.  
**Prerequisites:** layers of a city, spolia, the four ways a layer is added
(built over, reused, converted, removed), and the Roman, Byzantine, Ottoman
and Republic eras from Chapter 1.  
**Learning objective (Bloom: Apply):** Apply the rule "a thing belongs to the
layer in which it arrived at this place" to assign twelve things seen on Day 2
to one of five historical layers, with at least 10 of 12 correct on the first
attempt, including at least 2 of the 3 trap cards.

### Activities

1. **Name the four ways (2 min):** Before starting, recall the four ways a
   layer gets added: built over, reused, converted and removed. For each card,
   say which way it was added before choosing its layer.
2. **Place the cards (6 min):** Work through all twelve. For every card, say
   aloud two dates: when it was made, and when it arrived here.
3. **Read the stack (3 min):** On the result screen, count the cards in each
   layer (Roman 1, Byzantine 5, Latin occupation 2, Ottoman 3, Republic 1).
   Find the three Byzantine cards that are much older than Byzantium.
4. **On site (Day 2):** Standing in Sultanahmet Square, place the Blue Mosque
   (1609–1616) in its layer, and say which of the four ways added it.

### Assessment

Mastery is 10 of 12 correct on the first attempt, with at least 2 of the 3
trap cards correct. A follow-up question: *On Day 7 at Ephesus we will see
statues of the four Virtues in niches on the Library of Celsus. The originals
were taken to Vienna in the early 1900s, and copies were set up when the
façade was re-erected in the 1970s. Which layers do the removal and the copies
belong to?* The removal belongs to the Ottoman layer, when the originals were
taken away, and the copies to the Republic layer, when they were set up.

## References

1. [Hippodrome of Constantinople](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hippodrome_of_Constantinople) — Wikipedia. The racetrack begun under Septimius Severus and its monuments: the Obelisk of Thutmose III, the Serpent Column and the Walled Obelisk.
2. [Obelisk of Theodosius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Obelisk_of_Theodosius) — Wikipedia. Carved for Thutmose III about 1450 BCE and raised in the Hippodrome in 390 CE.
3. [Serpent Column](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serpent_Column) — Wikipedia. Cast at Delphi after Plataea in 479 BCE and moved to Constantinople by Constantine.
4. [Walled Obelisk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walled_Obelisk) — Wikipedia. Restored by Constantine VII and stripped of its bronze plates by the Fourth Crusade.
5. [German Fountain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Fountain) — Wikipedia. The gift of Wilhelm II to Abdülhamid II, assembled in the Hippodrome in 1900.
6. [Hagia Sophia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hagia_Sophia) — Wikipedia. The dome rebuilt in 562, the Komnenos mosaic, the Fossati restoration of 1847–1849, the calligraphic roundels and the ablution fountain of Mahmud I.
7. [Enrico Dandolo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enrico_Dandolo) — Wikipedia. The Doge of Venice who led the Fourth Crusade and was buried in Hagia Sophia in 1205.
8. [Basilica Cistern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basilica_Cistern) — Wikipedia. Built under Justinian in 532, with Medusa heads reused as column bases, and restored from 2017 to 2022.
