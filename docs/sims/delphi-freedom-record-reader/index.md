## About This MicroSim

The polygonal wall below the Temple of Apollo carries hundreds of inscriptions,
and most of them record the same transaction: an enslaved person gaining
freedom through a "sale" to Pythian Apollo. More than a thousand such
manumission records survive at Delphi, most of them carved between about
200 BCE and 100 CE. They follow a standard form, and once we know the form we
can read any of them.

This MicroSim shows that form as eight clauses of one record. The record is a
**simplified composite**, not a translation of a single inscription, and the
bracketed words stand for the names a real record gives. For each clause the
learner chooses two answers: its **name** and its **effect**.

| # | Clause | Name | Effect |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | "In the year when [name] was archon, in the month [month]," | Date | Records the facts |
| 2 | "[owner's name], son of [father's name], sold to Pythian Apollo" | Sale to the god | Protects the freed person |
| 3 | "a woman named [name], [her homeland] by birth," | Person freed | Records the facts |
| 4 | "for a price of [sum] of silver, and he has received the whole price," | Price | Records the facts |
| 5 | "as [name] entrusted the purchase to the god, on condition that she be free and claimed by no one, doing what she wishes and going where she wishes." | Freedom clause | Protects the freed person |
| 6 | "Guarantor, according to the law of the city: [name]." | Guarantor | Protects the freed person |
| 7 | "[name] shall remain with [owner's name] for as long as he lives, doing what she is told." | Paramone | Limits the freed person's freedom |
| 8 | "Witnesses: the priests of Apollo, [name], [name]." | Witnesses | Protects the freed person |

The totals carry the lesson: four clauses protect her freedom, three record
the facts, and one keeps her bound. The same document guaranteed a freedom no
one could later deny and, in many cases, kept the freed person serving the old
household for years.

Clause 2 is the key to the whole form. The money was usually the enslaved
person's own savings, handed over through the god, but the buyer on the record
is Apollo. Since no human could claim a god's property, the sale itself made
re-enslavement impossible. The guarantor and the witnesses were not
formalities either: they were the living people who could defend the freedom
if anyone challenged it.

**Notes on the evidence.** The clause wording follows the standard Delphic
formula in simplified English. The Greek formula's key phrases are the sale to
"Pythian Apollo", the entrusting of the purchase to the god (*episteuse tōi
theōi tan ōnan*), the freedom clause "free and untouchable by anyone, doing
what she wishes and going where she wishes", the guarantor "according to the
law", and the *paramone* condition "let her remain with ... as long as he
lives, doing what she is told". Real records vary: the order of clauses
differs, some name two or more guarantors, many have no *paramone* clause at
all, and some add penalties or arrangements for buying out the *paramone*
early. Counts of the records differ between studies; "more than a thousand"
is the safe figure.

## How to Use

1. Read the whole record. Hover over (or tap) a dotted word in the glossary
   line or in the record to read its definition.
2. Clause 1 is selected. Choose its **name** from the eight name buttons and
   its **effect** from the three colored effect buttons. The choices appear
   as tags beside the clause, and the selection moves to the next unanswered
   clause.
3. Click any clause to change its answers. When all eight are answered, press
   **Check**. Only the first Check counts toward mastery.
4. Each clause is marked green or red. Click a red clause to read why: a name
   crossed out means the name is wrong; an effect crossed out means the name
   is right but the effect is wrong. Correct clauses lock.
5. Change the red clauses and press **Check** again as often as you like.
   After the second Check, **Show Answers** fills in any that remain.
   **Start Over** clears everything.

## Lesson Plan

**Audience:** adult travelers reading Chapter 18, "Day 12: Delphi", before or
after walking past the polygonal wall.  
**Duration:** 10–15 minutes.  
**Prerequisites:** manumission, manumission records, archon, guarantor,
witnesses, *paramone*, and the sale to the god (Chapter 18, "Manumission
Records"); inscription (Chapter 10); the polygonal wall (Chapter 18).  
**Learning objective (Bloom: Analyze):** Deconstruct a simplified Delphic
manumission record into its eight clauses, naming each clause and judging
whether it protects the freed person, limits the freed person's freedom, or
records the facts of the sale, with at least 7 of the 8 judgments correct.

### Activities

1. **Read first (2 min):** Before answering, read the record aloud and
   underline the single clause that sounds least like freedom.
2. **Take it apart (8 min):** Name every clause and judge its effect, then
   press Check once and note the first score.
3. **Discuss (3 min):** Why did the record need a god as the buyer? What could
   a former owner's heir do if the record had been a private promise instead?
4. **On site (2 min):** At the polygonal wall, use the low-light trick from
   the chapter to find a line of letters, and imagine it as one clause of a
   record like this one.

### Assessment

Mastery is at least 7 of 8 clauses correct on the first Check, where a clause
counts only when both its name and its effect are right. A follow-up question:
*How could one document both free a woman and keep her bound?* A strong answer
names the *paramone* clause as the limit, and the sale to the god, the freedom
clause, the guarantor, and the witnesses as the protections.

5. [Manumission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manumission) — Wikipedia. The legal act of freeing an enslaved person, across ancient and later societies.

7. William Linn Westermann, *The Slave Systems of Greek and Roman Antiquity* (American Philosophical Society, 1955). An early synthesis that drew heavily on the Delphic records for the forms and conditions of release.
