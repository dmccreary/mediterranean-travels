## About This MicroSim

The Ottoman Empire did not lose its lands in one collapse. It withdrew over
240 years, from the failed siege of Vienna in 1683 to the founding of the
Republic of Turkey in 1923, and it withdrew unevenly. This MicroSim divides
those years into four eras:

| Era | Years | Turning points |
|---|---|---|
| Era 1 | 1683–1829 | Siege of Vienna; Treaty of Karlowitz (1699); Crimea independent (1774) and annexed by Russia (1783) |
| Era 2 | 1830–1878 | Greek independence and the French conquest of Algiers (1830); Congress of Berlin (1878) |
| Era 3 | 1879–1913 | British occupation of Egypt (1882); Italo-Turkish War (1911–1912); Balkan Wars (1912–1913) |
| Era 4 | 1914–1923 | World War I, the Arab Revolt, and the treaties that followed |

Fourteen places are asked in a fixed order. Before we answer, we see only the
place and a short description with no date of loss. We commit to an era, and
only then does the sim show what happened, with dates, and color the place's
dot by its era. Istanbul and Ankara are shown as reference cities and are not
scored.

"Leaving Ottoman rule" here means the end of effective Ottoman government,
which was not always the end of the sultan's formal title. Two places show the
difference. Cyprus came under British administration in 1878 and Egypt under
British occupation in 1882, but both stayed nominally Ottoman until 1914; the
sim scores them by the earlier date and says so in its feedback. Crete is
scored by its autonomy in 1898, not its union with Greece in 1913, and Serbia's
three steps (1830, 1867, 1878) all fall in Era 2.

The sim also shows that a lost province did not always become a nation-state
at once. Crimea went to Russia, Algiers to France, Rhodes to Italy, and Cyprus,
Egypt, Baghdad and Jerusalem to Britain. The base map has relief and sea
depths but no modern borders, so it does not draw today's frontiers onto the
past.

## How to Use

1. Read the question below the map, for example *Buda: In which era did this
   place leave Ottoman rule?* The place's dot is highlighted in yellow, and a
   short description appears in gray italics.
2. Press **Era 1**, **Era 2**, **Era 3**, or **Era 4** (or press 1 to 4).
   Each place has one attempt. The correct era turns green and a wrong choice
   turns red.
3. Read the feedback: why the place left when it did, followed by what
   happened, with dates. The dot takes its era's color.
4. Press **Next place** (or Enter). The score in the top right counts correct
   first answers.
5. After place 14, press **See result**. Then replay the retreat with the
   **By 1829**, **By 1878**, **By 1913**, and **By 1923** buttons, or press
   **Replay next era**. At each step, colored dots have left Ottoman rule and
   white dots are still Ottoman. Click any dot to read about it.
6. **Try Again** clears every dot and the score. The mouse wheel scrolls the
   page, not the map; zoom with **+** and **−** and drag to pan.

## Lesson Plan

**Audience:** adult travelers preparing for the trip.  
**Duration:** 10–15 minutes.  
**Prerequisites:** Ottoman decline, the treaties of Karlowitz (1699) and
Küçük Kaynarca (1774), nationalism, the Congress of Berlin (1878), the Balkan
Wars, and World War I.  
**Learning objective (Bloom: Understand):** Classify fourteen places on a map
of the former Ottoman Empire by the era in which they left Ottoman rule, with
at least 10 of 14 correct on the first attempt.

### Activities

1. **Predict the shape (2 min):** Before starting, say which region you think
   the empire lost first and which it lost last: the Balkans, North Africa, or
   the Arab Middle East. Write the guess down.
2. **Classify (8 min):** Work through all fourteen places. For each, say aloud
   which power was pressing on it (Habsburg Austria, Russia, France, Britain,
   Italy, or a national movement) before choosing an era.
3. **Replay the eras (3 min):** Step from By 1829 to By 1923 and compare the
   picture with your guess. Notice that the Balkan and Black Sea lands went
   before 1914, while Mecca, Baghdad, Jerusalem and Damascus stayed Ottoman
   until World War I.
4. **Who got it? (2 min):** On the By 1913 map, name the place that became
   independent and the places that passed to another empire. Only Athens and
   Belgrade became capitals of new states before 1914.

### Assessment

Mastery is 10 of 14 correct on the first attempt. As a follow-up, ask: *We
visit Rhodes on Day 8 and Athens on Day 10. Which left Ottoman rule first, and
which became Greek first?* A strong answer says Athens left first (Greece
independent in 1830) and became Greek first; Rhodes left in 1912 but went to
Italy and became Greek only in 1947.
