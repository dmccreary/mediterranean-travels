# Paul's Journeys and Our Route

## About This MicroSim

The Book of Acts tells one continuous story of Paul's travels. Study Bibles
divide it into three numbered missionary journeys and a final voyage to Rome as
a prisoner. The numbering is a modern convenience, and the dates are scholars'
reconstructions that differ by a year or two.

| Journey | About | Told in |
|---|---|---|
| First | 46–48 CE | Acts 13–14 |
| Second | 49–52 CE | Acts 15:36–18:22 |
| Third | 52–57 CE | Acts 18:23–21:17 |
| Voyage to Rome | 59–60 CE | Acts 27:1–28:16 |

This MicroSim asks one question about twelve places, in a fixed order: **on
which journey does Acts first bring Paul there?** The answer is the first
visit, not the longest stay. Ephesus is the classic trap: Paul spent two to
three years there on the third journey, but Acts first brings him there
briefly at the end of the second.

Before we answer, the map shows only the twelve places, our own route from
Istanbul to Athens and Delphi, and three reference markers: Tarsus (Paul's
birthplace, according to Acts), Damascus (his call), and Jerusalem (his
arrest). The journey routes stay hidden, so each answer is a prediction. After
the twelfth answer, all four routes appear and can be switched on and off one
at a time. Each line joins the places Acts names, in Acts' order. The lines
are not the actual roads or sea lanes, and where Acts names only a region
("Syria and Cilicia," "the region of Galatia and Phrygia"), the line passes
through that region's main city.

The reveal also shows what Paul and our trip share: Athens, Rhodes, and the
region of Ephesus, where our ship docks at Kuşadası. Acts never brings Paul to
Byzantium (Istanbul), Heraklion, or Delphi, and his only time on Crete was as
a prisoner, sheltering at Fair Havens on the south coast before the storm that
wrecked his ship on Malta.

## How to Use

1. Read the question below the map. The current place is the yellow dot.
2. Press **First**, **Second**, **Third**, or **Voyage to Rome** (or keys
   1 to 4). Each place has one attempt. The correct journey turns green and a
   wrong choice turns red.
3. Read the feedback, which cites the passage in Acts. The place's dot takes
   the color of its correct journey; click it to read the feedback again.
4. Press **Next place** (or Enter). After place 12, press **Reveal the
   journeys**.
5. The score shows: "You placed *n* of 12. Mastery is 10." Use the four
   journey buttons to show or hide each route, and **All** (key 5) to show or
   hide them together. Click a route to list its stops, or a colored dot to see
   every journey that passes through it, with the first visit marked.
6. The **Our route** checkbox shows or hides our cruise and land route.
   **Try again** clears all answers and hides the routes. The mouse wheel
   scrolls the page, not the map; zoom with **+** and **−** and drag to pan.

## Lesson Plan

**Audience:** adult travelers preparing for the visit to Ephesus.  
**Duration:** 10–15 minutes.  
**Prerequisites:** Paul's missionary journeys, the Book of Acts as the source
for them, Roman roads and the sailing season, the Roman province of Asia, and
our cruise route (Chapter 2).  
**Learning objective (Bloom: Understand):** Classify twelve places by the
journey on which the Book of Acts first brings Paul there (first, second, or
third journey, or the voyage to Rome), correctly placing at least 10 of 12.

### Activities

1. **Predict (2 min):** Before starting, guess which journey reached the most
   of the twelve places, and whether Paul ever came to Crete as a missionary.
2. **Classify (6 min):** Work through the twelve places. For each one, say
   aloud the scene Acts tells there, if you know one (a vision, a convert, a
   stoning), before choosing.
3. **Compare the routes (4 min):** After the reveal, show one journey at a
   time. Notice that the second journey reached the most places and first
   crossed into Europe, that Ephesus lies on two journeys, and that Paul
   reached Crete only as a prisoner.
4. **Connect to our trip (2 min):** With **Our route** on, name the three
   places where our path and Paul's meet, and the stops on our trip that Acts
   never mentions.

### Assessment

Mastery is 10 of 12 correct on the first attempt. As a follow-up, ask:
*Tomorrow's guide in Ephesus says, "Paul's first visit to Ephesus lasted three
years." What is wrong with that sentence?* A strong answer says Acts first
brings Paul to Ephesus briefly at the end of the second journey; the stay of
two to three years came on the third.
