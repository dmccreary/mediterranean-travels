## Description

This map follows the Viking Star's seven-night *Ancient Mediterranean
Treasures* voyage from Istanbul to Athens, November 14–21, 2026: the middle part
of our November 11–23 trip. The cruise calls at six ports in Turkey and Greece:

1. **Istanbul**, Turkey: Nov 14–15, overnight in port (trip days 4–5)
2. **Çanakkale**, Turkey, the port for Troy and Gallipoli: Nov 16 (trip day 6)
3. **Kusadasi**, Turkey, the port for Ephesus: Nov 17 (trip day 7)
4. **Rhodes**, Greece: Nov 18 (trip day 8)
5. **Heraklion**, Crete, Greece, the port for Knossos: Nov 19 (trip day 9)
6. **Athens (Piraeus)**, Greece: Nov 20, disembarking Nov 21 (trip days 10–11)

The map labels each stop by **cruise day** (Istanbul is cruise days 1–2). The
daily chapters use **trip days**, which start with our own three days in
Istanbul on November 11.

### How to Use It

- **Step through the ports** with the **Next →** and **← Previous** buttons,
  the **←** and **→** arrow keys, the numbered dots, or by clicking a numbered
  pin on the map. The map flies to each port as you select it.
- **Read the port card** on the right. It shows the date, arrival and departure
  times, a short introduction, the key things to see, and a practical tip.
- **Follow the route.** Legs already sailed are solid navy, the leg that brought
  you to the current port is gold, and legs still ahead are dashed blue.
- **Click Whole route** to zoom back out to the full itinerary.
- **Switch base maps** with the layer control in the top-right corner of the map:
  street map, satellite imagery, or OpenStreetMap.

The sea route is approximate. It shows the general track through the
Dardanelles and around the islands, not the ship's exact navigation.

## Lesson Plan

This map is for us as travelers, not for a classroom. Here is how to get the
most from it before and during the cruise.

### What We Will Get From It

- A clear picture of where each port is and which waters the ship sails through
  to reach it.
- The one sight at each port that matters most, and the civilization that built
  it.
- A realistic plan for each shore day that fits the port's arrival and departure
  times.

### Before We Leave

1. **Route tour (5 minutes).** Step through all six ports in order. At each
   one, notice the water the ship crosses to get there: the Sea of Marmara, the
   Dardanelles, the Aegean Sea, and the Sea of Crete.
2. **Choose at Çanakkale.** Troy and Gallipoli lie in opposite directions, so
   decide in advance which story we most want to stand inside: the Bronze Age
   city of Homer's *Iliad*, or the 1915 battlefields that shaped modern Turkey,
   Australia, and New Zealand.
3. **Book early.** Check which included excursion Viking offers at each port.
   The Ephesus and Knossos tours fill up first.

### Each Evening Aboard

1. **Look ahead.** Open the next port, read its card, then read that day's
   chapter.
2. **Plan the day.** Using the arrival and departure times and the tip, sketch
   a schedule that gets us back to the ship at least 30 minutes before it sails.
3. **Look closer.** Switch to the satellite map and zoom in on tomorrow's port.
   At Rhodes, find the walls of the medieval town; at Heraklion, find the
   Venetian fortress guarding the old harbor.

### Looking Back

- On the flight home, sketch the route from memory and label all six ports.
- Put the main sight at each port in order of age, from Knossos (about 1900 BC)
  to Topkapi Palace (15th century AD), and note which empire built each one.
