# The Parthenon's Five Lives

## About This MicroSim

The Parthenon did not go straight from temple to ruin. For about 2,100 years
after it was finished it was in use, first as Athena's temple, then as a
church, then as a mosque, and it became a ruin only in 1687. This MicroSim
asks us to sort twelve events into the building's five lives:

| Life | From | To |
|---|---|---|
| Temple of Athena | 447 BCE | End of the 4th century CE, when the old sacrifices were banned (the closing date is debated) |
| Church (Orthodox, then Latin Catholic from 1204) | Probably by the late 6th century CE (debated) | 1458 |
| Mosque | 1458 (the conversion followed soon after; the exact year is not known) | 1687 |
| Ruin, with a small mosque inside | 1687 | 1833 |
| Protected monument, with its sculptures in museums | 1833 | Today |

Each card shows a date and an event, such as *1018 CE: the emperor Basil II
gives thanks for his victories in the building*. We choose a life before any
dates for the lives appear. Feedback gives the reason, and the card drops onto
a timeline at its date. After card 12, the five lives appear as colored bands
behind the cards. The years between the temple's closing and the church's
opening are drawn as a striped band, because scholars disagree about when the
conversion happened.

Two cards are traps, and mastery requires both:

- **Card 7, September 26, 1687.** The Venetian shell that ignited the
  gunpowder stored inside ended the mosque's life, but it hit a working
  mosque, not a ruin.
- **Card 9, 1801–1812.** Lord Elgin's agents worked on a roofless ruin with a
  small mosque inside, not on a complete building.

**Dates.** Card 8, Morosini's attempt to lower the horses of Athena's chariot
from the west pediment, is dated early 1688 here. The specification gave
1687; most accounts place the attempt in the months after the explosion,
before the Venetians left Athens in April 1688. Either way, the building was
already a ruin. The 1674 drawings are traditionally credited to Jacques
Carrey, though the attribution has been questioned.

## How to Use

1. Read the card: its number, date, and event.
2. Choose one of the five lives (or press keys 1 to 5). Only the first answer
   counts toward the score.
3. Read the feedback. **Correct.** gives the reason; otherwise the feedback
   says which life the building was living and why. The card appears on the
   timeline; a red dashed border marks a card you missed.
4. Press **Next card** (or Enter). After card 12, press **Show the five lives**.
5. Use **Full span** and **Close-up: 1400–2030 CE** to change the view, and
   drag the timeline to move it. Hover over or click a card to read it again.
6. **Try Again** clears all twelve cards.

## Lesson Plan

**Audience:** adult travelers preparing to visit the Acropolis.  
**Duration:** 10–15 minutes.  
**Prerequisites:** the Parthenon, the Parthenon sculptures, the Parthenon as
church and mosque, Lord Elgin, the Acropolis Museum, and restoration (Chapters
4, 7 and 15).  
**Learning objective (Bloom: Understand):** Classify twelve events in the
Parthenon's history by which of the building's five lives it was living when
each event happened, classifying at least 10 of 12 correctly on the first
attempt.

### Activities

1. **Name the lives (2 min):** Before card 1, read the five choices aloud and
   guess roughly when each began.
2. **Classify (8 min):** For each card, ask what the building was being used
   for on that date: was there a roof, a priest, an imam, a garrison, or a
   restoration crew?
3. **Read the bands (3 min):** After card 12, compare the widths of the bands.
   The temple and church lives each lasted about a thousand years; the ruin
   began only in 1687.

### Assessment

Mastery is 10 of 12 correct on the first attempt, including cards 7 and 9. As
a follow-up, ask: *Was the Parthenon a ruin when Lord Elgin's agents took its
sculptures, and what made it one?* A strong answer says yes: it had been a
roofless ruin since a Venetian shell ignited the Ottoman gunpowder stored
inside on September 26, 1687, while it was still in use as a mosque.
