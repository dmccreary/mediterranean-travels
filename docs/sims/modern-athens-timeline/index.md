## About This MicroSim

Most of what a visitor notices in Athens is not explained by antiquity. A bent
iron gate on Patission Street, the name on a coffee menu, the metro to the
airport, the empty shops and the new bakeries were all shaped by events of the
last sixty years. This MicroSim puts fourteen of those events on one timeline,
from the colonels' coup of 1967 to the Athens Declaration of 2023, and asks us
to work backward from eight typical sights to the event that left each mark.

| Sight | Event that explains it |
|---|---|
| Flowers at a twisted iron gate on Patission Street, and a march every November 17 | Polytechnic uprising, November 14–17, 1973 |
| "Greek coffee" on a menu where older Greeks remember "Turkish coffee" | Cyprus and the fall of the junta, July 1974 |
| Euro prices in a Plaka souvenir shop | Euro notes and coins, January 1, 2002 |
| A metro line to the airport and a tram to the sea | Athens Olympics, August 2004 |
| Memories of queuing for a daily €60 at a cash machine | Banks close, June–July 2015 |
| Empty shops, and a son who now works in Germany | First rescue loan, 2010 (the October 2009 deficit revelation is also accepted) |
| A Syrian bakery and an Afghan grocery | Sea crossings of 2015 |
| Greek and Turkish leaders promising regular meetings | Athens Declaration, December 2023 |

Five events are context and explain none of the sights: the 1967 coup, joining
the European Economic Community in 1981, the 1999 earthquakes, the EU–Turkey
deal of March 2016, and the end of the rescues in August 2018. The sights are
composites of things visitors commonly see, not specific places.

When all eight are matched, six of them point to events between 2002 and 2023.
Much of the city a visitor walks through was shaped in the last two decades.

**Before this timeline begins.** The timeline starts in 1967, but the city it
explains was made earlier. Athens became the capital of the new Greek kingdom in
1834, moving from Nafplio under the young Bavarian king Otto, and was replanned
as a neoclassical capital around the small Ottoman town. It hosted the first
modern Olympic Games in 1896, in the rebuilt Panathenaic Stadium. After the
population exchange of 1923, which followed the Greek defeat in Asia Minor,
about 1.2 million Orthodox refugees came to Greece, and Athens and Piraeus grew
whole new districts, such as Nea Smyrni and Nea Ionia, named for the places they
had lost. German forces occupied Athens from April 1941 to October 1944, and the
famine of the first occupation winter killed tens of thousands in the capital.
These events are told in Chapters 10 and 15.

## How to Use

1. Read the sight in bold and the question "Which event explains this?"
2. Click an event on the timeline, or choose it from the list. Its date and
   description appear below the list. Selecting events to read them is
   exploration; nothing is scored until you commit.
3. Press **Commit** (or Enter). A correct first choice adds to the score
   "n of 8 correct on the first attempt". A wrong first choice turns red and
   gives a hint; you get one more try. After a second miss the answer is
   revealed and the sight counts as missed.
4. The matched event gets a gold badge with the sight's number (S1 to S8).
   Press **Next sight** to go on.
5. **Close-up: 2008–2019** spreads out the crowded crisis years; **Full span**
   returns to 1967–2023. Drag the timeline sideways to pan. **Restart** clears
   every match.

## Lesson Plan

**Audience:** adult travelers reading Chapter 20, "Day 13: Athens, Museums,
Tutors, and the City Today", on the last evening in Athens.  
**Duration:** 10–15 minutes.  
**Prerequisites:** the military dictatorship (junta), the Polytechnic uprising,
the debt crisis, rescue loans and austerity, the 2015 sea crossings, the euro,
the 2004 Olympics, Cyprus 1974, the 1999 earthquakes, the Athens Declaration,
and the renaming of Greek coffee (Chapter 20, Parts Three, Four and Five).  
**Learning objective (Bloom: Understand):** Infer which event in modern Greek
history, from 1967 to 2023, explains each of eight things a visitor can see in
Athens today, matching at least 7 of the 8 correctly on the first attempt.

### Activities

1. **Predict (2 min):** Before starting, guess how many of the eight sights
   will point to events after the year 2000.
2. **Match (8 min):** Work through the eight sights in order, committing one
   event for each. Note the first-attempt score.
3. **Discuss (3 min):** Which sight surprised you most? Why might a visitor who
   knows only ancient history misread the empty shops, or the coffee menu?

### Assessment

Mastery is at least 7 of 8 sights matched on the first attempt. A follow-up
question: *Name one thing you saw in Athens that none of these fourteen events
explains, and find the event that does.* A strong answer names a specific sight
and a dated event, for example the refugee neighborhoods of Nea Smyrni and the
population exchange of 1923.
