## About This MicroSim

Writing systems differ most in what one basic sign stands for. This MicroSim
gives twelve cards, each describing a script in two pieces of evidence: what
one sign stands for, and how many signs the script uses. Our job is to sort
each one into the right kind:

| Kind | What one sign stands for | Typical sign count |
|---|---|---|
| Logographic | A word or part of a word | Hundreds or more |
| Syllabary | A syllable | About 40 to 100 |
| Abjad | A consonant; most vowels are left to the reader | Under 40 |
| Alphabet | A vowel or a consonant | Under 40 |

Three cards target the most common confusions. Egyptian hieroglyphs include
some signs for single sounds, but a few sound-signs do not make an alphabet.
Hebrew and Arabic are often called alphabets in everyday speech, but they
leave most vowels unwritten, which makes them abjads. And Linear A, which no
one can read, can still be classified: its sign count and its shared signs
with Linear B mark it as a syllabary.

After the twelfth card, every card shows its sign count, sorted from most to
fewest in each bin, so we can see the counts fall into the rule-of-thumb bands
from the chapter's tip.

## How to Use

1. Read the card in the white panel.
2. Press the button under the bin where you think it belongs (or press 1 to
   4). Each card has one attempt.
3. The correct bin is outlined in green, a wrong choice in red, and the card
   settles into its correct bin. The feedback explains why.
4. Press **Next card** to continue. The score in the top right counts correct
   first attempts.
5. After the twelfth card, press **See result**. Each card now shows its sign
   count. Hover over any card to read it again; missed cards have a red border.
6. **Restart** reshuffles the cards and clears the score.

## Lesson Plan

**Audience:** adult travelers preparing for the trip.  
**Duration:** 10–15 minutes.  
**Prerequisites:** writing system, logographic script, syllabary, abjad,
alphabet, the sign-count rule of thumb, and Linear A and Linear B from Part 1.  
**Learning objective (Bloom: Understand):** Classify twelve writing systems as
logographic, syllabary, abjad, or alphabet from what one basic sign stands for
and how many signs the script uses, with at least 10 of 12 correct on the
first attempt.

### Activities

1. **Warm up (2 min):** Say the four kinds aloud with one example of each from
   the chapter before starting.
2. **Sort (6 min):** Work through all twelve cards. For each, find the two
   pieces of evidence on the card before choosing: what one sign stands for,
   and how many signs there are.
3. **Read the counts (3 min):** On the result screen, check that every count
   falls in its bin's band. Which script sits closest to a band's edge?
4. **Extend (3 min):** Turkey switched from the Arabic script to a Latin
   alphabet in 1928. Using the Arabic and Modern Turkish cards, explain in one
   sentence why the switch made Turkish easier to read aloud.

### Assessment

Mastery is 10 of 12 correct on the first attempt. A follow-up question:
*Japanese katakana has 46 basic signs, each a vowel, a consonant plus a vowel,
or a final n. Which kind of script is it?* A syllabary, like hiragana.
