## About This MicroSim

Ephesus and nearby Selçuk hold some of the most visited Christian sites in
Turkey: the Church of Mary, where a council met in 431 CE; the Basilica of
St. John on the hill of Ayasuluk; the House of the Virgin Mary on Bülbül
Dağı; and a hilltop tower called St. Paul's Prison. Guides tell all of their
stories in the same confident voice. This MicroSim asks us to separate them
by the strength of the evidence, using three ratings:

| Rating | Definition |
|---|---|
| **Documented** | Contemporary or near-contemporary written or archaeological evidence supports it. |
| **Debated** | Some early evidence supports it, but scholars disagree about whether it is reliable. |
| **Tradition only** | First recorded long after the events, with no early evidence for or against. |

Ten claims appear one at a time, in a fixed order. For each one we choose a
rating, then one of two reasons, shown in random order. A card counts only
when both the rating and the reason are right, because a correct label
reached for the wrong reason is a lucky guess, not a judgment. Five claims
are documented, two debated, and three tradition only.

The sim targets four common mistakes:

- **A real ancient building proves the story attached to it.** Claims 3 and 4
  are about the same tower. The tower is securely dated to the city wall of
  Lysimachus, early 3rd century BCE; the name "Paul's Prison" is a later
  local tradition, since Acts mentions no imprisonment in Ephesus.
- **"Tradition" means "false."** It does not. It means the evidence cannot
  settle the question either way (claim 9).
- **Papal visits prove an identification.** Three popes have visited the
  House of the Virgin Mary, but the Catholic Church has not ruled on whether
  Mary lived there (claim 8).
- **Everything in Acts is either proven or invented.** The riot of the
  silversmiths is told only in Acts, a generation later, but its local
  details fit the city: plausible, not proven (claims 6 and 7).

One detail is worth knowing for claim 1: the council's acts name the church
"called Mary" in which it met. Identifying that church with the ruined
church near the harbor is the standard view, though archaeologists have
debated exactly when the older Roman hall was converted into a church.

## How to Use

1. Read the three rating definitions under the colored headings.
2. Read the claim in the white panel and ask: *how strong is the evidence?*
3. Choose a rating with the buttons below the columns, by clicking a column,
   or with keys 1 to 3.
4. Two reasons appear inside the panel. Choose the better one by clicking it
   or pressing A or B.
5. Read the feedback. The correct reason is outlined in green, and a wrong
   choice in red. The card joins the column of its correct rating, marked ✓,
   ✗, or "reason ✗" when the rating was right but the reason was not.
6. Press **Next card** (or Enter). After claim 10, press **See result**:
   *You judged n of 10. Mastery is 8.* Hover over any card to review it.
   **Judge again** restarts from claim 1.

## Lesson Plan

**Audience:** adult travelers preparing for Ephesus, the Basilica of
St. John, and the House of the Virgin Mary.  
**Duration:** 10–15 minutes.  
**Prerequisites:** primary sources and myth versus history (Chapter 4); the
Book of Acts and Paul in Ephesus (Chapter 9); the Church of Mary, the Council
of Ephesus, John the Apostle, the Basilica of St. John, the House of the
Virgin Mary, St. Paul's Prison Tower, and the riot of the silversmiths
(Chapter 10).  
**Learning objective (Bloom: Evaluate):** Judge the strength of the evidence
for ten claims about the Christian sites of Ephesus and Selçuk, rating each
as documented, debated, or tradition only, with at least 8 of 10 correct and
the reason chosen for each.

### Activities

1. **Warm up (2 min):** Before starting, name the oldest source you can think
   of for any one of the sites. How many years after the event was it
   written?
2. **Judge (8 min):** Rate the ten claims. Before choosing a reason, say
   aloud who first made the claim and when.
3. **Compare cards 3 and 4 (2 min):** Why does one tower get two different
   ratings?
4. **Plan the visit (3 min):** Choose one site we will visit. Write one
   sentence a guide could say there that is true by the historian's test and
   respectful of the pilgrims around us.

### Assessment

Mastery is 8 of 10 cards with both rating and reason correct, on the first
attempt. As a follow-up, ask: *A guide at the Basilica of St. John says,
"This is the tomb of John the Apostle, who wrote his Gospel here." Rate each
part of the sentence and give the evidence.* A strong answer says the
basilica is documented (Procopius), the tomb is tradition first written down
about 190 CE, and John's life and writing at Ephesus are debated, resting on
Irenaeus about 180 CE.

   publishing his Gospel at Ephesus, and Polycrates' letter (about 190 CE,

6. [Lysimachus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lysimachus) — Wikipedia. The

   Turkish City* (Cambridge University Press, 1979). The Christian city, its
   churches, and its pilgrim sites.
