# Source, Myth, or Contested?

## About This MicroSim

Every guide in Hagia Sophia tells stories, and they are not all the same kind
of story. Some come from people who saw the building new. Some first appear
centuries later. Some rest on dates nobody disputes, and some are arguments
about what those dates should mean today. This MicroSim gives ten statements
we are likely to hear on Day 2 and asks which of four kinds each one is:

| Kind | Definition used in the sim |
|---|---|
| Primary source | Evidence written or made at the time, by someone who was there or close to it. |
| Myth | A story first recorded long after the event, with no support from the time. |
| Contested history | The facts are agreed, but their meaning or proper use is disputed today. |
| Settled history | A fact agreed by the sources and by modern historians. |

The statements are built to catch four common mistakes. A primary source is
not the same as a true source: Procopius and Niketas Choniates were both close
to what they described, and both wrote with a purpose. A myth is not
worthless: the Solomon line tells us how proud later Byzantines were of their
church. Contested history is not unknown history: the dates of Hagia Sophia's
conversions are agreed, and the argument is about values. And a story told by
every tour guide is not history just because it is told often.

## How to Use

1. Read the four definitions across the top.
2. Read the statement in the white panel and press the button for its kind
   (or press 1 to 4). Each statement is scored once and cannot be changed.
3. The correct kind is outlined in green, a wrong choice in red, and the
   reason appears below the statement.
4. Press **Next statement** to continue. The numbered tiles show your
   answers so far.
5. After the tenth statement, press **See result**. The panel shows your score
   and lists the statements you missed. Click any numbered tile to review that
   statement and its reason.
6. **Try Again** reshuffles the same ten statements.

## Lesson Plan

**Audience:** adult travelers preparing for Day 2 in Istanbul.  
**Duration:** 10–15 minutes.  
**Prerequisites:** primary source, secondary source, myth, history and
contested history, from Part One of the chapter, "Whose Story Is This?"  
**Learning objective (Bloom: Understand):** Classify ten statements about
Byzantine Constantinople as a primary source, a myth, contested history or
settled history, with at least 8 of 10 correct on the first attempt.

### Activities

1. **Three questions (2 min):** Before starting, say aloud the question each
   kind of statement answers: *who saw it, and why did they write?*; *when did
   this story first appear?*; and *who is this past important to now?*
2. **Classify (6 min):** Work through all ten statements. Before choosing, ask
   who is speaking and when. A named author from the time points to a primary
   source; "should" or "belongs" points to contested history.
3. **Review (3 min):** On the result screen, click the tiles for the two
   eyewitness statements, Procopius on the dome and Choniates on the sack.
   Each is a primary source even though each is one-sided. Say what purpose
   each author had.
4. **On site (any time on Day 2):** When a guide tells a story, ask which of
   the four kinds it is, and what we would need to know to be sure.

### Assessment

Mastery is 8 of 10 correct on the first attempt. A follow-up question: *A
guide says the "weeping column" in Hagia Sophia grants wishes to anyone who
turns a thumb in its hole. Which kind of statement is this, and how would we
test it?* It is a myth: we would look for the earliest source of the story and
ask how long after the building it was written.
