## About This MicroSim

At the Council of Jerusalem, the leaders of the early Jesus movement decided
what Gentile believers had to keep and what they did not: faith was the
purpose, and circumcision and the whole law were the inherited form of
belonging that could not be imposed on newcomers. Chapter 11 argues that
every redesign of a school turns on the same distinction:

- **Essential:** part of what a school is for.
- **Inherited:** one way of doing it that answered the constraints of an
  earlier age.

This MicroSim gives twelve practices, one at a time, and asks for a judgment
on each. Every card carries the chapter's test: *What problem did this solve,
and does that problem still exist in the same form?* Six practices are
essential and six inherited. Four of the inherited forms come straight from
the chapter: the Carnegie unit, which the Carnegie Foundation for the
Advancement of Teaching defined in 1906 as about 120 hours of class in one
subject; grading by age, which spread with schools such as Boston's Quincy
Grammar School in 1848; the 50-minute period; and explaining new material to
a whole room at one pace.

"Inherited" does not mean worthless. Each inherited form solved a real
problem and may still be useful, and each one on this list serves an
essential purpose on the same list, which is exactly why it is hard to
change. The hardest cards are a **trap pair**: card 5, *someone checks that a
student has really learned a skill before building on it*, is essential;
card 11, *every student takes the same unit test on the same day*, is one
inherited way of doing it. After the last card the two are linked by a line
labeled "purpose and form."

The judgments follow this chapter's criterion. Reasonable people may weigh
some forms differently; rows of desks, for instance, still suit a lecture.
The point is to name the purpose before defending or discarding a form.

## How to Use

1. Read the criterion at the top: what *essential* and *inherited* mean.
2. Read the practice in the white panel and ask the chapter's test.
3. Judge it: press **Essential** or **Inherited**, click a column, or press
   **E** or **I**. Only the first choice counts.
4. Read the feedback. The card joins the column of its correct judgment,
   marked ✓ or ✗. Press **Next card** (or Enter).
5. After card 12, read the score and look for the line linking cards 5 and
   11. Hover over any card to review it; inherited cards also show the
   purpose they serve. **Try again** repeats all twelve.

## Lesson Plan

**Audience:** adult travelers, especially teachers and school leaders, who
have read Part Five of Chapter 11.  
**Duration:** 10–15 minutes.  
**Prerequisites:** essential versus inherited, the Council of Jerusalem, the
grammar of schooling, seat time and the Carnegie unit, direct instruction,
and the AI-native school (all in Chapter 11).  
**Learning objective (Bloom: Evaluate):** Judge whether each of twelve
school practices is an essential purpose of schooling or an inherited form
that served that purpose under earlier constraints, judging at least 10 of
12 correctly, including both items of the trap pair.

### Activities

1. **Warm-up (2 min):** Name one practice from your own schooling that felt
   permanent. Was it a purpose or a form?
2. **Judge (6 min):** Work through the twelve cards. For each, say aloud what
   problem the practice solved when it began.
3. **Discuss the trap pair (4 min):** Why is checking mastery essential while
   the shared test date is inherited? What other ways could a school check
   that a student has learned?
4. **Map the dependencies (3 min):** Hover over each inherited card and read
   the purpose it serves. Pick one and describe a different form that could
   serve the same purpose, as the AI-native morning does for explanation.

### Assessment

Mastery is 10 of 12 correct on the first attempt, with cards 5 and 11 both
correct. As a follow-up, ask: *A colleague says, "If we drop the unit test,
we stop checking learning." What would you answer?* A strong answer
separates the purpose (checking that each student has learned) from the form
(one test for everyone on one day) and names another form that keeps the
purpose.
