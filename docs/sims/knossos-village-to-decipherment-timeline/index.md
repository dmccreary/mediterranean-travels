## About This MicroSim

The story of Knossos runs for 9,000 years, but almost all of its famous
events crowd into two short stretches: the palace age of about 1900 to 1200
BCE, and the rediscovery that began in 1878. Between them lies a silence of
more than 3,000 years in which no one could read the palace's records. This
timeline is drawn to scale from 7000 BCE to 2030 CE, in two bands, **Bronze
Age Crete** and **Rediscovery**, so those gaps show as distances.

The timeline starts almost empty: only the first village on the Kephala
hill, about 7000 BCE. Four prediction questions, each answered once before
its events appear, fill it in.

| # | Prediction | Events added |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Did the Thera eruption come before or after most palaces were destroyed? | Thera erupts (c. 1600 BCE); Mycenaeans at Knossos (c. 1450 BCE) |
| 2 | About how many years separate the two? | First palace, second palace, Phaistos Disc, Snake Goddess figurines |
| 3 | Did Knossos burn before or after the mainland palaces such as Pylos? | Knossos burns (c. 1375 BCE); mainland palaces fall (c. 1200 BCE) |
| 4 | How many years passed between the first tablets and their reading? | Kalokairinos (1878) through the UNESCO listing (2025) |

After each answer the timeline zooms to the events just added and labels
the gap the question asked about: about 150 years from Thera to the fall of
the palaces, about 175 years between the fires at Knossos and at Pylos, and
52 years from Evans's first tablets in 1900 to Ventris's reading in 1952.
In the full view, each band's events collapse into one bar that opens with a
click, and after the last question a striped bar marks the more than 3,000
years in which Linear B went unread.

Bronze Age dates are approximate and debated. The sim uses the chapter's
dates: the Thera eruption about 1600 BCE (radiocarbon dating; an older
archaeological dating puts it near 1500 BCE), and the final destruction of
the palace of Knossos about 1375 BCE, which some scholars place later.

## How to Use

1. Read the first question below the timeline and choose an answer. Each
   question counts once.
2. Watch the timeline zoom to the new events, which are outlined in gold,
   and read the reason in the feedback.
3. Click any event to read its description in the right-hand panel.
4. Press **Next question** for the next prediction.
5. Use **Full span**, **Close-up: Bronze Age**, **Close-up: Rediscovery**,
   and the **Earlier** and **Later** buttons to move around. Dragging the
   timeline also pans it; the mouse wheel scrolls the page, not the
   timeline.
6. **Start over** hides the events again and resets the score.

## Lesson Plan

**Audience:** adult travelers preparing for Day 9 at Knossos and the
Heraklion Archaeological Museum.  
**Duration:** 10–15 minutes.  
**Prerequisites:** the Knossos Palace, Arthur Evans, and the Evans
reconstructions; the palace economy and the Thera eruption; the Snake
Goddess figurines, the Phaistos Disc, Linear A, Linear B, and palace
scribes; the Bronze Age collapse (Chapter 8).  
**Learning objective (Bloom: Understand):** Interpret a timeline of Knossos
from its first village to the decipherment of Linear B, answering at least
3 of 4 prediction questions about the order and spacing of events
correctly before each answer is revealed.

### Activities

1. **Sketch first (2 min):** On paper, draw a line from 7000 BCE to today
   and mark where you think the palace age and Evans's dig fall.
2. **Predict (6 min):** Answer the four questions in order. Before each
   answer, say aloud why you chose it.
3. **Compare (3 min):** Press **Full span**. How does the real spacing
   compare with your sketch? Most people draw the palace age far too long
   and the village age far too short.
4. **Explain (3 min):** Using the timeline, explain to a partner why the
   Thera eruption could not have destroyed the palaces all at once, and
   why Knossos and Pylos left baked tablets nearly two centuries apart.

### Assessment

Mastery is 3 of 4 predictions correct on the first answer. Follow-up
question: *A guidebook says "the eruption of Thera wiped out the
Minoans." What does the timeline say?* The eruption, about 1600 BCE, came
about 150 years before most palaces were destroyed, about 1450 BCE, so it
weakened Minoan Crete but did not end it.
