## About This MicroSim

A Turkish meal can be read like an archaeological site, in layers. Each
ingredient arrived at a particular time, brought by particular people. This
MicroSim sorts fourteen foods we will meet in Istanbul into five layers:

| Layer | What it means |
|---|---|
| 1. Ancient Mediterranean and Anatolia | Grown or caught here since long before Byzantium |
| 2. Turkic herders from Central Asia | Brought by Turkish-speaking herders who moved into Anatolia after 1071 |
| 3. Ottoman trade and court | Arrived through the empire's trade routes and palace kitchens, often from the Arab and Persian worlds |
| 4. The Americas, after 1492 | Arrived through the Columbian Exchange |
| 5. The Turkish Republic, after 1923 | A 20th-century change |

The cards mix the familiar and the surprising. Tomatoes and red pepper flakes
feel as old as the Mediterranean, but both are American plants. Tea feels like
the most Turkish thing on the table, but it is the newest layer of all: the
Ottomans drank coffee, and the tea plantations at Rize date from the 1930s and
1940s. The cards come in a fixed shuffled order, the same for every learner,
so a group can compare answers card by card.

## How to Use

1. Read the food on the card in the white panel.
2. Press the button for the layer you think it belongs to (or press 1 to 5).
   Each card has one attempt.
3. The correct layer is outlined in green, a wrong choice in red, and the card
   settles into its correct bin. The feedback gives the reason.
4. Press **Next card** to continue. The score in the top right counts correct
   first placements.
5. After the fourteenth card, press **See result**. Hover over any card in the
   bins to read its reason again; missed cards have a red border.
6. **Restart** clears the bins and starts over in the same order.

## Lesson Plan

**Audience:** adult travelers preparing for the trip.  
**Duration:** 10–15 minutes.  
**Prerequisites:** food as history, the Columbian Exchange, kaymak, the Turkic
herders' arrival after 1071, and Rize.  
**Learning objective (Bloom: Understand):** Classify fourteen foods found in
Istanbul by the historical layer they came from, with at least 11 of 14
correct on the first attempt.

### Activities

1. **Ask the four questions (2 min):** Before starting, recall the chapter's
   four questions for any dish: where each ingredient first grew, who brought
   it and when, what the words say, and how people lived.
2. **Sort (6 min):** Work through all fourteen cards. When a word gives a
   clue (*yoğurt* and *kaymak* are Turkic words, *pilav* is Persian, *şerbet*
   is Arabic), say it aloud before choosing.
3. **Read breakfast (3 min):** On the result screen, find the foods from
   tomorrow's breakfast table and name their layers. Four of the five layers
   are on the table.
4. **Read menemen (3 min):** Menemen is eggs cooked with tomatoes and green
   peppers in olive oil, served with tea. Name the layer of each part, and say
   why the dish in its current form can be no older than the 1800s.

### Assessment

Mastery is 11 of 14 correct on the first attempt. A follow-up question:
*Black Sea cooks make a corn bread called* mısır ekmeği. *Which layer does its
main ingredient come from, and what does the word* mısır *tell us?* Corn is
from the Americas (layer 4), and *mısır* also means Egypt, the route by which
corn arrived.
