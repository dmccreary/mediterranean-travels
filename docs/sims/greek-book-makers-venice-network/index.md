## About This MicroSim

Aldus Manutius's name stands on the title pages, but a Greek book printed in
Venice around 1500 was the work of a team: an editor to prepare the text, a
punchcutter to make the letters, a partner to put up the money, teachers who
had trained the editors, authors who came to the press, and patrons who
rewarded it. Venice also had Greek presses besides Aldus's.

The sim starts with ten people as unconnected boxes, showing only their names
and dates. Ten contribution cards come in a fixed order, and for each one we
choose who did that job. Only the first choice is scored; after a wrong
choice we keep choosing until we find the right person. Each correct answer
shows the person's role in their box and, for eight of the cards, draws a
link between two people:

| Link | Card |
|---|---|
| Musurus edited for Aldus | The first printed Plato, 1513 |
| Griffo cut type for Aldus | The italic and the Greek types |
| Torresani financed Aldus | Partner and father-in-law |
| Lascaris taught Musurus | Greek lessons for the young editor |
| Kallierges printed for Vlastos | The *Etymologicum Magnum*, 1499 |
| Erasmus was printed by Aldus | The *Adagia*, 1508 |
| Bembo edited for Aldus | Petrarch and Dante in small format |
| Leo X was the patron of Musurus | The Plato dedication and the call to Rome, 1516 |

As the cards are answered, the network grows around Aldus, with a separate
pair, Kallierges and Vlastos, beside it. After card 10 a last question asks
how many of the ten were born on Crete. The answer is three, Musurus,
Kallierges, and Vlastos, and their boxes then get gold borders: Cretans
worked both inside Aldus's circle and in a rival press.

**Notes on the facts.** Life dates for Griffo, Kallierges, and Vlastos are
approximate, so the sim shows them with "about" or "active." Sources differ
on two details. Musurus is said to have been born in Rethymno in some
accounts and in Candia (Heraklion) in others; either way he was a Cretan.
And the 1499 *Etymologicum Magnum* was published by Kallierges and Vlastos
with the patronage of Anna Notaras, so Vlastos was its financier and
publisher, but not its only backer.

## How to Use

1. Read the card below the network, for example *Cut the punches for the
   italic type and for Greek types.*
2. Press the name of the person who did that job. A wrong choice turns red
   and stays disabled; keep choosing until the right person turns green.
3. When you are right, the person's role appears in their box and the card's
   link is drawn. Press **Next card** (or Enter).
4. The score in the top right counts the cards you got right on your first
   choice.
5. After card 10, press **Last question**, type how many of the ten were born
   on Crete, and press **Check**.
6. Click any person you have placed to read about them. Zoom with **+** and
   **−**, and **Reset view** fits the whole network again. **Try Again**
   clears the links and the score.

## Lesson Plan

**Audience:** adult travelers preparing for the trip.  
**Duration:** 10 minutes.  
**Prerequisites:** Aldus Manutius, Marcus Musurus, Janus Lascaris, Andrea
Torresani, Francesco Griffo, Zacharias Kallierges, Nikolaos Vlastos,
Erasmus, Pietro Bembo, Pope Leo X, Aldine editions, and collation and
emendation.  
**Learning objective (Bloom: Analyze):** Attribute ten contributions to Greek
and humanist printing in Venice around 1500 to the people who made them,
attributing at least 8 of 10 correctly on the first attempt.

### Activities

1. **Name the jobs (2 min):** Before starting, list the jobs a printed book
   needs: editing, publishing, type cutting, financing, teaching, writing, and
   patronage. Guess which of the ten people did each.
2. **Attribute (6 min):** Work through the ten cards. Before each choice, say
   which job the card describes, then which person.
3. **Read the network (2 min):** On the finished network, count the people
   linked to Aldus and to Musurus. Notice that the Cretan editor is linked to
   the publisher, a teacher, and a patron, and that the second press stands
   apart from Aldus's.

### Assessment

Mastery is 8 of 10 correct on the first choice, plus the answer 3 to the Crete
question. As a follow-up, ask: *Why does the name of Aldus stand on the 1513
Plato, when Musurus prepared its text?* A strong answer separates the
publisher, who chose the book and paid for its printing, from the editor, who
compared the manuscripts and corrected the Greek.
