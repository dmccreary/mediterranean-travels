# Democracy's Workplaces in the Agora

## About This MicroSim

Walking the Ancient Agora today, we see a park of foundations. This MicroSim
turns those foundations back into a working government. A schematic plan shows
ten places, and nine job cards ask where an Athenian would go to do each job
of public life.

| # | Job card | Where it was done |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Read a proposed new law, or check the army call-up lists | Monument of the Eponymous Heroes |
| 2 | Prepare the agenda for the next Assembly | Bouleuterion |
| 3 | Eat dinner at public expense as a councillor on duty | Tholos |
| 4 | Look up the exact wording of an old decree | Metroon |
| 5 | File a charge of impiety, as Meletus did against Socrates | Royal Stoa |
| 6 | Sit in the shade and talk with Socrates | Stoa of Zeus Eleutherios |
| 7 | Hear Zeno teach beside the painting of Marathon | Stoa Poikile |
| 8 | Buy goods in a shop given by a king of Pergamon | Stoa of Attalos |
| 9 | Pray to the god of the forge | Temple of Hephaestus |

The tenth place, the boundary stone of about 500 BCE, can be clicked and read,
but it is never the answer.

Card 5 is the key card. Many people expect a trial to begin in a courtroom.
In Athens a charge of impiety was a matter of religious law, so it was filed
first with the King Archon at the Royal Stoa, where Plato sets the opening of
his dialogue *Euthyphro*. Card 8 is a time trap: the Stoa of Attalos, the one
building we can walk into, was built about 150 BCE, some 250 years after
Socrates.

At the end, six of the nine badges cluster along the west side, outlined in
gold: archive, council hall, dining hall, notice board, the King Archon's
office, and Socrates' favorite stoa. This was the democracy's street of
offices.

**Notes on the evidence.** The plan is schematic: it places buildings by side
of the square, not by measured position, and it leaves out many buildings,
including the Middle Stoa, the Odeon of Agrippa, and the law courts, whose
locations are less certain. Some of the buildings also changed over time:

- The Monument of the Eponymous Heroes whose foundations we see dates from
  about the 330s BCE; an earlier version stood somewhere in the Agora in the
  5th century, probably in a different spot.
- The city archive moved into the Old Bouleuterion at the end of the 5th
  century BCE, when a new council hall was built behind it. The Metroon whose
  foundations we see is a Hellenistic rebuilding of the 2nd century BCE.
- Ancient writers, Xenophon and Plato among them, place Socrates in
  conversation at the Stoa of Zeus Eleutherios; he also talked in workshops,
  gymnasia, and private houses.

## How to Use

1. Click any place on the plan to read about it in the yellow strip. Reading
   is free and does not count.
2. Read the job card. Click the place where that job was done, or choose it in
   the list, then press **Answer**. Only the first answer counts.
3. The correct place turns green, a wrong choice turns red, and the card's
   number is pinned beside the correct place: green if you got it right, red
   if you missed it.
4. Press **Next card**. After card 9, press **See the plan** to see all nine
   badges and the street of offices.
5. On the final plan, click a numbered badge to read its job again.
   **Try Again** repeats all nine cards.

## Lesson Plan

**Audience:** adult travelers reading Chapter 16, "Day 11: Athens, the Agora,
Democracy, and Paul on the Areopagus", before or after walking the Agora.  
**Duration:** 10 minutes.  
**Prerequisites:** the Ancient Agora of Athens, stoa, Royal Stoa, Metroon,
Bouleuterion, Tholos, Monument of the Eponymous Heroes, Stoa Poikile, Stoa of
Attalos, Temple of Hephaestus, the Council of 500, and the trial of Socrates.  
**Learning objective (Bloom: Understand):** Classify nine jobs of Athenian
public life by the Agora building or monument where each was done, choosing
correctly for at least 7 of 9 on the first attempt.

### Activities

1. **Explore (2 min):** Click all ten places and read their descriptions.
   Which ones were offices of government, and which were for shopping,
   teaching, or worship?
2. **Classify (6 min):** Work through the nine cards.
3. **Walk it (2 min):** On site, start at the boundary stone and walk north
   along the west side. Name what happened in each building as you pass it.

### Assessment

Mastery is 7 of 9 correct on the first attempt, including card 5, the
impiety charge. A follow-up question: *Why did a charge against Socrates go to
a stoa rather than a court?* A strong answer says that impiety was religious
law, handled by the King Archon, whose office was the Royal Stoa; the trial
before a jury came afterward.

5. Aristotle, *Constitution of the Athenians* 43–44 and 53.4 — The Council of 500, the councillors on duty (prytaneis) who dined in the Tholos with some staying overnight, and notices posted at the Eponymous Heroes.
6. Plato, *Euthyphro* 2a — Socrates at the Royal Stoa, the King Archon's office, to answer the charge of impiety.
7. Xenophon, *Oeconomicus* 7.1 — Socrates in conversation in the Stoa of Zeus Eleutherios.
8. Diogenes Laertius, *Lives of the Eminent Philosophers* 7.5 — Zeno teaching in the Stoa Poikile, and the Stoics named after it.
9. [Stoa of Attalos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stoa_of_Attalos) and [Temple of Hephaestus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Temple_of_Hephaestus) — Wikipedia. The stoa's 42 shops and its rebuilding of 1953–1956; the temple begun about 449 BCE.
