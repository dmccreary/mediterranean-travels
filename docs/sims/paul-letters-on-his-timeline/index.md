## About This MicroSim

Nearly all scholars, of every faith and none, agree that Paul himself wrote
seven of the thirteen letters that bear his name: Romans, 1 and 2 Corinthians,
Galatians, Philippians, 1 Thessalonians, and Philemon. None of them is dated.
Historians place them in Paul's life the way a detective works, from clues
inside each letter (who is with Paul, where he is, what has just happened)
matched against the outline of his travels in Acts and a few fixed points such
as the term of the proconsul Gallio in Corinth, about 51–52 CE, known from an
inscription found at Delphi.

This MicroSim gives us the same clues. The timeline runs from 30 to 70 CE and
shows six periods of Paul's life:

| Band | Period | About |
|---|---|---|
| A | Call and early years | 33–46 CE |
| B | First journey | 46–48 CE |
| C | Second journey | 49–52 CE |
| D | Third journey, including two to three years in Ephesus | 52–57 CE |
| E | Prison in Caesarea | 57–59 CE |
| F | Voyage and Rome | 59–62 CE |

Four events give fixed points: the meeting in Jerusalem about Gentile
believers (about 49), Gallio in Corinth (51–52), Paul in Ephesus (about
52–55), and Paul's death in Rome according to later tradition (about 62–67).
All the dates are scholars' reconstructions and differ by a year or two.

Most of the letters have one accepted period. Three do not, because scholars
genuinely disagree. **Galatians** is usually placed in the third journey and
sometimes in the second (a minority view, which the sim does not score as
correct, dates it before the Jerusalem meeting, about 48). **Philippians** and
**Philemon** were written from prison, and Paul does not say which prison: an
Ephesian imprisonment that Acts does not mention, custody in Caesarea, or
house arrest in Rome. The sim accepts every period that has wide scholarly
support and, at the end, outlines the other accepted periods with dashed
boxes. The order of the letters in the New Testament is by length, not date,
so Romans, which comes first in our Bibles, was among the last written.

## How to Use

1. Read the six periods on the top row of the timeline and in the key below
   it. Hover over a period or an event for details, or click an event to read
   it in the panel.
2. Choose a letter card in the tray. Its clue and passage appear in the panel
   at the top.
3. Place the card: drag it onto the timeline over a period, or click a period
   in the card's own row. (Keys: 1 to 7 select a card; A to F place it.) We
   can move a card as often as we like before checking.
4. When all seven are placed, press **Check**. Accepted cards turn green,
   lock, and show why. The others return to the tray with a hint.
5. Place the returned cards again and press **Check again**. After the
   second Check, any card still wrong moves to its accepted period and is
   marked "shown" in amber.
6. Select any card to read its reasoning. **Start over** clears everything.

## Lesson Plan

**Audience:** adult travelers preparing for the visit to Ephesus.  
**Duration:** 10–15 minutes.  
**Prerequisites:** the seven undisputed letters, Paul's missionary journeys,
the Book of Acts, Timothy and Titus, Phoebe and Cenchreae, and the Ephesian
imprisonment theory (Chapter 9).  
**Learning objective (Bloom: Analyze):** Organize the seven undisputed letters
onto a timeline of Paul's life, using one clue from inside each letter,
placing all seven in an accepted period within two attempts.

### Activities

1. **Predict (2 min):** Before reading any clues, guess which letter was
   written first. Many people guess Romans, because it comes first in the
   New Testament.
2. **Read and place (7 min):** For each card, underline in your head the
   *person* and the *place* in its clue (Timothy, Athens; Ephesus; Titus,
   Macedonia; Phoebe, Cenchreae), then find the period when Paul was there.
3. **Check and revise (3 min):** Press Check, read the reasons, and place any
   returned cards again.
4. **Look at the pattern (3 min):** Count how many letters landed in period D.
   Notice that the seven letters come from about ten years, not thirty, and
   that only the prison letters remain debated.

### Assessment

Mastery is all seven placed in accepted periods within two Checks. As a
follow-up, ask: *Why can we say for certain that 1 Corinthians was written in
Ephesus, but not Philippians?* A strong answer says 1 Corinthians names
Ephesus ("I will stay in Ephesus until Pentecost"), while Philippians names
only a praetorium and Caesar's household, which existed in any provincial
capital as well as in Rome.
