## About This MicroSim

In 1952 Michael Ventris showed that the Linear B tablets from Knossos and
Pylos were written in an early form of Greek. He did it without a bilingual
text: Alice Kober's patterns and his own grid of signs that shared a
consonant or a vowel let one good guess unlock dozens of signs at once. This
MicroSim replays a small, real piece of that work in five steps.

| Step | What the learner does | Scored? |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Count the signs | Decide from "about 90 sound-signs" what kind of script this is | Practice |
| 2. Kober's triplet | Decide what the changing signs of *a-mi-ni-so*, *a-mi-ni-si-jo*, *a-mi-ni-si-ja* share; two signs join one grid row | Practice |
| 3. Guess the place names | Match three sign groups found only at Knossos to Knossos, Amnisos, and Tylissos (Phaistos is a distractor) using three clues | Yes, first Check |
| 4. Unlock the grid | Watch eight signs get values from the place names, then the signs of the next tablet | No |
| 5. Read the tripod tablet | Predict the objects named by four words, then see the scribe's pictograms | Yes, first Check |

The signs are real Linear B characters from the Unicode Linear B Syllabary
block, drawn with the Noto Sans Linear B font and labeled with their sign
numbers from Emmett Bennett's catalog (B070 is *ko*, B052 *no*, B012 *so*,
and so on). They gain sound values only when the learner unlocks them. The
values are the ones Ventris and Chadwick published.

Step 3 is labeled "a simplified version of Ventris's test." Ventris's real
grid grew over 20 Work Notes and had many more rows; the three clues here
stand in for that pattern work. Step 5 uses the tablet Carl Blegen dug up at
Pylos in 1952 and read with Ventris's values in 1953, now known as PY Ta
641. Its words are *ti-ri-po-de* (two tripods), *qe-to-ro-we* (a
four-handled cup), *ti-ri-o-we-e* (two three-handled cups; the chapter
quotes the stem *ti-ri-o-we*, and the same tablet also writes
*ti-ri-jo-we*), and *a-no-we* (a cup with no handles). The pictograms are
simplified drawings after the tablet, with the count written, as the
scribe did, in short upright strokes.

## How to Use

1. **Step 1:** Look at the sixteen unread signs, then choose the kind of
   script with the buttons. A wrong answer gives a hint; try again.
2. **Step 2:** Compare the three words. The highlighted sign changes from
   word 1 to words 2 and 3. Choose what the two signs probably share. Watch
   them move into one row of the grid on the right.
3. **Step 3:** Read the three clues and choose a place name for each group
   from its menu, then press **Check**. Only the first Check counts; you can
   change wrong answers and check again for practice.
4. **Step 4:** See the eight values in the grid, then press **Unlock the
   tablet signs**.
5. **Step 5:** Use the word bank and the values under each sign to choose an
   object for each word, then press **Check**. The pictograms appear beside
   the words.
6. On a narrow screen, and in step 5, tick **Show the grid** to see the grid
   in place of the puzzle. **Start over** returns to step 1.

## Lesson Plan

**Audience:** adult travelers preparing for the Linear B tablets in the
Heraklion Archaeological Museum on Day 9.  
**Duration:** 15–20 minutes.  
**Prerequisites:** syllabaries and the sign-count rule (Chapter 2); Linear
B and its spelling rules, including the worked example "spelling Knossos the
Mycenaean way"; Alice Kober's triplets, Ventris's grid, and the place-name
guess.  
**Learning objective (Bloom: Apply):** Solve a simplified version of the
1952 decipherment by matching three Linear B sign groups to Cretan place
names with grid clues and then using the resulting sound values to predict
four objects on the Pylos tripod tablet, getting all three place names and
at least 3 of 4 objects right on the first attempt.

### Activities

1. **Warm up (2 min):** Spell *Knossos*, *Amnisos*, and *Tylissos* the
   Mycenaean way on paper, using the chapter's spelling rules.
2. **Solve (10 min):** Work through the five steps. In step 3, say aloud
   which clue rules out each wrong name before you check.
3. **Reflect (3 min):** In step 4, find *si* in the grid. Why did it need no
   new guess?
4. **Discuss (5 min):** Why did the tripod tablet convince the doubters when
   the place names alone did not? (The scribe's pictures were independent
   evidence that no one could have arranged to fit Ventris's values.)

### Assessment

Mastery is all three place names right on the first Check in step 3 and at
least 3 of 4 objects right on the first Check in step 5. Follow-up question:
*A friend says Ventris "just guessed" the decipherment. What would you
answer?* The guess was a tested hypothesis: Kober's patterns and the grid
meant one correct guess gave values to many signs, and those values then
read new tablets, like the tripod tablet, correctly.
