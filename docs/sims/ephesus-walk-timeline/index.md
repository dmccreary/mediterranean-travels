# One Walk, Seven Centuries

## About This MicroSim

The standard walk through Ephesus runs downhill, from the upper city past
Curetes Street to the Library of Celsus, then along Marble Street to the Great
Theater. It is easy to assume that the monuments we pass are about the same
age, or that the walk runs steadily from older to newer. Neither is true.

This MicroSim takes nine monuments in the order we pass them. Each one gets
its own row on a timeline from 300 BCE to 500 CE. For monuments 2 to 9 we
predict, before its date appears, whether it was begun **earlier (older)** or
**later (younger)** than the monument we just passed, shown as a gold bar.
Then its bar appears with the reason.

| # | Monument | Date | Bar |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Memmius Monument | Late 1st century BCE | c. 40–1 BCE |
| 2 | Gate of Hercules | 4th–5th century CE, built with older reliefs | c. 350–450 CE |
| 3 | Fountain of Trajan | Early 2nd century CE | c. 102–114 CE |
| 4 | Temple of Hadrian | Dedicated before 138 CE | c. 117–138 CE |
| 5 | Terrace House 2 | 1st century BCE to 3rd century CE | c. 25 BCE – 270 CE |
| 6 | Library of Celsus | Begun c. 110 CE | c. 110–125 CE |
| 7 | Gate of Mazaeus and Mithridates | 4–3 BCE | c. 4–3 BCE |
| 8 | Great Theater | First built 3rd century BCE; rebuilt 1st–2nd century CE | c. 290–250 BCE and c. 41–117 CE |
| 9 | Harbor Street (Arcadiane) | Rebuilt c. 400 CE | c. 395–408 CE |

A monument is compared by the year its first bar begins. The bars are rounded
for display, and every one is marked "c." Most monuments were built over
years and repaired for centuries. The Library's end date, 125 CE, is
illustrative only: its completion date is not known exactly. The Gate of
Hercules is dated by when it was assembled, although its carved reliefs are
older blocks reused as spolia.

After the ninth monument, a reference row adds Paul's years in Ephesus, about
52 to 55 CE, and the riot of the silversmiths, about 55 CE. The riot filled a
theater whose Roman rebuilding was still under way.

## How to Use

1. Read the first stop, the Memmius Monument, and its bar. Press
   **Walk on**.
2. For each new stop, read its name and where it stands, then predict
   **Older** or **Younger** than the gold bar (keys O and Y). One prediction
   per stop; it cannot be changed.
3. The bar appears with feedback, and the row label is marked ✓ or ✗. The
   score counts correct predictions out of 8.
4. Press **Walk on** (or Enter) for the next stop. After stop 9, press
   **See the whole walk**.
5. Drag the timeline sideways, or use **+**, **−**, and **Full span**, to
   compare bars. Hover over a bar for its date and reason. **Walk again**
   restarts from the first stop.

## Lesson Plan

**Audience:** adult travelers preparing to walk through Ephesus.  
**Duration:** 10–15 minutes.  
**Prerequisites:** reading a ruin and spolia (Chapter 4); the Memmius
Monument, Curetes Street, the Gate of Hercules, the Fountain of Trajan, the
Temple of Hadrian, the terrace houses, the Library of Celsus, the Gate of
Mazaeus and Mithridates, the Great Theater, and the Harbor Street
(Chapter 10).  
**Learning objective (Bloom: Understand):** Compare the construction dates
of nine monuments passed on the downhill walk through Ephesus, predicting for
each one whether it was begun before or after the monument passed just before
it, with at least 6 of 8 predictions correct.

### Activities

1. **Predict the pattern (2 min):** Before starting, guess whether the walk
   will run from older to newer, from newer to older, or neither.
2. **Walk (8 min):** Make the eight predictions. Before each one, say which
   emperor or king the monument belongs to, if you know.
3. **Read the finished timeline (3 min):** Trace the bars from row 1 to row 9
   and count how many times the walk jumps back in time. Which two neighbors
   are furthest apart in date?
4. **Connect to Paul (2 min):** Find the reference row. Which monuments on
   the walk already stood when Paul was in Ephesus?

### Assessment

Mastery is 6 of 8 correct predictions. As a follow-up, ask: *A guide says,
"Everything you see on Curetes Street is Roman, from the 2nd century." Use
three monuments from the walk to correct the statement.* A strong answer
names the Augustan Memmius Monument, the 2nd-century Fountain of Trajan and
Temple of Hadrian, and the late-antique Gate of Hercules built of older
blocks.

## References

1. Peter Scherrer, ed., *Ephesus: The New Guide*, rev. ed. (Ege Yayınları,
   2000). The standard site guide by the Austrian excavators, with dates for
   each monument on the walk.
2. [Ephesus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ephesus) — Wikipedia. The city's
   history and its main monuments, including the theater, the terrace houses,
   and the Arcadiane.
3. [Library of Celsus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Library_of_Celsus) —
   Wikipedia. Commissioned in the 110s CE by Tiberius Julius Aquila in memory
   of his father, and completed under Hadrian, after Aquila's death.
4. [Hadrian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hadrian) — Wikipedia. The
   emperor (117–138 CE) to whom the small temple on Curetes Street was
   dedicated, and Trajan's successor.
5. Helmut Koester, ed., *Ephesos, Metropolis of Asia* (Harvard Theological
   Studies 41; Trinity Press International, 1995). Essays by the excavators
   on the city's buildings, inscriptions, and religion, including the
   theater and the Roman rebuilding of the city center.
6. [Arcadius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arcadius) — Wikipedia. The
   emperor (395–408 CE) whose name the rebuilt Harbor Street carries.
7. Acts 19, in any modern translation, for example the
   [New Revised Standard Version](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Acts+19&version=NRSVUE).
   Paul's stay in Ephesus and the riot in the theater.
