## About This MicroSim

Everyone knows the Colossus of Rhodes as a giant with a foot on each side of the harbor. This poster puts that popular picture on the left and what the evidence supports on the right, for three parts of the story: the pose, the place, and the end. It applies the book's *myth versus history* test from [Chapter 4](../../chapters/04-byzantine-constantinople/index.md): find the earliest source, and ask how long after the event it was written.

The right-hand figure is an artist's guess, drawn pale to say so. The evidence gives the god, the sculptor, the height, and the dates, but not the pose.

## How to Use

**Explore mode** (the default):

1. **Hover** over a zone to see a short tooltip with its name and its idea.
2. **Click** a zone to open an infobox below the image with its details. On a
   tablet or phone, tap a zone instead.

**Quiz mode:** press **Quiz Mode** at the bottom. The name pills disappear, and
a question appears below the image. Click the zone that answers it. A correct
answer shows a short explanation; a wrong one shakes the zone so you can try
again. The score counts your correct answers, and **Try Again** starts a new
round with the questions in a new order.

**Edit mode:** add `?edit=true` to the address of `main.html` to drag the
corners of each zone, then press **Copy JSON** and paste the zones into
`data.json`.

## The Zones

1. **Popular: The Figure**: The famous engravings show a giant with a foot on each side of the harbor entrance, and the book's evidence does not support it.
2. **Likely: The Figure**: The evidence gives the god, the sculptor, the height, and the dates, but not the pose.
3. **Popular: The Site**: The popular story places the statue's feet at the entrance to Mandraki, the old harbor.
4. **Likely: The Site**: Scholars think it more likely stood on land, near the harbor or in a sanctuary of Helios higher in the city.
5. **Popular: The End**: A Byzantine chronicle says the bronze was sold and carried away on hundreds of camels.
6. **Likely: The End**: An earthquake brought it down after about 54 years, and a Roman writer saw the fallen pieces.

## Lesson Plan

### Learning Objective

The learner will separate what most people imagine about the Colossus of Rhodes from what the book's evidence supports, and apply the *myth versus history* test from Chapter 4 to three parts of the story: the pose, the place, and the end.

### Grade Level

Adult travelers on the trip (Mediterranean Travels).

### Duration

10–15 minutes

### Prerequisites

Myth versus history, from [Chapter 4](../../chapters/04-byzantine-constantinople/index.md), and the Colossus, from [Chapter 12](../../chapters/12-rhodes/index.md).

### Activities

1. **Exploration** (5 min): Hover over every zone, then click each one and read
   its infobox.
2. **Guided Practice** (5 min): For each of the three rows, find the earliest source named and say how long after the event it was written.
3. **Assessment** (5 min): Switch to Quiz mode and answer all 9 questions.

### Assessment

The learner answers at least 8 of the 9 quiz questions correctly on the first
try and can name which part of the popular picture the evidence does not support.
