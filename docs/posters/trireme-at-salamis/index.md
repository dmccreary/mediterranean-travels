## About This MicroSim

The Athenian fleet won the battle of Salamis in 480 BCE, and this cutaway shows what it was won with. It shows the trireme, with its oarsmen on three levels, its bronze ram, and its officers and marines, and the fleet and the narrows beyond it, and the seat of Xerxes on the hill above.

The point the poster makes is about democracy. The ships were rowed by many of the poorest citizens, and that gave them a claim on the city. The chapters give the ram, the three levels, and the numbers of rowers, but not the oar arrangement or the steering oars, so the drawing is illustrative there.

## How to Use

**Explore mode** (the default): hover over a numbered marker or its label to
read about that part. The marker and its label light up together.

**Quiz mode:** press **Quiz**. The labels disappear and a description appears.
Click the marker that matches it. A correct answer scores a point; a wrong one
lets you try again. **Try Again** starts a new round.

**Edit mode:** add `?edit=true` to the address of `main.html` to drag the
markers, then press **Copy JSON** and paste the positions into `data.json`.

## Lesson Plan

### Learning Objective

The learner will explain how the Athenian trireme linked a sea victory to a change in who held power, by naming what the ship was made of, who rowed it, and how Athens's fleet gave the poorest citizens a claim on the city.

### Grade Level

Adult travelers on the trip (Mediterranean Travels).

### Duration

10–15 minutes

### Prerequisites

Salamis and Themistocles, from [Chapter 15](../../chapters/15-athens-acropolis/index.md), and Athenian democracy, from [Chapter 16](../../chapters/16-athens-agora-democracy/index.md).

### Activities

1. **Exploration** (5 min): Hover over the markers from 1 to 8 and read what each part is.
2. **Guided Practice** (5 min): Start with marker 1, the oarsmen, and say why who rowed matters to who could vote.
3. **Assessment** (5 min): Switch to Quiz mode and identify all 8 parts.

### Assessment

The learner identifies at least 7 of the 8 parts from their descriptions on the first try and can name who rowed the ships and why that mattered for democracy.
