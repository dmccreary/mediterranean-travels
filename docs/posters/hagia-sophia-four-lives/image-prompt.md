## The Four Interior Tiles: One Camera, One Building

The four tiles show **the same room from exactly the same camera position**:
standing on the floor of the nave, a little west of the center, looking east
along the length of the church. Draw the architecture **once, identically, in
all four tiles**, and change only what was put into it or taken out of it.

The fixed architecture, the same in all four:

- The great central dome, centered horizontally in the tile, with its crown
  about 22% down from the top of the tile (the top 12% stays plain, see
  below). A ring of small arched windows runs around the base of the dome, so
  light seems to hold it up.
- Below the dome, on each side, a huge curved triangle of stone (a pendentive)
  turns the square space into a circle, resting on great arches and massive
  piers at the left and right edges of the tile.
- Straight ahead, a half-dome sits below the main dome, and below that a
  curved apse at the far (east) end.
- On both sides, two levels of arcades: columns below, a gallery above.
- The floor runs across the bottom 10 percent of the tile, in pale marble.
- Warm daylight enters from the dome windows.

The four lives, and what changes in each:

### Tile 1 (top left): the cathedral, 537 to 1453

**Mood**: gold, deep blue, and warm cream marble, lit by many hanging oil lamps.
**What changes**: the apse and half-dome glow with gold mosaic, and in the
apse a seated figure of the Virgin holding a child, drawn simply, in deep blue
on the gold. Gold crosses and flat patterns decorate the upper vault. A
raised altar with a canopy stands before the apse. A few small figures in
long Byzantine robes and a priest in a rich vestment stand on the floor. No
crescent, no calligraphy, no carpets, no scaffolding.

### Tile 2 (top right): the mosque, 1453 to 1934

**Mood**: turquoise, cream, and terracotta.
**What changes**: the apse mosaic is gone under plain cream plaster, with a
pointed prayer niche in the apse wall and, beside it, a stepped pulpit with a
pointed canopy. **Four large round discs**, each filled with abstract curling
gold flourishes on a dark ground (no letters), hang high on the piers and
pendentives, two on each side. Round hanging lamps drop low from long chains.
Deep red and blue carpets cover the floor. A few worshippers in long robes
stand far away, seen from behind.

### Tile 3 (bottom left): the museum, 1935 to 2020

**Mood**: cool, pale gray stone, bright and even daylight.
**What changes**: the apse mosaic of the Virgin and child is uncovered again,
in gold and blue as in Tile 1. The round discs from Tile 2 are still on the
piers. The floor is bare pale marble with no carpets and no lamps hanging
low. In the far left bay, a light wooden scaffold stands against an arch. A
few visitors in plain everyday clothes of different ages walk across the
floor, looking up, one pointing at the apse. A low rope barrier runs along
one side.

### Tile 4 (bottom right): the mosque again, 2020 to today

**Mood**: warm reds and sage greens with soft light.
**What changes**: a soft sage-green carpet with parallel stripes covers the
whole nave floor, with lines running toward the apse. The apse mosaic is
softly veiled by a pale curtain, and the apse wall behind it keeps its prayer
niche and the stepped pulpit as in Tile 2. The round discs hang on the piers
as before. A few small worshippers, seen from behind and very far away, stand
and kneel in rows toward the apse. In the gallery level high on the left side,
a few small visitors stand along the rail and look down. The mood is calm and
respectful.
