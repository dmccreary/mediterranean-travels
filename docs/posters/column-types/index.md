## About This MicroSim

Greek architects worked in three related systems of design, called the
**classical orders**: the [Doric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doric_order),
the [Ionic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ionic_order), and the
[Corinthian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corinthian_order). Each order fixes
the shape of a column's base, shaft, and capital, the parts of the beam it
carries, and its proportions. This infographic shows one column of each order
side by side, drawn to the same height, so their differences are easy to
compare. Each column also carries a short piece of its own entablature.

The capital is the quickest clue. A plain cushion means Doric, a pair of
spiral scrolls means Ionic, and a bell of acanthus leaves means Corinthian.
The base, the flutes, and the proportions then confirm the answer.

## How to Use

**Explore mode** (the default):

1. **Hover** over a column to see a short tooltip with its name and key feature.
2. **Click** a column to open an infobox below the image with its base, shaft,
   capital, frieze, proportions, origins, and where we see it on our route.
   On a tablet or phone, tap a column instead.

**Quiz mode:** press **Quiz Mode** at the bottom. The name pills disappear, and
a question appears below the image. Click the column that answers it. A correct
answer shows a short explanation; a wrong one shakes the column so you can try
again. The score counts your correct answers, and **Try Again** starts a new
round with the questions in a new order.

**Edit mode:** add `?edit=true` to the address of `main.html` to drag the
corners of each column's zone, then press **Copy JSON** and paste the zones into
`data.json`.

## The Three Columns

1. **Doric**: no base, about 20 sharp-edged flutes, a cushion capital under a
   square slab, and a frieze of triglyphs and metopes. Sturdy, about 4 to 6
   diameters tall. The Parthenon.
2. **Ionic**: a molded base, about 24 flutes separated by flat strips, and a
   capital with two spiral volutes. Slender, about 8 to 9 diameters tall. The
   Temple of Artemis at Ephesus and the Erechtheion.
3. **Corinthian**: an Ionic-like base and shaft with a tall bell-shaped capital
   wrapped in acanthus leaves. The most slender, about 9 to 10 diameters tall.
   Roman Ephesus and the Temple of Olympian Zeus in Athens.

## Lesson Plan

### Learning Objective

The learner will identify the Doric, Ionic, and Corinthian orders from their
capitals, bases, flutes, and proportions, and name a building on our route
built in each order.

### Grade Level

Adult travelers on the trip (Mediterranean Travels).

### Duration

10–15 minutes

### Prerequisites

The parts of a column and its entablature (base, shaft, flutes, capital,
architrave, frieze, cornice), introduced in
[Chapter 10: Ephesus](../../chapters/10-ephesus-greco-roman-city/index.md).

### Activities

1. **Exploration** (5 min): Hover over all three columns, then click each one
   and read its infobox. Name the single feature that best separates each order
   from the other two.
2. **Guided Practice** (5 min): Compare the Ionic and Corinthian infoboxes.
   Which features do they share, and which one feature tells them apart?
3. **Assessment** (5 min): Switch to Quiz mode and answer all nine questions.

### Assessment

The learner answers at least 8 of the 9 quiz questions correctly on the first
try and can explain, in one sentence, why a fluted drum alone cannot separate
Ionic from Corinthian.
