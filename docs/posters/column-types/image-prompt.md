## Overall Composition

Three ancient Greek columns stand side by side in a straight front view
(elevation, eye level, no perspective distortion), each centered in its own
third of the image. From left to right: a **Doric** column, an **Ionic** column,
and a **Corinthian** column. All three are the same total height, so the
viewer can compare their thickness and decoration directly. Each column
carries a short section of its own entablature (the horizontal beam) on top.

All three stand on one shared stone platform (stylobate) of three low stone
steps that runs across the full width of the image at the bottom.

## What to Draw

### 1. Doric column (left third)

**Position**: centered horizontally at 16.7% from the left edge.
**Visual**: the sturdiest of the three, clearly the thickest: its height is about
5½ times its bottom diameter (about 125 px wide at the bottom). **No base**: the
shaft rises straight from the top step of the platform. The shaft tapers
slightly toward the top, with a gentle swelling (entasis), and has about 20
shallow flutes that meet in sharp vertical ridges. The capital is very plain: a
round, cushion-shaped molding (echinus) under a flat, square slab (abacus).
Above it, a short entablature: a plain smooth beam (architrave), then a frieze
of alternating triglyphs (blocks carved with three vertical grooves) and plain
square metope panels, then a projecting cornice.

### 2. Ionic column (middle third)

**Position**: centered horizontally at 50% from the left edge.
**Visual**: noticeably more slender than the Doric: its height is about 9 times
its bottom diameter (about 78 px wide at the bottom). It stands on a **molded
base** of stacked rounded rings (two convex rings separated by a concave
groove) resting on the top step. The shaft has about 24 deeper flutes
separated by narrow flat strips (fillets). The capital has **two large spiral
scrolls (volutes)**, one curling down on the left and one on the right, joined
by a band with a small egg-and-dart molding, under a thin square slab. Above
it, a short entablature: an architrave of three plain horizontal bands, each
stepped slightly forward; a smooth continuous frieze; and a cornice with a row
of small square blocks (dentils) beneath it.

### 3. Corinthian column (right third)

**Position**: centered horizontally at 83.3% from the left edge.
**Visual**: the most slender of the three: its height is about 10 times its
bottom diameter (about 70 px wide at the bottom). It has the same kind of
molded ring base and the same 24 flutes with flat strips as the Ionic column.
The capital is tall and **bell-shaped, wrapped in two rows of deeply cut,
curling acanthus leaves**, with small spiral tendrils (helices) curling out
toward each corner and a thin square slab with concave sides on top, with a
small flower in the center of each side. Above it, an ornate short
entablature: an architrave of three stepped bands, a frieze carved with a
flowing acanthus-scroll relief, and a cornice with dentils and small carved
brackets beneath it.
