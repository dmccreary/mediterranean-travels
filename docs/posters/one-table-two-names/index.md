## About This MicroSim

Turkey and Greece share many dishes and quarrel over their names. This poster shows one long table seen from above: Turkish dishes in the top row, Greek in the bottom row, and six pairs lined up in columns. Click a pair to see both names, what the chapters say about it, and where the two versions differ.

This is an empathy poster, not an argument about who invented what. The two kitchens grew from a shared Ottoman and Byzantine past, and from the population exchange of 1923, which sent families and their recipes across the Aegean. Each country calls its own version its own, and each is right to.

## How to Use

**Explore mode** (the default):

1. **Hover** over a zone to see a short tooltip with its name and its idea.
2. **Click** a zone to open an infobox below the image with its details. On a
   tablet or phone, tap a zone instead.

**Quiz mode:** press **Quiz Mode** at the bottom. The name pills disappear, and
a question appears below the image. Click the zone that answers it. A correct
answer shows a short explanation; a wrong one shakes the zone so you can try
again. The score counts your correct answers, and **Try Again** starts a new
round with the questions in a new order.

**Edit mode:** add `?edit=true` to the address of `main.html` to drag the
corners of each zone, then press **Copy JSON** and paste the zones into
`data.json`.

## The Zones

1. **Vine Leaves**: One small plate, two names, on a table both kitchens share.
2. **Yogurt Dip**: Cool yogurt with cucumber and garlic, a little thinner on the Turkish table.
3. **Eggplant**: A smoky eggplant salad, and a family of cousins, found on both sides of the Aegean.
4. **Anise Spirit**: Two clear aniseed spirits that turn milky white with water and belong to a long meal.
5. **Baklava**: The dish is agreed on, and the argument is over whom it belongs to.
6. **Coffee**: The same coffee, made the same way, under a name that changed because a word can carry a wound.

## Lesson Plan

### Learning Objective

The learner will match six Turkish and Greek dish pairs, give both names for each, and explain, without taking a side, why the two kitchens share so many dishes: one empire for centuries, and the population exchange of 1923.

### Grade Level

Adult travelers on the trip (Mediterranean Travels).

### Duration

10–15 minutes

### Prerequisites

Meze, coffee, and baklava, from [Chapter 6](../../chapters/06-bazaars-scripts-presses/index.md), and the population exchange, from [Chapter 7](../../chapters/07-end-of-empire-modern-turkey/index.md).

### Activities

1. **Exploration** (5 min): Hover over every zone, then click each one and read
   its infobox.
2. **Guided Practice** (5 min): Choose one pair and say both names aloud. Then say one thing the two versions share and one thing that differs.
3. **Assessment** (5 min): Switch to Quiz mode and answer all 10 questions.

### Assessment

The learner answers at least 9 of the 10 quiz questions correctly on the first
try and can give both names for at least four pairs.
