## About This MicroSim

We plan to be in Istanbul on a Friday, and a visit to a working mosque is easier when you know the building. This cutaway follows a visitor from the courtyard and its ablution fountain, past the shoe racks, into the prayer hall, and up to the dome. It ends at the mihrab, the minbar, and the minaret that carries the call to prayer.

Each marker says what the part is for and what a respectful visitor does there, including dress, shoes, and photography. The prayer times in the chapter are its own calculation, so check the day's times on arrival.

## How to Use

**Explore mode** (the default): hover over a numbered marker or its label to
read about that part. The marker and its label light up together.

**Quiz mode:** press **Quiz**. The labels disappear and a description appears.
Click the marker that matches it. A correct answer scores a point; a wrong one
lets you try again. **Try Again** starts a new round.

**Edit mode:** add `?edit=true` to the address of `main.html` to drag the
markers, then press **Copy JSON** and paste the positions into `data.json`.

## Lesson Plan

### Learning Objective

The learner will name nine parts of an Ottoman mosque and its courtyard in the order a visitor meets them, and say what each part is for and what a respectful visitor does there.

### Grade Level

Adult travelers on the trip (Mediterranean Travels).

### Duration

10–15 minutes

### Prerequisites

Daily prayer, ablution, the minaret, and the call to prayer, from [Chapter 5](../../chapters/05-ottoman-istanbul/index.md).

### Activities

1. **Exploration** (5 min): Hover over the markers from 1 to 9 in order, as if
   walking in. For each, say what you would do there.
2. **Guided Practice** (5 min): Find the three parts that tell you which way to
   face, and the one that carries the call to prayer.
3. **Assessment** (5 min): Switch to Quiz mode and identify all nine parts.

### Assessment

The learner identifies at least 8 of the 9 parts from their descriptions on the first try and can state one thing a respectful visitor does at the fountain, the entrance, and the prayer hall.
