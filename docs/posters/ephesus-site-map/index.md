## About This MicroSim

This map is a photo of the information sign at the site of Ephesus (in Turkish,
*Efes Antik Kenti*), taken on
[Day 7](../../chapters/10-ephesus-greco-roman-city/index.md). The sign numbers 27
places, and each overlay marker sits on top of the sign's own number, so the
labels and descriptions here are the sign's key brought to life. North is up.

Three hills frame the map. The Roman city lies in the valley between
**Panayırdağ** to the north (the ancient Mount Pion) and **Bülbüldağ** to the
south (Mount Koressos). The hill of **Ayasuluk**, in the top right corner, is
where people moved as the harbor silted up, and it is today's town of Selçuk.
Reading the map from left to right reads Ephesus's story in order: the lost
harbor, the Roman city, the Temple of Artemis on the plain, and the church,
mosque, and fortress on Ayasuluk.

Three numbers appear more than once on the sign: 15 and 18 are city walls that
run around the city, and 22 marks two roads to the Temple of Artemis. Each has
one marker here.

## How to Use

**Explore mode** (the default): hover over a numbered marker or its label to
read about that place. The marker and its label light up together.

**Quiz mode:** press **Quiz**. The labels disappear and a description appears.
Click the marker that matches it. A correct answer scores a point; a wrong one
lets you try again. **Try Again** starts a new round.

**Edit mode:** add `?edit=true` to the address of `main.html` to drag the
markers, then press **Copy JSON** and paste the positions into `data.json`.

## Lesson Plan

### Learning Objective

The learner will locate the main sites of Ephesus on the site map and explain
how the city's layout, from the silted harbor to the hill of Ayasuluk, records
the order in which it was built, used, and abandoned.

### Grade Level

Adult travelers on the trip (Mediterranean Travels).

### Duration

15–20 minutes

### Prerequisites

The harbor silting, the Temple of Artemis, and the walk down Curetes Street,
from [Chapter 10](../../chapters/10-ephesus-greco-roman-city/index.md).

### Activities

1. **Exploration** (5 min): Hover over markers 1 to 12 in order. This is roughly
   the walk most groups take, downhill from the Magnesian Gate to the theater
   and Harbor Street.
2. **Guided Practice** (5 min): Find the two wall circuits (15 and 18) and the
   harbor (14). Explain why the later wall is so much shorter.
3. **Making Connections** (5 min): Find one place each that is Greek, Roman,
   Christian, and Turkish. Notice where each one sits on the map.
4. **Assessment** (5 min): Switch to Quiz mode and identify all 27 places.

### Assessment

The learner identifies at least 22 of the 27 places from their descriptions on
the first try and can explain why the later sites cluster on Ayasuluk rather
than in the old city.
