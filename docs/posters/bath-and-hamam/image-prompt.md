## Critical Rule

**This image must contain absolutely no text of any kind: no title, no heading,
no labels, no letters, no numbers, no digits, no arrows, no callout lines, no
scale bars, no compass, no signature and no watermark.** Signs, banners, scrolls,
tablets, and awnings must show only blank surfaces or soft abstract squiggles
that are clearly not writing. The room names are added later as an interactive
HTML overlay, and any text in the picture would clash with it.

- **Format**: PNG

- **Style**: a clean, flat editorial illustration with soft shading and crisp

## Overall Composition

Two wide panels, one above the other, each **1504 px wide and 488 px tall**. The
upper panel is a **Roman bath** and the lower panel is a **Turkish hamam**. Each
is a **floor plan seen from directly above, with the roof removed** (a cutaway),
drawn with thick wall bands, a thin soft shadow along the inside of each wall,
and tiny simplified people seen from above (head, shoulders, and a wrap or
towel, about 25 px across).

Both plans have **exactly the same outer footprint**: the same rectangle, the
same wall thickness, the same entrance on the left, the same service area on the
right. A viewer should be able to look down any vertical line through both plans
and compare what stands in the same place. The bather walks from left to right:
from the entrance, through cool, warm, and hot rooms, toward the furnace.

Both plans use the **same temperature colors**, so the eye can follow a gradient
across each panel: the entrance and cool rooms in pale blue-grey and cool stone,
the warm room in sand and ochre, the hot room in terracotta, and the service
area in charcoal with a soft ember-orange glow.

Both panels are divided into **five equal vertical columns**, each about 300 px
wide, numbered 1 to 5 below from the left. Column edges fall at about 1.0%,
20.6%, 40.2%, 59.8%, 79.4% and 99.0% of the image width. Stay inside the
column edges: nothing but the thin dividing walls may cross them (and in the
hamam, there is one wall missing, described below).

## What to Draw

### Upper panel (y about 1.6% to 49.2%): the Roman bath

**Surrounding color**: warm limestone (#E8DCC2). The top 12% of this panel is
plain flat limestone, with nothing in it (see Layout Notes). The plan fills the
rest of the panel, from y about 7.3% to 49.2% of the image height.

**Column 1, the changing room and garden court.** An arched entrance doorway in
the far left wall, at mid height. In the upper two thirds of the column, a
square room with stone benches along the walls and rows of small wall niches,
where three people in simple tunics are undressing or folding clothes. In the
lower third, an open court with two small trees and a low gravel path, and a
snack seller at a small stall under a blank striped awning, serving one person.

**Column 2, the cold room.** Pale blue-grey stone floor. A large rectangular
cold pool in the center, with calm flat water, and two bathers in towels sitting
on its edge. A wide arched doorway in the left wall and another in the right
wall line up at the same height as the entrance.

**Column 3, the warm room.** Sand and ochre floor. Wide marble benches along two
walls. Two bathers in towels sit and talk; a small oil jar and a stack of folded
cloths on a bench. Doorways line up with the others.

**Column 4, the hot room.** Terracotta floor. A raised rectangular hot basin
against the far wall, with a little steam rising as soft wispy curves, and
two bathers beside it. A thin soft orange glow runs *inside* the thick walls on
three sides, showing hot air in the walls.

**Column 5, the furnace.** A dark charcoal service room. In its center, an arched
furnace mouth glowing ember orange, with a stack of split firewood beside it
and a stoker in a plain tunic feeding it. The furnace is connected on its left
side to a narrow dark channel.

**The hypocaust (a cutaway strip).** Along the bottom quarter of the Roman plan,
across columns 3, 4, and 5, the floor is cut away like a lifted tile, showing the
space below it: rows of short squat stacks of square brick tiles holding up the
floor above, with a soft ember-orange glow flowing between them toward the
rooms, leaving the furnace at column 5. Keep this strip clearly marked by a
straight cut edge. This is the underfloor heating system.

### Lower panel (y about 50.8% to 98.4%): the Turkish hamam

**Surrounding color**: soft pale turquoise-cream (#DCE9E4). The top 12% of this
panel is plain flat turquoise-cream, with nothing in it. The plan fills the rest
of the panel, from y about 56.5% to 98.4% of the image height.

**Columns 1 and 2 together, the cool hall.** One **wide domed hall with no wall
between columns 1 and 2**. Its floor is pale blue-grey stone. The dome is
suggested by faint concentric rings on the floor, as if its shadow fell there.
Along the walls are raised wooden-and-cushion seats. An entrance doorway in the far
left wall at the same height as the Roman entrance. Five people sit and lie on
the seats, wrapped in thin cotton cloths, one of them holding a small
tulip-shaped glass of tea; a low round tray with two more tea glasses sits
between them. A row of closed small lockers stands along the top wall.

**Column 3, the warm room.** Sand and ochre floor, a modest square room with a
marble bench along one wall and two small niches with stone basins. Two bathers
in cloths sit quietly. A folded stack of cloths on the bench.

**Column 4, the hot steam room.** Terracotta floor with a faint pattern of
interlocking circles. At the exact center, a large round or eight-sided
**heated marble platform** (the "navel stone"), pale and glossy with a soft warm
glow, with two bathers lying on it in cloths. Beside it an attendant in a wrap
scrubs a bather's back with a rough mitt, with soft clouds of white soap foam
around them. Four small arched alcoves in the walls each hold a stone basin.
Soft wisps of steam.

**Column 5, the furnace area.** The same dark charcoal service room as above:
an arched furnace mouth glowing ember orange, a stack of firewood, a stoker. No
cutaway strip and no underfloor pillars in this panel.

### Between the two panels

Only the 16 px parchment gap. No connecting lines or arrows.

## Layout Notes (important for the interactive overlay)

- **Leave the top 12% of each panel (about 60 px) completely plain**: only the
  flat surrounding color of that panel, with nothing in it. Interactive name
  labels will be placed in this band, so no wall, dome, tree, or head may reach
  into it.
- The two plans must have the **same outer rectangle**. The only difference in
  the walls is the missing wall between columns 1 and 2 in the hamam.
- Keep the doorways of each plan at the same height, so a straight line from the
  entrance would pass through all the rooms.
- Keep every figure and object inside its own column and its own panel.
- Draw bathers small and simple, **everyone covered by a wrap, a towel, or a
  short tunic**. No nudity.
- Do not draw frames, plaques, or ribbons that look like they should hold text.
- Do not draw flags, crescents, crosses, or other national or religious symbols.
- Once more: **no text, letters, numbers, labels, or marks of any kind** anywhere
  in the image.
