## About This MicroSim

The Antikythera mechanism is the bronze machine we see at the National Archaeological Museum, an ancient prediction machine that could tell its owner the Moon's phase and the next eclipse. This picture opens it up: the crank, the gear train, the front dial with its zodiac and calendar rings, the back dials, and the corroded fragments that survive.

The picture is an orientation, not a reconstruction. Which dials and pointers survive, and which are reconstructed, is not settled by the chapter, so the drawing shows them complete.

## How to Use

**Explore mode** (the default): hover over a numbered marker or its label to
read about that part. The marker and its label light up together.

**Quiz mode:** press **Quiz**. The labels disappear and a description appears.
Click the marker that matches it. A correct answer scores a point; a wrong one
lets you try again. **Try Again** starts a new round.

**Edit mode:** add `?edit=true` to the address of `main.html` to drag the
markers, then press **Copy JSON** and paste the positions into `data.json`.

## Lesson Plan

### Learning Objective

The learner will name the main parts of the Antikythera mechanism, the crank, the gear train, and the front and back dials, and match each dial to the question an owner could ask of it, from the Moon's phase to the next eclipse.

### Grade Level

Adult travelers on the trip (Mediterranean Travels).

### Duration

10–15 minutes

### Prerequisites

The Antikythera mechanism and ancient prediction machines, from [Chapter 20](../../chapters/20-athens-museums-city-today/index.md).

### Activities

1. **Exploration** (5 min): Hover over the markers from 1 to 9 and read what
   each part does.
2. **Guided Practice** (5 min): For each dial, name the question an owner could
   ask of it.
3. **Assessment** (5 min): Switch to Quiz mode and identify all nine parts.

### Assessment

The learner identifies at least 8 of the 9 parts from their descriptions on the first try and can say which dial predicts eclipses.
