## Critical Rule

**This image must contain absolutely no text of any kind: no title, no heading,
no labels, no letters, no numbers, no digits, no arrows, no callout lines, no
scale bars, no signature and no watermark.** Carved blocks, inscriptions,
signs, and the sides of monuments must show only plain stone or soft abstract
squiggles that are clearly not writing: no hieroglyphs, no Greek letters, no
Arabic or Turkish calligraphy. The names and numbered markers are added later
as an interactive HTML overlay, and any text in the picture would clash with it.

## Image Specifications

- **Format**: PNG
- **Dimensions**: 1536 × 1024 px (landscape, 3:2)
- **Background**: the picture fills the whole canvas. Where the flat parchment
  cream (#F5EFE3) is wanted, it shows only as a plain 2% margin around the
  edge, with no texture, scenery, or vignette.
- **Style**: a clean, flat editorial illustration with soft shading and crisp
  outlines, the look of a good travel-history textbook. Not photorealistic.
  Warm morning light from the left. Suitable for an adult reader. Any people
  are tiny, simplified, and dignified, with no caricature.

## Overall Composition

One wide **side view (a flat elevation)** of the open square in Sultanahmet,
Istanbul, as it looks today, seen from its west side looking east. The ground
line is a straight horizontal at **76% down** the image, and the long paved
square runs the whole width of the picture. Along the old track line, at
the same camera height and the same flat view, stand five things in a row from
left (north) to right (south): a small fountain, three monuments, and, far
behind them on the right, a large mosque. The sky is pale morning blue with a
few thin clouds. Soft haze fades the distant skyline.

The three monuments are the point of the picture. They must read as three
different objects of three different materials and ages, standing apart from
each other with clear sky between them, so a marker can sit on each one.

## What to Draw

Positions are given as percent of the image width (x) and height (y), measured
from the top-left corner.

### 1. The paved square (the long ground)

**Position**: the band from 76% down to the bottom edge, full width.
**Visual**: pale warm paving stones, drawn flat and simple, with a faint darker
stripe running the full length along the middle of the band at about 93% down,
like the shadow of a long low spine. Nothing stands on the stripe. A few tiny
visitors, no more than 6% tall, walk on the paving at y 78 to 84, kept clear of
the monuments' feet: at x 14 to 20, x 36 to 39, x 58 to 62, and x 74 to 80.

### 2. The fountain (far left)

**Position**: centered at x = 9%, standing on the ground line, from y = 46% to
76%, about 13% wide.
**Visual**: a small **eight-sided** garden pavilion in pale stone, with slender
columns between the faces and a low rounded dome topped by a small finial. A
little warm gold on the dome. European-looking in its detail, with no
inscription band. Dark green trees rise softly behind it from x = 0 to 20% and
y = 32% to 70%.

### 3. The tall slender obelisk with its carved base

**Position**: centered at x = 27%. Stepped base from x = 22% to 32% and y = 67%
to 76%. Shaft from y = 67% up to a pointed tip at y = 8%.
**Visual**: a very tall, slender, four-sided stone shaft that tapers gently to a
small pointed pyramid-shaped top, in warm rose-tan stone. It is about 4% wide at
the base and 2.5% wide near the tip, with smooth faces and a few faint
squiggle-like carvings that are clearly not writing. It stands on a
**stepped marble base**: two plain steps, then a taller block in pale cream
marble, with a **carved panel on the front showing one simplified crowned figure in
relief, a portrait of an emperor**. No lettering. This base is the one carved
human figure in the picture.

### 4. The twisted bronze column in its pit

**Position**: centered at x = 48%. The column rises from the bottom of a pit at
y = 88% to a flat broken top at y = 50%, about 6% wide. The pit is cut into the
ground line from x = 40% to 56% and from y = 76% down to y = 88%.
**Visual**: a short column of **three bronze snakes twisted around each other**
in a tight rope-like spiral, dark green-brown bronze with warm gold highlights.
The top is flat and broken: **no heads**, just three cut ends where the heads
used to be. The pit is drawn in **cross-section**: a rectangular hollow with
plain stone-lined walls on the left and right and a stone floor, so the
viewer sees that the column's lower part stands below the surrounding ground. A
low stone rim rings the pit at ground level. This column is about **one third
the height** of the obelisk.

### 5. The rough-stone obelisk

**Position**: centered at x = 68%, standing on the ground line, rising from
y = 76% up to a flat top at y = 6%. About 6% wide at the base and 4% at the top.
**Visual**: a tall, four-sided tower **built of rough, mismatched stone blocks**,
in sandy grey and tan, tapering gently to a flat top. It is the tallest of
the three. The face is plain and weathered. Show **rows of small empty
rectangular holes and pale rectangular patches** across the upper two thirds,
where metal plates were once fixed and are now gone. No gold, no bronze, and no
lettering. Its base is a simple low plinth.

### 6. The great mosque (far background, right)

**Position**: x = 78% to 99%, rising from the ground line at 76% up to a
highest dome at y = 24%. It sits behind and beyond the monuments, slightly
hazier and paler than the foreground.
**Visual**: a **cascade of domes**: one large central dome, with half-domes and
smaller domes stepping down on each side, in soft blue-grey stone and lead
colors. **Six slender pencil-shaped minarets** with pointed caps rise around
it, two at the far right edge, two at the left of the group, and two behind the
central dome. Windows are plain pale arches with no pattern. Drawn with respect,
as a serious place of worship, with no figures on the roofs.

## Layout Notes (important for the interactive overlay)

- Numbered markers will sit at about these places, so each must be **clear and
  uncluttered**: the paving stripe at (30, 92); the fountain at (9, 58); the
  obelisk shaft at (27, 34); its carved base at (27, 72); the bronze column at
  (48, 56); the pit wall at (54, 82); the rough-stone shaft at (68, 30); the
  mosque domes at (89, 40). Keep **at least 6% of free space** between any two
  of these places.
- Keep the **top 3% of the image** plain sky, with no tips or clouds touching
  the edge.
- Do not put trees, people, signs, or clouds in front of any of the three
  monuments or the pit.
- Do not draw frames, plaques, banners, or ribbons that look like they should
  hold text.
- Keep the same line weight, shading, and light direction on every object.
- Once more: **no text, letters, numbers, hieroglyphs, labels, arrows, or marks
  of any kind** anywhere in the image.
