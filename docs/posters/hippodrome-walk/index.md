## About This MicroSim

Walking down the old Hippodrome square in Istanbul, we pass three monuments along the old track line: an Egyptian obelisk, a twisted bronze column, and a rough masonry obelisk. None was made for this place. This poster reads them as three borrowed layers, a picture of the word *spolia*, and asks of each where it came from, who set it up here, and what has happened to it since.

The heights and the north-to-south order in the picture are illustrative. The chapters list the monuments but give no distances, and say only that the base carries a portrait of Theodosius.

## How to Use

**Explore mode** (the default): hover over a numbered marker or its label to
read about that part. The marker and its label light up together.

**Quiz mode:** press **Quiz**. The labels disappear and a description appears.
Click the marker that matches it. A correct answer scores a point; a wrong one
lets you try again. **Try Again** starts a new round.

**Edit mode:** add `?edit=true` to the address of `main.html` to drag the
markers, then press **Copy JSON** and paste the positions into `data.json`.

## Lesson Plan

### Learning Objective

The learner will read the three monuments on the Hippodrome's old track line as three borrowed layers, and for each one state where it came from, who set it up here, and which way of adding a layer it shows: built over, reused, converted, or removed.

### Grade Level

Adult travelers on the trip (Mediterranean Travels).

### Duration

10–15 minutes

### Prerequisites

The Hippodrome, spolia, and Justinian, from [Chapter 4](../../chapters/04-byzantine-constantinople/index.md).

### Activities

1. **Exploration** (5 min): Hover over the markers from 1 to 8 and read what each part is.
2. **Guided Practice** (5 min): Name the three monuments in order and say, for each, where it came from.
3. **Assessment** (5 min): Switch to Quiz mode and identify all 8 parts.

### Assessment

The learner identifies at least 7 of the 8 parts from their descriptions on the first try and can give one example of a borrowed monument and who moved it.
