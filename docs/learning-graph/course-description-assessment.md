## Strengths

- **Specificity.** Named people, objects, scripture references, and dates give the generator concrete labels rather than vague topics.
- **Built-in dependencies.** The tables already pair a historical story with a modern concept, which maps directly onto learning-graph edges.
- **Source awareness.** The description repeatedly asks the guide to say which source a claim comes from (Acts vs. Paul's letters, Herodotus, Plato), which supports concepts like *Primary Source* and *Myth Versus History*.
- **Empathy and honesty.** The "where the parallel breaks down" and "caution" sections add evaluative concepts that most travel guides lack.

## Gaps and Under-Represented Areas

- **No explicit exclusions.** A short "What this guide does not cover" list (for example detailed military history, Byzantine theology, or sites off the route such as Pergamon) would earn the remaining points and keep chapters focused.
- **Rhodes' Italian period (1912–1947)** and **modern Crete** are only hinted at; they matter for understanding the 1944 deportation and the island's present.
- **Daily life today** is a single topic; it could name specific themes (economy, migration, family, faith) per country.
- **Istanbul's day-by-day split** across Days 1–5 is left open; naming a focus per day would sharpen chapter boundaries.

## Bloom's Taxonomy Suggestions

The outcomes already cover all six levels of the 2001 revised taxonomy. Two
small improvements:

- **Create** outcomes could name a final artifact for the closing chapter, such as a one-page "lessons from the route" summary that combines the journal, the leadership reflection, and the Delphi reflection.
