# Extensions and Excursions

Viking sells two kinds of add-ons to the *Ancient Mediterranean Treasures*
cruise. **Extensions** are hotel stays of two to four nights before the ship
leaves Istanbul or after it reaches Athens. **Shore excursions** are guided
outings from the ship in each port. This appendix lists both, as Viking showed
them for the 2026 Istanbul-to-Athens sailing, and notes which ones we took.

We booked neither extension. We spent three days on our own in Istanbul
before boarding and three days on our own in Athens after leaving the ship,
including the drive to Delphi. Comparing our own plan with Viking's packages
shows what the packages cover and what we covered by planning ourselves.

!!! note "Check before you book"
    These lists come from Viking's itinerary pages in November 2026. Viking
    changes its excursions, prices, and hotels from year to year, so confirm
    any option on the
    [Ancient Mediterranean Treasures](https://www.vikingcruises.com/oceans/cruise-destinations/western-mediterranean/ancient-mediterranean-treasures/index.html)
    page or in *My Viking Journey* before booking.

## How the Two Kinds of Add-On Work

| | Extensions | Shore excursions |
|---|---|---|
| When | Before embarkation or after disembarkation | During each port day |
| Length | 2–4 nights in a hotel | A few hours to a full day |
| What is included | Hotel, transfers, some meals, and one to three guided tours | A guide and transport; some include a meal or tasting |
| Cost | Extra, priced per extension | One excursion per port is included in the fare; the rest cost extra |
| Where to book | With the cruise booking, or later in *My Viking Journey* | In *My Viking Journey*, before the trip or on board |

The included excursion in each port is usually the classic introduction:
the Old Town walk in Rhodes, the main street of Ephesus, or the archaeological
museum in Heraklion. The optional excursions go farther away, go deeper into one
subject, or add food, wine, or a boat.

**Istanbul (2 nights)** is the short version. One guided day covers the Blue
Mosque, the Basilica Cistern, a Bosphorus cruise with a Turkish lunch, and a
carpet presentation in the Grand Bazaar. The first and last days are for
arriving and moving to the ship.

**Cappadocia (3 nights)** is the only extension that leaves the coast. After
one night in Istanbul, the group flies to central Anatolia and stays in a hotel
carved into the soft volcanic rock. The full day there covers the fairy
chimneys of the Dervent Valley, a pottery demonstration in Avanos, an
underground city, the Göreme Open Air Museum with its rock-cut churches, and
Uçhisar Castle. A dawn hot-air balloon ride is an optional extra. Viking notes
that the cave hotel is not recommended for guests who have trouble walking.

**Best of Istanbul (3 nights)** is the fully escorted version of what we did on
our own. It has three guided tours: the Sultanahmet monuments, a gourmet lunch
and the Grand Bazaar, and a day on the Asian shore with Beylerbeyi Palace and
the Spice Bazaar.

### What We Did Instead

Our three days in Istanbul before boarding covered most of the Best of
Istanbul ground at our own pace:

- **Day 1**, [Istanbul, a City on Two Continents](../chapters/03-istanbul-two-continents/index.md): the Bosphorus, the Golden Horn, and the city's layout.
- **Day 2**, [Byzantine Constantinople and Hagia Sophia](../chapters/04-byzantine-constantinople/index.md): Hagia Sophia, the Basilica Cistern, and the Hippodrome.
- **Day 3**, [Ottoman Istanbul, Topkapı, and Friday Prayer](../chapters/05-ottoman-istanbul/index.md): Topkapı Palace and the Süleymaniye and Blue Mosques.

The one thing none of our days covered is Cappadocia, which is a flight away.
It is the strongest case for an extension on this route, because it cannot be
done as a day trip from the ship.

### Our Excursion: The Villages of Crete

Crete was the one port where we chose a village day over the famous ruins. The
excursion drove inland from Heraklion to two stops. The first was a vineyard
and winery at Avli, with a tasting of Cretan wines. The second was
**Thrapsano**, a village known across Greece for its potters, who throw the
tall storage jars called *pithoi*.

Those jars connect the village day back to Knossos. The storerooms of the
Minoan palace held rows of *pithoi* almost as tall as a person, used for olive
oil, wine, and grain about 3,500 years ago. Thrapsano's potters still make jars
in the same shape for the same purposes, and for gardens. A village workshop
shows how the craft moved from palace to farm to the present day, something a
museum case cannot show.

The village day also fits the book's habit of asking who lives in a place now.
Heraklion's Old Town shows Crete's history. Thrapsano and the vineyards show
how many Cretans make a living today: in farming, wine, and crafts, most of it
far from the cruise port.

### Our Excursion: The Temple of Poseidon and the Acropolis

We took this excursion on **Friday, November 20, 2026**, the day the ship
reached Piraeus. The location data on our photos shows the order was the
reverse of Viking's listing. We were at the Temple of Poseidon on Cape Sounion
from about 9:30 to 10:30 in the morning, were back in central Athens by noon,

Delphi is offered from the ship as a single long day. We saved it for a

**Athens (2 nights)** is mostly a hotel stay with a Viking host, two
breakfasts, and transfers. Sightseeing is on your own, with optional guided
outings to Cape Sounion, the Agora, and the Plaka.

**Classic Greece (4 nights)** is the guided tour of the mainland's great
sites: a day for the Acropolis and its museum, a day for Mycenae and Epidaurus
in the Peloponnese, and a day for Delphi, with lunch in the mountain village of
Arachova.

**Greek Traditions (3 nights)** moves to Nafplio, the first capital of modern
Greece, and centers on food and farming: an olive oil mill, an apiary, a
winery, a cooking class, and the ruins of the Temple of Zeus at Nemea.

## Choosing Well in November

- **Short days.** Daylight ends before 5:30 PM in Athens in late November, and
  many sites close earlier in winter. The longest excursions (Delphi from
  Piraeus, Gallipoli, Lindos, Phaistos) leave little slack.
- **Weather.** Open-air sites like Troy, Ephesus, Lindos, and Delphi have
  little shelter. Bring a rain layer and shoes with grip for wet marble.
- **Walking.** Lindos (195 steps), the Acropolis, and Assos involve steep or
  uneven climbs. The museum excursions are the gentle choice in each port.
- **One deep day beats two shallow ones.** In a port with two famous sites,
  such as Troy and Gallipoli, or Knossos and Heraklion's museum, choose the one
  you most want to understand and read that chapter the night before.
