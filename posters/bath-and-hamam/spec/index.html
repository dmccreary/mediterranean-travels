    Spec: Roman Bath and Turkish Hamam¶

    Status: spec and draft data only. No image has been generated, and there is no index.md or main.html yet, so this poster is not in the nav.

    Learning Objective¶

    The learner will compare the rooms of a Roman bath with the rooms of a Turkish hamam, and for each of five stages state what the two share, where the match breaks, and why the hamam is best described as adapted from the Roman and Byzantine baths, not copied from them.

    Why This Poster¶

    Chapter 6 covers the bath in two short sections, Roman Bath Culture and The Turkish Bath, and the reader has to hold the Roman sequence in mind while reading the Turkish one. A visitor may well book a hamam on Day 4, so this is also a practical poster: it shows what the rooms are, what is worn, and what happens on the heated stone. Two plans on the same footprint, one above the other, let the eye run down a column and see the pair at once. The modest claim the chapter makes, "descended from the Roman and Byzantine baths but adapted", is the thread of every zone.

    Layout Decision¶

    The first sketch in Poster Ideas allowed "two cutaway panels stacked or side by side". This spec uses two stacked floor plans (Roman above, hamam below), each a wide strip with the roof removed, with the rooms in the order a bather walks through them, left to right. Stacked strips make the five columns line up, so a zone is one column through both plans and one hover compares the pair. This is the same idea as the diptych tiles in Oracle and Algorithm, turned on its side. Image generators keep two wide strips aligned far better than two squarish plans.

    Column Roman bath Hamam Zone id
    1 Changing room, with a garden court beside it Cool domed hall, first half changing
    2 Cold room (frigidarium) Cool domed hall, second half cold
    3 Warm room (tepidarium) Warm room warm
    4 Hot room (caldarium) Hot steam room with the göbek taşı hot
    5 Furnace, with the hypocaust shown under the floors Furnace area (generic) heat

    The hamam has three main rooms in the chapter and the Roman bath has four, so in the lower plan the cool hall is drawn as one wide room across columns 1 and 2, with no wall between them. That is deliberate: it shows the first place where the parallel breaks. The poster says so in the changing and cold zones.

    Tone Rules¶

    1. Keep the continuity claim modest. Say "descended from" and "adapted to", as the chapter does. Never "the hamam is a Roman bath" and never "a direct copy". No zone says which people invented which room.
    2. Say where each pair breaks. Every zone has a "Where the parallel breaks" fact or a fact that makes the break plain: three rooms against four, a shared pool against flowing water, a hypocaust against a heated stone.
    3. Everyone is covered. Bathers in both plans wear a wrap, a towel, or a short tunic. No nudity. Drawn from above they are small, simplified, and dignified.
    4. Bathing is ordinary and respectful. The hamam is a living practice and a place visitors can go. Etiquette appears as practical facts only (the cloth, the locker, the booking), with no gender jokes and no orientalizing.
    5. No ladder. The Roman plan is not the "early version" of the hamam. They are two designs for related habits.

    Quiz¶

    Nine questions are drafted in data.json, covering all five stages. They ask about ideas and features (which room matches the caldarium, which stage had the hypocaust, why the hamam is "adapted") and not about what is drawn. Two of them (the cold room, the "adapted" idea) depend on understanding where the parallel breaks, not on matching names.

    To Verify Before Approval¶

    The chapter does not give these, so none of them are in data.json.

    1. How a hamam is heated. The chapter names the heated göbek taşı but does not describe a furnace, flues, or an underfloor system for a hamam. The image shows a generic furnace area in column 5, which may overstate a link to the Roman system. Check a source on Ottoman hamam heating before approval, or redraw column 5 with only a service room.
    2. Turkish room names. The usual Turkish names for the three rooms (the cool hall, the warm room, the hot room) are not in the chapter. Add them only after checking a source.
    3. A cold plunge pool in the Roman cold room. The chapter only says that Islamic ideas of cleanliness prefer flowing water to "sitting in a shared pool". The prompt draws a pool in the Roman cold room to match that line. Confirm that a pool in the frigidarium is a fair picture.
    4. What the warm room was for. In both baths, the chapter names the warm room but gives it no job of its own, and it does not say in which room the scrubbing and soaping took place. The prompt puts that scene in the hot room, around the stone. Check where it happened.
    5. The Roman changing room's Latin name (apodyterium) and whether the garden court beside it matches the chapter's "exercise courts, gardens".
    6. Dates and sizes for the Baths of Zeuxippus beyond what the chapter gives (beside the Hippodrome, decorated by Constantine, burned in 532), and which hamams by Sinan are open. The poster gives none of them.

    Open Questions for the Author¶

    1. Two zones per column? One zone per column compares the pair on one hover. If you want the Roman and the hamam halves clickable on their own, split each column at the gap between the plans (ten zones). The facts would need to be split too, and chip labels like "Roman: Hot Room" fit in 300 px.
    2. How much of the Roman bath? The chapter mentions exercise courts, gardens, libraries, and snack sellers. The prompt shows a garden court and a snack stall in column 1 only, to keep the footprints equal. Add a library and a courtyard if you want the Roman plan to look larger than the hamam's.
    3. Which hamam? The poster draws a generic hamam. The chapter says two historic hamams by Sinan are open to visitors. If you visited one, a plan of that bath would be more personal, but then the poster is about one building.
    4. Canvas. If your image tool can produce 1792 x 1024, the columns would be about 350 px wide and the figures larger. If you switch, tell me and I will recompute the zones and the prompt.
