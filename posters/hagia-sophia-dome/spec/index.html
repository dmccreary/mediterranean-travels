    Spec: Inside the Hagia Sophia Dome¶

    Status: spec and draft data only. No image has been generated, and there is no index.md or main.html yet, so this poster is not in the nav.

    Learning Objective¶

    The learner will name the parts that hold up the Hagia Sophia dome, say what each one does, and tell which parts are Justinian's, which came after the collapse of 558, and which are later Ottoman additions.

    Why This Poster¶

    Chapter 4 tells the dome story across three places: the dome section in Part Four, the exterior-reading worked example in Part Two, and the restoration section. A visitor on Day 2 will stand under the dome for a few minutes and wonder what is holding it up, and Chapter 5 then compares it with Sinan's Süleymaniye the next day. A cross-section shows the load path from the ring of windows down through the pendentives and piers to the buttresses, and puts the 558 collapse and the Ottoman minarets in the same picture.

    Layout Decision¶

    One cross-section, call-out engine. Numbered markers with leader lines, from the supports upward and then outward: piers, pendentives, window ring, dome, half-domes, buttresses, the collapse and rebuild, and the minarets. It is the companion to the idea "Hagia Sophia: One Building, Four Lives", which is not built yet: that poster would answer "what happened to it", and this one answers "what is it". East is on the right, as the chapter's "to the east and west, two half-domes" suggests.

    Marker positions in data.json are estimates from the positions stated in image-prompt.md. They must be calibrated against the real image with main.html?edit=true. Markers are at least 8 percentage points apart.

    Sources¶

    All facts come from Chapter 4 and from the comparison in Chapter 5, "Ottoman Mosque Architecture" and "Mimar Sinan". Chapter 4 states the dimensions (about 31 m across, about 55 m high), the ring of 40 windows, and the dates of the collapse and rebuild (558 and 562), so these are in data.json and not under "To Verify". The two Isidores, the earthquakes of 553, 557, 989 and 1346, and the Fossati restoration of 1847 to 1849 are also Chapter 4's.

    Tone Rules¶

    1. Hagia Sophia is a working mosque. The minaret marker says so, once, and says who uses it and what it means to them is where contested history lives, as Chapter 4's own method does. No wording that takes a side on its status.
    2. No hero-worship of Justinian. Procopius wrote for the emperor, and the window marker says to read his "golden chain" with that in mind.
    3. No "ladder." The Süleymaniye is compared, not ranked: narrower, steeper, with hidden buttresses, a different answer to the same problem.
    4. The faint ghost arc is a drawing aid. The chapter says only that the first dome was shallower, and marker 7's tip says so.

    Quiz¶

    Ten questions are drafted here. The call-out engine builds its own quiz mode from each marker's hint, and its data.json has no quiz[] field, so these are for the author's review and for any companion page.

    # Question Answer Explanation
    1 Which element turns a square room into a round base for a dome? 2, pendentives A curved triangle of masonry fills each corner between two arches, so the dome sits on the ring they make.
    2 Which element makes the dome seem to float, and what did Procopius call the effect? 3, window ring Its 40 windows flood the base with light and hide the supports; he wrote of a golden chain.
    3 Which elements carry the four great arches? 1, piers Four arches rise from four piers, and everything above rests on them.
    4 Which marker is about 31 m across and about 55 m high? 4, central dome For its time it was a daring piece of engineering.
    5 Which elements extend the space east and west into one continuous room? 5, half-domes Two half-domes open the central space.
    6 Which element stops the walls spreading under the dome's weight? 6, buttresses Every builder after Justinian has held the walls in, and Sinan added more in the 1570s.
    7 When did the first dome fall, and why was the new one built higher? 7, collapse and rebuild Its eastern part fell in May 558; the rebuilt dome was steeper and pushed outward less.
    8 Which parts show Hagia Sophia's Ottoman layer outside? 8, minarets Four, each built by a different sultan; two of them by Sinan in the 1570s.
    9 What did Sinan's later domes learn from the rebuilt dome? 7 A steeper curve pushes outward less.
    10 Which marker shows a source we should read with its purpose in mind? 3, window ring Procopius wrote his praise for Justinian.

    Open Questions for the Author¶

    1. How much of the Süleymaniye comparison belongs here? Marker 4 gives the Süleymaniye's dome size and marker 1 its slimmed piers. Keep them, or leave the comparison to the mosque poster and Chapter 5?
    2. Ghost arc. The faint outline of the first dome helps marker 7 but may confuse a quiz question. If the generator makes it heavy, remove it from the prompt and keep the collapse in the marker text alone.
    3. Companion sims. The lesson page should link Read the Ruin - Which Layer? and Source, Myth, or Contested?. The companion poster is Anatomy of an Ottoman Mosque.

    To Verify Before Approval¶

    • The rededication date. Chapter 4 says the rebuilt dome was dedicated in 562, while its sources table dates Paul the Silentiary's poem to 563 "at the rededication." Check which year the chapter means before the page is built.
    • Which buttresses are Byzantine and which are Sinan's. The chapter says both built them but does not say which, so the picture draws one plain type.
    • Details the picture invents: the shape and height of the first dome (the chapter says shallower, and the rebuild about 6 to 7 m higher), the number of arches under the half-domes, and the relative size of the piers. None is a claim.
    • Current visitor arrangements. Chapter 4 says the ground floor is kept for worshippers and visitors take a gallery route since January 2024. Check on the day.
