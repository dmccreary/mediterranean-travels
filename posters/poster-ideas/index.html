    Poster Ideas¶

    This page is a planning list, not a finished poster. Each idea below is a candidate for an interactive overlay infographic like The Three Classical Orders: one illustration with an interactive layer on top. The ideas favor two kinds of picture, because those are the ones an overlay improves most.

    • Side-by-side comparisons (grid engine): two to five panels drawn at the same scale, with a hover zone and a detail panel for each. The reader learns by looking back and forth.
    • Annotated illustrations (call-out engine): one picture with numbered markers and leader lines that point at parts, in the order a visitor would meet them.

    Every poster also gets Quiz mode for free, so each idea lists one sample question.

    Ground Rules for Every Poster¶

    1. No text in the image. Titles, labels, and numbers live in data.json and are drawn at runtime. This keeps facts editable and checkable.
    2. Same scale, same viewpoint in comparison panels. Differences should come from the subject, not from the artist's framing.
    3. Every claim in a zone carries a "where we see it on our route" line, as the Orders poster does. A poster that does not connect to a stop on the trip has not earned its place.
    4. Dates and numbers marked "verify" below must be checked against a cited source before they go into data.json. Treat the figures in this list as leads, not facts.
    5. No image is generated until the author approves the idea and its image prompt. Each poster starts with a short image-prompt.md, as the Orders poster did.

    Recommended First Four¶

    If only a few get built, these give the most for the effort. They cover the book's three threads: the cities of layers, the Oracle and the Algorithm, and Learning for All.

    Rank Idea Why first
    1 The Oracle and the Algorithm, Side by Side The book's central comparison, and no existing sim shows it as one picture
    2 Five Classrooms Across Three Thousand Years The Learning for All thread made visible in one view
    3 Hagia Sophia: One Building, Four Lives The clearest "city of layers" subject; a strong photo-realistic illustration is easy to describe
    4 The Acropolis, Then and Now Nearly everyone on the trip will stand there; orientation is practical

    Where Each Idea Stands¶

    Side-by-Side Comparisons¶

    1. The Oracle and the Algorithm, Side by Side¶
    Chapters Chapter 17, Chapter 18, Chapter 19
    Engine Grid, six tiles in a 3 × 2 grid, one zone per matched pair
    The picture Six small diptychs. In each, Delphi is on the left and today's algorithm is on the right, drawn from the same camera height.
    Status Built: the poster and its spec.

    Zones (each zone is one whole tile, so one hover compares the pair):

    1. Who asks: a petitioner with a yes-or-no choice, and a user typing a prompt.
    2. Where the knowledge came from: the sanctuary as a hub of traders' and envoys' reports, and training data.
    3. The answer's form: an ambiguous verse, and a probability or a fluent paragraph.
    4. Who interprets: the priests, and the human in the loop.
    5. What goes wrong: motivated reading, and confirmation bias and data bias.
    6. Feedback: a prophecy that shapes the decision, and a prediction that changes the thing predicted.

    Sample quiz question: "Which row is the best match for the training data of a model?" (Answer: where the knowledge came from.)

    Watch-outs. The point is the parallel, not a claim that the Pythia was secretly a data center. Each zone should name where the parallel breaks. Related sims: oracle-gears-or-data-sorter, oracle-answer-hedging-test, prediction-feedback-loops.

    2. Five Classrooms Across Three Thousand Years¶
    Chapters Chapter 2, Chapter 12, Chapter 14, Chapter 16, Chapter 20
    Engine Grid, five equal-width panels
    The picture Five classrooms in a row, drawn from the same camera height, in date order: a philosopher walking with students at the Lyceum, a rhetoric lesson on Rhodes, an Aldine print shop with a bound octavo book, a medrese courtyard beside a mosque, and one adult tutoring one child with a tablet.
    Status Built: the poster and its spec.

    Each zone follows the book's own four-step pattern from Chapter 2: locked up, opened, resisted, and widened but never all the way. The last step is the thread's question, "who was left outside?"

    Sample quiz question: "Which classroom made one-to-one attention cheap enough for every child?" (Leads into the tutoring-effect sim.)

    Watch-outs. Do not draw the five as steps on a ladder of progress. Several widened access while others narrowed it again. Needs care with the Ottoman panel: the medrese and külliye were charitable foundations, which the suleymaniye-kulliye-sorter sim already covers. Related sims: tutoring-effect-size-explorer, world-literacy-over-time.

    3. Hagia Sophia: One Building, Four Lives¶
    Chapter Chapter 4
    Engine Grid, four panels in two rows, plus an optional shared-features zone
    The picture The same interior view of the nave, drawn four times: as Justinian's cathedral (dedicated 537 CE), as an Ottoman mosque after 1453, as a museum (1935), and as a mosque again (2020). Same dome and same camera.

    Zones. One per life, each listing what was added, covered, or revealed. A fifth zone lists what never changed: the dome, the pendentives, the ring of windows.

    Sample quiz question: "In which life do the large round calligraphic medallions first appear on the walls?"

    Watch-outs. This is a living religious site and a politically contested one. Use neutral, factual wording, and keep the "who decided and why" in a labeled contested history note rather than in the zone summary. Verify the dates and the sequence of cover-ups and uncoverings. Related sims: read-the-ruin-layers, place-the-site-in-its-era.

    4. The Acropolis, Then and Now¶
    Chapter Chapter 15
    Engine Grid, two panels, plus a matching set of call-outs inside each panel
    The picture One aerial view of the rock from the northwest, drawn twice: in the late 5th century BCE with paint and bronze, and today with scaffolding, the Parthenon's missing roof, and tourists.

    Call-outs in each panel: the Propylaea gateway, the temple of Athena Nike, the Parthenon, the Erechtheion with its Caryatid porch, and the path up.

    Sample quiz question: "Which monument stands on the bastion at the right of the entrance?"

    Watch-outs. The "then" image must avoid overconfident color. Mark painted details as reconstruction in the zone text. Related sims: parthenon-five-lives-timeline, parthenon-marbles-fact-or-position.

    5. A Scribe's Page and an Aldine Page¶
    Chapters Chapter 6, Chapter 14
    Engine Grid, two facing pages, zones for each feature
    The picture Two open books at the same size on a desk. Left: a hand-copied Greek manuscript with a wide margin and decorated initial. Right: a small Venetian octavo in Aldus Manutius's italic type.

    Zones: the writing surface, the hand or type, the layout, who made it, how long it took, and who could afford it.

    Sample quiz question: "Which feature let a scholar carry a classic in a pocket?"

    Watch-outs. The time and cost figures need sources before they appear. Greek in the image must be avoided, since the generator will garble it. Draw blurred lines of text instead. Related sims: scribe-or-press, folio-quarto-octavo-paper-calculator, greek-book-makers-venice-network, when-printing-arrived.

    6. Roman Bath and Turkish Hamam¶
    Chapter Chapter 6
    Engine Grid, two cutaway panels stacked or side by side
    The picture Two cutaway floor plans drawn from above, the same footprint. Left, a Roman bath with its changing room, cold, warm, and hot rooms and the underfloor hypocaust. Right, a Turkish hamam with its entrance hall, cool, warm, and hot rooms and the heated central marble stone.

    Zones: the entrance room, the cold room, the warm room, the hot room, the heat source, and the social function of each.

    Sample quiz question: "Which room in a hamam does the same job as the Roman caldarium?"

    Watch-outs. Keep this practical. A visitor may well go to a hamam, so the overlay can include etiquette in one zone. Keep the continuity claim modest: the hamam developed from Roman and Byzantine bath culture, but it is not a copy.

    7. One Table, Two Names¶
    Chapters Chapter 6, Chapter 20
    Engine Grid, a mirrored table with matched dish pairs
    The picture A top-down view of a long shared table. The left half holds a Turkish meze spread, the right half a Greek one, with the shared dishes aligned across the middle: stuffed vine leaves, a yogurt-and-cucumber dip, a layered honey pastry, a thick strong coffee, and an anise spirit.

    Zones: one per pair, each with both names, the shared roots, and the differences in how each country makes it.

    Sample quiz question: "Which pair shares a name for the same coffee-making method?"

    Watch-outs. This is an empathy poster. Do not argue about who invented what. State that the dishes grew out of a shared Ottoman and Byzantine past, and that each country calls its own version its own. Cross-link the food-history-layers-sorter sim, and point to the chapter's own notes on the population exchange of 1923.

    8. The Colossus: Popular Picture and Likely Truth¶
    Chapter Chapter 12
    Engine Grid, two panels, "What most people imagine" and "What the evidence supports"
    The picture Left, a giant bronze figure straddling the harbor mouth with ships passing between its legs. Right, a standing figure on a stone base beside the harbor, with scaffolding to show how it was built.

    Zones: pose, location, height (verify), how it was built, how it fell (verify), and what became of the bronze.

    Sample quiz question: "Which detail of the popular image has no good evidence behind it?"

    Watch-outs. This one teaches the book's myth versus history habit. The zone text must say clearly that no source describes the straddling pose, and that the likely appearance is reconstructed. Verify the construction and collapse dates before they appear.

    Annotated Illustrations With Call-Outs¶

    9. Anatomy of a Mosque¶
    Chapter Chapter 5
    Engine Call-out, numbered markers with leader lines
    The picture A cutaway view of a classic Ottoman mosque and its forecourt: the dome and semi-domes, the minaret, the courtyard with its ablution fountain, the entry, the mihrab niche, the minbar pulpit, and the women's gallery or rear section.

    Order of markers: follow a worshipper's path from the outer gate, to the fountain, to the shoe racks, into the hall, then toward the qibla wall.

    Sample quiz question: "Which marker shows the direction of prayer?"

    Watch-outs. Write it with respect and for practical use, since we plan to be in Istanbul on a Friday. Include dress and shoe etiquette in the relevant zones. Related sims: day-3-prayer-time-planner, suleymaniye-kulliye-sorter.

    10. Delphi: Climbing the Sacred Way¶
    Chapter Chapter 18
    Engine Call-out
    The picture The sanctuary as one hillside cutaway, drawn from the lower slope up to the stadium: the Castalian Spring, the Athena Pronaia sanctuary and its round tholos below the road, the Sacred Way, treasuries, the Athenian treasury, the polygonal wall, the Temple of Apollo, the theater, and the stadium.

    Order of markers: the order we will climb. Each call-out names what a petitioner did there and what we see today.

    Sample quiz question: "Where would a petitioner have washed before entering the sanctuary?"

    Watch-outs. Keep the geography honest, since the real site is steep and the buildings are scattered. Related sims: delphi-consultation-steps, serpent-column-two-ends, delphi-oracle-strength-timeline.

    11. The Library of Celsus Facade¶
    Chapter Chapter 10
    Engine Call-out
    The picture A straight-on view of the two-story facade at Ephesus, with steps, paired columns, niches, and statues, shown before the earthquake damage and then, in a second layer, as reassembled.

    Call-outs: the steps and entry doors, the niches and the statues of virtues (verify the names), the ground-floor and upper-floor column orders, the burial of Celsus beneath the building, and what is reconstruction.

    Sample quiz question: "Which order of column appears on the lower story?"

    Watch-outs. This links to the classical-orders-identifier sim, so reuse its wording. Mark clearly which parts were reassembled in the 20th century. It also fits the Learning for All thread: this was a public library, built as a memorial. Verify the dates.

    12. Inside the Hagia Sophia Dome¶
    Chapter Chapter 4
    Engine Call-out on a cross-section
    The picture A clean architectural cross-section: the central dome, the ring of windows at its base, the pendentives carrying it onto four piers, the half-domes, the buttresses, and the minarets added later.

    Call-outs: the ring of windows, the pendentives, the piers, the half-domes, the buttresses, the earlier collapse and rebuild of the dome (verify), and the Ottoman additions outside.

    Sample quiz question: "Which element turns a square room into a round base for a dome?"

    Watch-outs. Companion to idea 3 rather than a replacement. Together they answer "what is it" and "what happened to it". Verify the dimensions.

    13. Walk Down the Hippodrome¶
    Chapter Chapter 4
    Engine Call-out
    The picture A side view of the open square as it looks today, with the three surviving monuments along the old track line: the Egyptian obelisk, the twisted bronze column, and the rough masonry obelisk.

    Call-outs: where each monument came from, who moved it, what it was originally for, and what was lost. This is spolia made visible: three eras of borrowed power in one row.

    Sample quiz question: "Which monument was first set up at Delphi?"

    Watch-outs. Related sims: serpent-column-two-ends, read-the-ruin-layers. Do not duplicate the serpent-column sim's content; link to it.

    14. A Trireme at Salamis¶
    Chapter Chapter 15
    Engine Call-out
    The picture A cutaway of an Athenian trireme from the side, with the three levels of oar benches, the ram at the waterline, the deck, and the steering oars.

    Call-outs: the three oar levels, the ram, the deck, the crew by role, and what crew size meant for who could vote. Citizens of modest means rowed the ships that saved Athens, and that history shaped the democracy.

    Sample quiz question: "Which part of the ship did the actual damage in a fight?"

    Watch-outs. The link to democracy is the interesting part, and it should lead the first call-out. Related sims: persian-wars-campaign-map, athens-who-could-vote.

    15. The Antikythera Mechanism, Opened Up¶
    Chapter Chapter 20
    Engine Call-out on an exploded view
    The picture A reconstruction of the front and back, with the case open: the front dial, the two back spirals, the crank, and the stacked gears.

    Call-outs: the crank, the main drive wheel, the sun and moon pointers, the calendar and eclipse dials, and the surviving fragments versus the reconstructed parts.

    Sample quiz question: "Which dial predicts eclipses?"

    Watch-outs. This is an "ancient prediction machine", and it ties directly to idea 1. The antikythera-gears sim already covers the gears. The poster's job is orientation: what the object looked like and which parts are certain. Verify the number of fragments, the date range, and the dials.

    Other Candidates (Not Yet Developed)¶

    • Topkapı Palace plan: the four courtyards as an annotated plan, in the order we walk them.
    • Agora of Athens: the stoa, the temple of Hephaestus, the council house, and the Areopagus hill, as one oblique view.
    • Venetian Candia: a bird's-eye view of the walls and bastions, with the Morosini fountain and the Koules fortress.
    • Knossos: What Survives and What Evans Rebuilt: the throne room, drawn twice. This overlaps the knossos-minoan-or-evans sim, so build it only if the sim's quiz does not already cover the idea well.

    Suggested Build Order¶

    1. Pick one poster. Write a one-paragraph spec and an image-prompt.md.
    2. After the author approves the prompt, generate the image.
    3. Draw zones with ?edit=true, then fill in data.json.
    4. Add facts with sources, then write the quiz questions.
    5. Create index.md with status: scaffold, then built. Only the author sets approved.
    6. Add the poster to the table in List of Posters and to nav: in mkdocs.yml.
