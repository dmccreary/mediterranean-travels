    Spec: Delphi, Climbing the Sacred Way¶

    Status: built (2026-11-25). The image is generated, the marker positions are calibrated to it, and the page is in the nav at Delphi: Climbing the Sacred Way. It is built, not approved: the author approves it after trying the controls.

    Learning Objective¶

    The learner will name nine places on the Delphi hillside in the order a visitor climbs them, say what a petitioner or an athlete did at each, and say what survives to see today.

    Why This Poster¶

    Chapter 18 is a walk, and Chapter 17 is the explanation of what the walk was for. A reader on Day 12 will have about an hour on a steep slope with ruins scattered up it, and a list of names does not tell them which stone is which or what is above what. One elevation shows the whole climb at once, with the Sacred Way as a visible zigzag, and each marker names what was done there and what is left.

    Layout Decision¶

    One hillside elevation, call-out engine. Numbered markers with leader lines, in the order of the chapter's walk: Castalian Spring, Sacred Way, treasuries, Athenian Treasury, polygonal wall, the temple terrace, theater, stadium, then down to Athena Pronaia and its Tholos. The picture is stylized and not to scale, but the relative order is the one the chapters give, and the prompt fixes it:

    • The sanctuary gate is at the southeast corner; the Sacred Way runs west along the lowest terrace, turns at the Athenian Treasury, climbs east along the polygonal wall, and turns up to the temple (Chapter 18, The Sacred Way).
    • The stadium is the highest feature, above the theater and outside the wall (Chapter 18, The Stadium; Chapter 17, The Sanctuary of Apollo).
    • The Castalian Spring is just east of the sanctuary; Athena Pronaia is a short walk further east, below the modern road (Chapter 18).

    East is therefore on the right, as if looking north up the slope from the valley.

    Marker positions in data.json are estimates from the positions stated in image-prompt.md. They must be calibrated against the real image with main.html?edit=true. Markers are at least 8 percentage points apart.

    Sources¶

    All facts in data.json come from Chapter 18 (Parts One to Four) and Chapter 17 (The Sanctuary of Apollo, The Temple of Apollo at Delphi, The Pythia, The Oracle Consultation). The drawing's only inventions are details of how a building looks, not claims about it.

    Tone Rules¶

    1. Say what is standing and what is not. Only the Athenian Treasury stands, rebuilt in 1903 to 1906. The temple is foundations and six re-erected columns. The markers say so.
    2. Keep the uncertain parts uncertain. The Athenian Treasury is "possibly" built from the spoils of Marathon. The Tholos has no known purpose. The omphalos is a copy and the original is lost.
    3. Treat the oracle as a living faith of its own time, as Chapter 17 does. No "primitive superstition" wording, and no foaming Pythia: the prompt draws no Pythia at all.
    4. No ladder, no ranking of sites. The poster does not say Delphi was better or more important than the other places on the trip.

    Quiz¶

    Ten questions are drafted here. The call-out engine builds its own quiz mode from each marker's hint, and its data.json has no quiz[] field, so these are for the author's review and for any companion page.

    # Question Answer Explanation
    1 Where would a petitioner have washed before entering the sanctuary? 1, Castalian Spring Nobody approached Apollo unwashed; purifying with its water was the second step of a consultation.
    2 Which road did rival cities line with monuments facing each other? 2, Sacred Way Pilgrims walked between the claims, as Pausanias described monument by monument.
    3 Which buildings held gifts, but were mainly there to carry a city's name? 3, Treasuries A treasury was not a bank; it displayed a city's wealth and piety.
    4 Which building stands today because archaeologists rebuilt it from its original blocks? 4, Athenian Treasury French archaeologists rebuilt it between 1903 and 1906, with money from Athens.
    5 Which wall is fitted without mortar, survived the earthquake of 373 BCE, and carries records of freedom? 5, Polygonal Wall The interlocking blocks resist earthquakes; the smooth face became the sanctuary's archive.
    6 Where was the goat sacrificed before a consultation? 6, temple terrace On the great altar before the temple's east front.
    7 Where were the musical contests of the Pythian Games held? 7, Theater Music came first in the program; the athletics followed higher up.
    8 Which structure is the highest, and lies outside the sanctuary wall? 8, Stadium It is about 178 m long, and in winter the path up is not always open.
    9 Which shrine was the first a pilgrim reached on the road from the east? 9, Athena Pronaia Its name means "before the temple."
    10 Which building is one of the most admired in Greece, though nobody knows why it was built? 9, Tholos No ancient writer says what it was for.

    Open Questions for the Author¶

    1. Scale and honesty. The picture squeezes the slope and enlarges buildings. The relative order is right. If you would rather add a small sidebar ("about 100 m of climb from entrance to stadium", from Chapter 17) on the lesson page, say so.
    2. Marker order. The numbering follows our climb and puts Athena Pronaia last, as Chapter 18 does. A pilgrim arriving from the east would have met Pronaia first. Keep the chapter's order, or renumber to the pilgrim's?
    3. Left or right. East on the right is what the chapter's directions give when viewed from the valley. A generator may mirror the picture; if so, the markers need only be moved, but the compass note in the lesson should be edited.
    4. Companion sims. The lesson page should link A Day at the Oracle, Two Ends of the Serpent Column, Read a Freedom Record on the Polygonal Wall, and Eleven Centuries of the Oracle.

    To Verify Before Approval¶

    • The real relative positions and heights of the terraces. The chapters give the order, not the distances, and the prompt only places the features by order.
    • Where the Siphnian Treasury stood on the Sacred Way. The chapters name it but do not locate it, so marker 3 is a general marker for the row.
    • Where the Stoa of the Athenians and the Castalian Spring's older fountain stand in relation to the road. The prompt draws them as the chapters word them.
    • The current state of the upper paths and the stadium climb on the day.
