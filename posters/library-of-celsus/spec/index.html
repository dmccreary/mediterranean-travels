    Spec: The Library of Celsus Facade¶

    Status: built (2026-11-25). The image is generated, the marker positions are calibrated to it, and the page is in the nav at The Library of Celsus Facade. It is built, not approved: the author approves it after trying the controls.

    Learning Objective¶

    The learner will read the front of the Library of Celsus part by part: say which parts are original design, which are copies or reconstruction, and what the building was for, a public library built as a son's memorial and a tomb.

    Why This Poster¶

    Chapter 10 teaches the classical orders and the reading of inscriptions on Day 7, then uses the library as the place to use both skills at once ("One square, and every skill of the day is in use"). It is also the book's example of library as public access in the Learning for All thread. A straight-on drawing with markers lets the reader find the capitals, the niches, the statue bases, and the removed bronze letters on one front, before standing in front of the real one. The Three Classical Orders poster teaches the orders on a single column each; this poster shows them in use on a building. See The Three Classical Orders.

    Layout Decision¶

    One straight-on view, call-out engine. The idea asked for two layers (before the earthquake and as reassembled). The chapter says only that the building burned in the 3rd century CE, that an earthquake later brought down the façade, and that it was re-erected in 1970 to 1978, so there is no detailed "before" to draw. The picture shows the front as it stands now, with one marker (8) for what was reassembled, and the "before" layer is dropped. Markers run from the street, up the front, then to the hidden chamber and the neighboring gate.

    # Marker Chapter 10 section
    1 Curetes Street and the square Curetes Street; Hadrian; The Temple of Hadrian at Ephesus
    2 Steps and statue bases Inscription; The Library of Celsus
    3 Podium and lower columns The Library of Celsus ("The trick"); The Corinthian Order
    4 Niches with statues The Library of Celsus ("The statues")

    9 Gate of Mazaeus and Mithridates The Library of Celsus ("The inscriptions")

    Marker positions in data.json are estimates from the positions stated in image-prompt.md. They must be calibrated against the real image with main.html?edit=true. Markers are at least 8 percentage points apart.

    Sources¶

    All facts come from Chapter 10: The Library of Celsus, Curetes Street, Hadrian, The Temple of Hadrian at Ephesus, Inscription, Classical Orders, The Corinthian Order, and "Learning for All: A Son's Gift of Books"; plus Chapter 4 for the restoration versus reconstruction distinction.

    What the Chapter Does Not Say¶

    The idea asked for several details that Chapter 10 does not give, so they are not in data.json; see "To Verify Before Approval". Hadrian does not appear on the facade: the chapter dates the library to around 110 CE and Hadrian's reign to 117 to 138 CE, so he appears only in marker 1, through the Temple of Hadrian on Curetes Street.

    Tone Rules¶

    1. Mark reconstruction as reconstruction. Marker 8 and marker 4 say what is a copy and what was put back, in the chapter's words.
    2. The 12,000 scrolls are a modern estimate, not an ancient number. The marker says so.
    3. Corinthian "type". The chapter says both stories have capitals "of the Corinthian type", and data.json repeats that.
    4. No ladder. The library is not ranked above the Library at Alexandria or any other. The marker ends on the book's own question, who was still outside the door.

    Quiz¶

    Ten questions are drafted here. The call-out engine builds its own quiz mode from each marker's hint, and its data.json has no quiz[] field, so these are for the author's review and for any companion page.

    # Question Answer Explanation
    1 Which order of capital stands on the front, in both stories, according to Chapter 10? 5, upper story Both stories have paired columns with capitals of the Corinthian type, the Roman favorite.
    2 Who is buried beneath the reading room, and why is that unusual? 7, burial chamber Celsus lay in a marble sarcophagus; Romans did not normally bury the dead inside a city.
    3 Which feature tells us who paid for the library's books and upkeep? 2, steps and bases The inscription records Aquila's 25,000 denarii, and the bases name Celsus in Greek and Latin.
    4 Which feature shows four virtues, and what is true of the ones we see? 4, niches They are copies, and the originals went to Vienna.
    5 Which marker shows work done in 1970 to 1978? 8, reassembly Austrian archaeologists re-erected the front from its fallen pieces.
    6 Why does the front look taller and grander than its width? 3, podium Podium under the lower columns, and slightly larger columns and spaces near the center.
    7 Which feature is the best example of "what was removed"? 9, the gate The holes are where bronze letters were once pinned.
    8 About how many scrolls is the library said to have held, and how do we know? 6, doorway About 12,000, a modern estimate from the size and number of the niches.
    9 Which street leads down to the library, and what is the footing like? 1, Curetes Street Marble-paved and polished smooth, so slippery when wet.
    10 Which feature shows a gift of knowledge that reaches only as far as readers' means? 6, doorway A reader had to walk in and be able to read, and most people in Roman Ephesus probably could not.

    Open Questions for the Author¶

    1. The "before" layer. Drop it, as drafted, or add a note to Chapter 10 about how the façade fell, so a second layer is possible?
    2. Marker 8 position. The prompt asks for a few paler blocks near the upper right corner as a visual device. Chapter 10 does not say which parts of the front are new, so move the marker, or drop the paler blocks, if the real image suggests a claim.
    3. Companion pages. The lesson page should link Doric, Ionic, or Corinthian?, One Walk, Seven Centuries, and Documented, Debated, or Tradition?. The first uses the same orders wording.

    To Verify Before Approval¶

    • The lower-story order. Chapter 10 says both stories carry Corinthian-type capitals. The idea entry asked which order is on the lower story, and published descriptions of this façade differ. Check a source before approving question 1.
    • Doors, windows, roofline, and relief detail. The chapter does not describe the entrance doors, the upper windows, or the pediment, so no marker names them and the prompt draws them generically.
    • Which parts of the front are original and which are new stone. Not in the chapter.
    • The date of the earthquake that brought down the façade. Not in the chapter.
