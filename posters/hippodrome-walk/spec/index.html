    Spec: Walk Down the Hippodrome¶

    Status: spec and draft data only. No image has been generated, and there is no index.md or main.html yet, so this poster is not in the nav.

    Learning Objective¶

    The learner will read the three monuments on the Hippodrome's old track line as three borrowed layers, and for each one state where it came from, who set it up here, and which way of adding a layer it shows: built over, reused, converted, or removed.

    Why This Poster¶

    Chapter 4 spends a worked example reading Sultanahmet Square layer by layer, and then a MicroSim asks readers to place each layer in its era. A single annotated view keeps the whole row in sight at once: an Egyptian obelisk raised by a Roman emperor in 390 CE, a Greek victory monument carried from Delphi, and a rough tower that a Byzantine emperor sheathed in bronze and Crusaders stripped in 1204. That is spolia made visible, and it is the first thing the group sees on Day 2. The Serpent Column's second end is at Delphi on Day 12, so the poster also closes a loop that Chapter 18 opens.

    Layout Decision¶

    Call-out engine, one wide elevation. The picture is one flat side view of the square, with the three monuments standing clear of each other along the track line, so each can carry a numbered marker without crossing the others. The fountain and the Blue Mosque are included for context, and the pit gets its own marker because it shows a layer literally. The poster does not retell the Serpent Column's two ends; the Two Ends of the Serpent Column MicroSim does that, and the Serpent Column marker's tip points to it.

    The engine is layout: "side-panel", orientation: "landscape", showNumbers: true. Marker positions in data.json are estimates taken from the positions stated in image-prompt.md. They must be calibrated with ?edit=true once the image exists.

    The order is the order a visitor reads the square: the whole shape first, then the row from the north end to the south, then the mosque beside it. Justinian and the Nika Riots live in marker 1, because they explain why the square is still a shape on the map.

    Tone Rules¶

    1. Spell out which layer, and why. Each monument marker names the layer by when it arrived, not when it was made. The trap statement from Chapter 4 is in marker 3's tip.
    2. Contested items go in a tip. The 30,000 death toll is one writer's count; who should keep the four bronze horses is opinion. Neither is stated as plain fact.
    3. The Blue Mosque is a working mosque. It is drawn with respect and no figures on the roofs. Its tip points readers to Chapter 5 for etiquette.
    4. No ladder, no villains. The Crusaders' removal and the Venetians' taking of the horses are stated as events; the poster does not rank eras.
    5. No photo-realism, no lettering. The obelisk's faint carving is drawn as squiggles, not hieroglyphs.

    To Verify Before Approval¶

    These details are in the idea entry or the image prompt but not in the chapters, so they are not in data.json.

    1. Order and spacing of the row. Chapter 4 lists the obelisk, the Serpent Column, and the Walled Obelisk in that order but does not say they are arranged north to south, or how far apart. The image prompt draws them that way, with the fountain at the north end and the Blue Mosque on the east side.
    2. Relative heights. The prompt makes the Walled Obelisk tallest and the Serpent Column about a third of the Egyptian obelisk. The chapters give no heights or pit depth.
    3. The carved base. Chapter 4 says only that the base is carved with Theodosius's portrait. The prompt draws one simplified crowned figure; the real carving's scenes are not in the book.
    4. Stone colors and the bare holes. The obelisk's color and the pattern of fixing holes on the Walled Obelisk are illustrative.
    5. Where the horses and the kathisma stood. The chapter says the horses stood "probably above the starting gates" and the kathisma joined the Great Palace. Neither is drawn, because the chapters give no position.

    Open Questions for the Author¶

    1. A ninth marker for the empty place of the horses? It would carry the 1204 taking to Venice, but nothing in the picture marks where they stood. Currently the story is in marker 1's tip.
    2. Add Hagia Sophia in the background? It stands to the north in reality, so it would sit left of the fountain and crowd marker 2.
    3. Companion links. The lesson page should link Two Ends of the Serpent Column and Read the Ruin - Which Layer?, and Chapter 18's Delphi section for the other end of the column.
