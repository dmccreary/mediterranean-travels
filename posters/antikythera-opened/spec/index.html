    Spec: The Antikythera Mechanism, Opened Up¶

    Status: built (2026-11-25). The image is generated, the marker positions are calibrated to it, and the page is in the nav at The Antikythera Mechanism, Opened Up. It is built, not approved: the author approves it after trying the controls.

    Learning Objective¶

    The learner will name the main parts of the Antikythera mechanism, the crank, the gear train, and the front and back dials, and match each dial to the question an owner could ask of it, from the Moon's phase to the next eclipse.

    Why This Poster¶

    Chapter 20 lists the dials in a table and sends readers to the Antikythera Gears MicroSim to see tooth counts at work. The MicroSim is a simplified teaching model of three outputs, and it says plainly that it omits lunar phases, eclipse prediction, and the calendar spirals. The poster fills the gap by orientation: what the whole object looked like, which dial answers which question, and what survives. It is the picture a reader needs before the sim makes sense. It also sets up the "ancient prediction machine" idea that connects the oracle chapters to the algorithm chapters.

    Layout Decision¶

    Call-out engine, one exploded view. The picture has three groups: the front dial lifted away on the left, the open wooden case with the gear train and crank in the middle, and the two back panels stacked on the right, with a few corroded fragments on the ground at lower left. Keeping the groups apart gives each dial a clear place for a marker. The picture is schematic, not a reconstruction of the actual gear layout, and the lesson page should say so. The poster does not repeat the sim's gear arithmetic; the crank marker points to the sim.

    The engine is layout: "side-panel", orientation: "landscape", showNumbers: true. Marker positions in data.json are estimates taken from the positions stated in image-prompt.md. They must be calibrated with ?edit=true once the image exists.

    Tone Rules¶

    1. Say what is certain. Each marker states only what Chapter 20 or the appendix says, in the chapter's own terms. Nothing about the mechanism is drawn from outside the book.
    2. Do not call it a modern computer. The tip on marker 9 repeats the appendix's warning: it was a calculator with one job, and a wrong ratio stayed wrong.
    3. Contested origin is a labeled tip. Marker 6's tip says that where it was made is argued, and what points away from Rhodes.
    4. No ladder. The poster ties the device to the oracle and the algorithm as a point on a line of ways to predict, not as the better way.
    5. No lettering. The real dials are covered in tiny Greek text. The picture shows tick marks and dots only.

    To Verify Before Approval¶

    The chapters and the sim give the 82 fragments, at least 30 gears, the 2nd century BCE date, the about 70 to 60 BCE wreck date, and the dial table, so those are in data.json. These are not in the chapters, and are not stated as facts there.

    1. Which parts are certain and which are reconstructed. The idea calls for "fragments versus reconstructed parts." Chapter 20 does not say which dials or pointers survive and which are inferred. The appendix says only that the five planets appear "in modern reconstructions." The image prompt draws the dials complete; the lesson page needs a source before it marks any of them as reconstructed.
    2. Details from the appendix, not a chapter. The exeligmos dial (marker 8), the Halieia reading (6), the pin-and-slot Moon gears (2), the Egyptian calendar (3), and the 235 = 5 × 47 and prime-223 arithmetic (5, 7) come only from the appendix. If the author wants chapter-only facts, drop or relocate them.
    3. Layout details in the image. The Metonic spiral drawn with five turns comes from the appendix (235 = 5 × 47). The Saros spiral's number of turns, the two front pointers, the case's open sides, the crank's side, and the number of gears drawn are illustrative and not in the book.
    4. "A main drive wheel." The idea lists one. Neither the chapter nor the appendix names it, so there is no marker for it.
    5. Dimensions. Chapter 20 says "about the size of a shoebox"; the appendix gives 34 × 18 × 9 cm. The picture is not to scale and states neither.

    Open Questions for the Author¶

    1. Keep the appendix-only details (markers 2, 3, 5 to 8)? The alternative is a smaller poster with six markers from Chapter 20 alone.
    2. Link the appendix? The lesson page should link Who Created the Antikythera Mechanism and the Antikythera Gears MicroSim, and note that the sim's ratios are not a reconstruction.
    3. Oracle thread. Chapter 20 sets the mechanism between the oracle and the learned model. A tenth marker could carry that link, but nothing in the picture suggests where it would sit. The lesson page can say it instead.
