    Spec: A Scribe's Page and an Aldine Page¶

    Status: built (2026-11-25). The image is generated, the zones are calibrated to it, and the page is in the nav at A Scribe's Page and an Aldine Page. It is built, not approved: the author approves it after trying the controls.

    Learning Objective¶

    The learner will compare a hand-copied Byzantine page with a small Aldine page along six features (surface, hand or type, page and size, who made it, how long it took, and who could own one), and for each pair name where the comparison stops being fair.

    Why This Poster¶

    Three chapters tell one story in pieces. Chapter 4 gives the Byzantine copyists and the filter of the 9th-century script change. Chapter 6 gives the cost of copying and the press's fixed cost. Chapter 14 gives Aldus, the octavo, and the many hands behind an Aldine book. They are Day 2, Day 4, and Day 9, so a reader meets them a week apart. One picture puts the two pages side by side and asks the book's third-thread question of each feature: who is still outside?

    Layout Decision¶

    Six small diptychs in a 3 × 2 grid, not one picture of two open books. The idea entry in Poster Ideas pictures two open books of the same size, with a zone for each feature. Features such as "how long it took" and "who could afford it" are not parts of a page, so they have no place on two open books. They would need captions or labels, which the image cannot carry. The Oracle and the Algorithm poster met the same problem and solved it with six diptychs, so this poster follows it. Each tile keeps the book idea: a gold seam, like a book's gutter, runs down the middle, with the scribe's page on the left and the Aldine page on the right. A zone is one whole tile.

    Column 1 Column 2 Column 3
    1. Writing Surface 2. Hand or Type 3. Page and Size
    4. Who Made It 5. How Long 6. Who Could Own

    The left half is a Byzantine monastery workshop, because Chapter 4's chain (papyrus, Byzantine minuscule, Italian print) is the one that ends in the 1513 Aldine Plato. The Ottoman copyists of Chapter 6 appear in the facts for tiles 4 and 5.

    Tone Rules¶

    1. No lettering, anywhere. Every page, sign, and slip is drawn as blurred gray bars. The prompt forbids decorative letters too, so the decorated initial is an abstract square of pattern.
    2. No ladder. The scribe's page is not the "before" of a better after. The chapter says copyists decided what the future could read, and also that print multiplied errors as freely as truth.
    3. Break each pair. Every zone carries a fact that begins "Where the pair breaks down", as the Oracle and the Algorithm poster does. Tile 3's comes before its route line.
    4. Illustrative numbers are labeled. Tiles 1, 3, and 5 carry numbers from the chapters' illustrative models, and each fact says "illustrative" or "in the chapter's example".
    5. Respect for the Islamic calligraphic tradition. The Ottoman copyists appear in facts as skilled calligraphers, not as a foil for print.

    Quiz¶

    Ten questions are drafted in data.json: two each for tiles 2, 4, 5, and 6 and one each for tiles 1 and 3. They ask about features and ideas, not about which half is drawn on which side. The sample question from the idea entry ("Which feature let a scholar carry a classic in a pocket?") is question 1.

    To Verify Before Approval¶

    These are not in the chapters, so none of them is in data.json.

    1. Whether parchment is a fair "scribe" surface. The chapters give Byzantine parchment, but they do not say which manuscripts used paper instead. Scribes also wrote on paper, so the surface pair is not strictly a scribe against a press. The zone says only that Aldus changed the number of sheets.
    2. A wide margin and a decorated initial. The chapters do not describe a Byzantine page's layout. The picture's large margin and initial are an illustrator's choices for tile 3.
    3. Sizes and prices. The chapters give formats (folio, quarto, octavo) and the "portable little books" description but no centimeters, no price of an Aldine octavo, and no price of a manuscript.

    Open Questions for the Author¶

    1. Facing pages or six diptychs? The prompt follows the Oracle poster. The alternative is a literal spread of two open books with only two zones, which loses the pair-by-pair quiz.
    2. A seventh pair: who checked it? Chapter 14's editor (collation and emendation, Musurus and Plato) and the copyists' slips are a strong pair, but the image would need a 4 + 3 layout.
    3. Which scribe on the left? The Byzantine copyist ties to Plato's chain. An Ottoman calligrapher would tie to Chapter 6's roundels and to Day 4.
    4. Companion sims. The lesson page should link Scribe or Press?, Folding the Sheet - Folio, Quarto, Octavo, Who Made a Greek Book in Venice?, and When Printing Arrived.
